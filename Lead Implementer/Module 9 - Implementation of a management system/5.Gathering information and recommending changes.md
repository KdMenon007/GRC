
During project implementation, some **work assignments are focused on gathering information** that will later be used to develop policies, procedures, controls, and improvement actions.

### 1. Purpose of Information Gathering

Information collected during assignments can be used to:

- Develop policies and procedures
    
- Understand current practices
    
- Identify gaps
    
- Identify outdated practices
    
- Identify security or operational problems
    
- Recommend improvements
    
- Reduce costs and risks
    
- Improve organizational performance
    

---

### 2. Observe Actual Operations

Do not rely only on documented procedures or what employees say should happen.

The project team should also:

- Observe how work is actually performed
    
- Ask employees questions
    
- Compare actual practices with documented procedures
    
- Identify differences between **"what should happen" and "what actually happens"**
    

### Important Warning Sign

Pay particular attention to statements such as:

> **"We've always done it this way."**

This may indicate:

- Outdated processes
    
- Lack of periodic review
    
- Inefficient practices
    
- Uncontrolled workarounds
    
- Potential security risks
    

---

### 3. Analyze Existing Information

Organizations may already have useful information that can reveal problems.

Examples:

- Incident records
    
- Audit findings
    
- Access logs
    
- Complaint records
    
- Performance reports
    
- Risk assessments
    
- Asset records
    
- Vulnerability reports
    
- Service reports
    
- Operational statistics
    

Analyzing this information can help determine:

- What is working well
    
- What is not working
    
- Where risks exist
    
- What needs improvement
    
- What controls should be implemented
    

---

### 4. Identify Outdated or Unusual Practices

The project team should use its experience to identify practices that appear:

- Outdated
    
- Inefficient
    
- Unusual
    
- Uncontrolled
    
- Inconsistent with current good practices
    

However, an unusual practice should not automatically be considered a problem.

The team should **speak with the people performing the activity** to understand:

- Why the practice exists
    
- What problems it creates
    
- Whether it is still necessary
    
- Whether there is a better approach
    

---

### 5. Identify Problems and Root Causes

Observation can reveal problems that employees or management may not have clearly identified.

For example:

**Problem:** Warehouse employees freely collect production parts.

Possible consequences:

- Inventory records become inaccurate
    
- Items may be lost
    
- Unauthorized access may occur
    
- Stock levels become difficult to monitor
    

The project team can investigate the cause and recommend appropriate controls.

---

### 6. Add Value Through Recommendations

The purpose of implementation work is not simply to create documentation.

The project team should also identify opportunities to:

- Improve performance
    
- Reduce risks
    
- Reduce costs
    
- Improve efficiency
    
- Strengthen controls
    
- Prevent recurring problems
    

### Example

If different types of waste are mixed together, disposal costs may increase.

A recommendation could involve:

- Separating waste categories
    
- Defining responsibilities
    
- Training employees
    
- Introducing monitoring
    
- Establishing appropriate disposal procedures
    

---

## ISO 27001 Perspective

For an ISO 27001 implementation, information gathering can help identify:

- Existing security controls
    
- Security gaps
    
- Weak processes
    
- Uncontrolled access
    
- Asset-management problems
    
- Outdated procedures
    
- Vulnerabilities
    
- Incident trends
    
- Compliance gaps
    

This information can then feed into the **gap analysis, risk assessment, treatment plan, policies, procedures, and improvement activities**.

### Key Principle

> **Do not implement controls based only on documents. Understand how the organization actually works, identify real problems, and design practical controls that address the organization's risks.**
