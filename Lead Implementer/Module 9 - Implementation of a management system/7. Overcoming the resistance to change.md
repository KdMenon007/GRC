
During management system implementation, employees may resist new practices because they are accustomed to existing ways of working. Managing this resistance is essential for successful implementation.

### 1. Identify Potential Resistance

Identify:

- Potential sources of opposition
    
- Reasons for resistance
    
- Possible obstacles
    
- Expected employee concerns
    
- Appropriate responses to each concern
    

### Example

A new waste-segregation process requires employees to spend additional time sorting waste.

**Possible resistance:**  
Employees may not properly separate the waste because they consider the process inconvenient.

---

## 2. Identify Obstacles and Supporting Factors

### Common Obstacles

- Insufficient containers
    
- Containers located too far away
    
- Lack of awareness
    
- Difficulty identifying similar-looking waste
    
- Employees not understanding the purpose of the change
    
- Lack of management support or visibility
    
- Existing habits and resistance to new procedures
    
- Perception that the change creates additional work
    

### Supporting Aids

- Posters and visual instructions
    
- Training and awareness
    
- Simple procedures
    
- Adequate equipment and resources
    
- Management encouragement
    
- Management leading by example
    
- Regular feedback
    

---

## 3. Prioritize Resistance Barriers

Not all obstacles have the same importance.

Create a hierarchy based on the **strength and potential impact of each obstacle**.

### Example

**High-priority obstacles:**

1. Employees do not believe management supports the new policy.
    
2. Correct containers are too far away.
    

**High-priority supporting actions:**

1. Visible top-management support and encouragement.
    
2. Place appropriate containers closer to employees.
    

This allows the project team to focus resources on the most significant barriers first.

---

## 4. Explain the Need for Change

Employees need to understand **why the organization needs to change**.

Explain how the new practice:

- Addresses current business needs
    
- Reduces risks
    
- Improves performance
    
- Reduces costs
    
- Supports organizational objectives
    
- Helps the organization respond to changing business conditions
    

### Example

Instead of simply telling employees:

> "You must segregate waste."

Explain:

- Current contaminated-waste disposal costs
    
- Cost of properly separated waste
    
- Potential savings
    
- Environmental benefits
    
- Organizational requirements
    

This makes the reason for the change clear.

---

## 5. Develop Countermeasures

After identifying and prioritizing resistance barriers, develop specific actions to overcome them.

### Example

**Obstacle:** Containers are too far away.

**Countermeasure:** Purchase additional containers and place them closer to work areas.

**Obstacle:** Employees do not believe management supports the change.

**Countermeasure:** Ask top management to communicate the importance of the policy and demonstrate support through their own behavior.

---

## 6. Plan the Change

Once the countermeasures have been identified, prepare a **change implementation plan**.

The plan should include:

- Communication to affected employees
    
- Training
    
- Technical assistance
    
- Distribution of policies and procedures
    
- Physical changes
    
- Technical changes
    
- Required resources
    
- Responsibilities
    
- Implementation timeline
    
- Monitoring schedule
    
- Evaluation activities
    
- Feedback mechanisms
    

---

## 7. Communicate the Change

Affected employees should understand:

- What is changing
    
- Why it is changing
    
- When it will change
    
- How it affects them
    
- What they need to do
    
- Where they can obtain help
    

Communication should occur before and during implementation.

---

## 8. Train and Provide Assistance

Where the change introduces new responsibilities or processes, provide:

- Training
    
- Demonstrations
    
- Instructions
    
- Technical support
    
- User guidance
    
- Assistance during the transition
    

This helps employees move from the **old way of working to the new way**.

---

## 9. Implement the Change

Execute the change according to the implementation plan.

However, the project team should remain flexible because:

> **Unplanned problems can occur during implementation.**

The team should respond quickly when problems arise rather than allowing them to undermine the change.

---

## 10. Monitor and Evaluate

After implementation, monitor whether the change is actually working.

Check:

- Are employees following the new practice?
    
- Are the required resources available?
    
- Are employees experiencing difficulties?
    
- Is resistance decreasing?
    
- Are the expected results being achieved?
    
- Is additional training required?
    
- Does the procedure need modification?
    

Use feedback and performance information to make adjustments.

---

## Change Management Process

**Identify Resistance**  
↓  
**Identify Obstacles & Supporting Factors**  
↓  
**Prioritize Barriers**  
↓  
**Explain the Need for Change**  
↓  
**Develop Countermeasures**  
↓  
**Plan Communication & Training**  
↓  
**Implement Change**  
↓  
**Monitor & Evaluate**  
↓  
**Adjust & Improve**

### Key Principle

> **Successful implementation is not only about creating a new policy or procedure. It is about helping people understand, accept, adopt, and consistently follow the new way of working.**
