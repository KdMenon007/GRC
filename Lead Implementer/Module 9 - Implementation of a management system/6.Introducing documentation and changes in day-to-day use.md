## Document Review, Approval, Implementation and Monitoring

During project implementation, the team produces various documents such as **policies, procedures, instructions, forms, and rules**. These documents must be reviewed, approved, implemented, and monitored to ensure they are effective in practice.

### 1. Document Quality Review

Before a document is formally approved, it should be reviewed by the people responsible for using it.

The review should determine whether the document is:

- Useful
    
- Suitable for the organization
    
- Adequate for the intended purpose
    
- Practical to implement
    
- Easy to understand
    
- As simple as possible
    

The review should also identify whether unnecessary requirements or complexity can be removed.

---

### 2. Initial and Final Approval

Documents should go through an appropriate approval process.

**Typical process:**

**Draft → User/Process Owner Review → Initial Approval → Management Approval → Implementation**

The people who will use the document should have an opportunity to review it before final approval.

Final approval should normally come from the **manager or authority responsible for that process or subject**.

---

### 3. Documentation Approval Authority

The organization should define **who has authority to approve different types of documents**.

This can be documented in:

- Project plan
    
- Documented approval matrix
    
- Management system documentation
    
- Responsibility matrix
    

Approval authority can be assigned based on:

- Department
    
- Process
    
- Document type
    
- Security topic
    
- Management level
    

### Example

|Document|Possible Approval Authority|
|---|---|
|Information Security Policy|Top Management|
|Access Control Policy|CISO / Management|
|IT Procedure|IT Manager|
|HR Security Procedure|HR Manager|
|Supplier Security Procedure|Procurement / Security Management|

---

### 4. Formal Introduction into the Management System

Once final approval is received, the document should be formally introduced into the management system.

It may be distributed:

- Digitally
    
- On paper
    
- Through an organizational document-management system
    
- Through an intranet or controlled repository
    

The organization should ensure that employees have access to the **current approved version**.

---

### 5. Implementation of New Practices

Approval of a document does **not automatically mean that employees will follow it**.

A new procedure may require:

- Employee awareness
    
- Training
    
- Communication
    
- Demonstration
    
- Management support
    
- Follow-up
    
- Practical assistance
    

Employees may initially resist changes, particularly when they do not understand the benefits.

Therefore:

> **Document approval is only the beginning; the actual practice must be implemented.**

---

### 6. Monitor Implementation

After implementation, the organization should verify whether the new procedures and practices are actually being followed.

Monitoring can include:

- Observation
    
- Employee feedback
    
- Interviews
    
- Performance metrics
    
- Record reviews
    
- Internal checks
    
- Small conformance verifications
    
- Before-and-after performance comparisons
    

---

### 7. Collect Feedback

Feedback should be obtained from:

- Employees using the procedure
    
- Process owners
    
- Managers
    
- Other affected stakeholders
    

The purpose is to determine:

- Is the procedure practical?
    
- Are employees following it?
    
- Are there difficulties?
    
- Are there unnecessary steps?
    
- Is the expected result being achieved?
    
- Does the procedure need modification?
    

---

### 8. Measure Results

Where possible, compare performance **before and after implementation**.

For example:

**Before implementation:**  
High number of security incidents caused by incorrect access provisioning.

**After implementation:**  
Access requests are formally approved and periodically reviewed.

The organization can then monitor whether unauthorized or inappropriate access has decreased.

---

### 9. Conformance Verification

Small checks can be performed to verify that new practices are being followed.

### Example

If an organization introduces waste-segregation procedures, a daily check could verify whether containers contain the correct type of waste.

For ISO 27001, similar checks could include:

- Verifying that terminated users are disabled.
    
- Checking whether privileged accounts are reviewed.
    
- Checking whether backups are completed.
    
- Verifying that security logs are being monitored.
    
- Checking whether employees completed security awareness training.
    

---

## Overall Process

**Develop Document**  
↓  
**Review by Users / Process Owners**  
↓  
**Management Approval**  
↓  
**Publish Controlled Version**  
↓  
**Train & Communicate**  
↓  
**Implement New Practice**  
↓  
**Monitor Compliance & Performance**  
↓  
**Collect Feedback**  
↓  
**Make Adjustments / Improve**

### Key Principle

> **A document is not effective merely because it has been approved. It becomes effective when the organization understands it, implements it, follows it, and verifies that it achieves the intended result.**
