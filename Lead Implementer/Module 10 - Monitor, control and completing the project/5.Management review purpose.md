
## 1. Purpose of Management Review

As the implementation approaches completion, **top management must objectively evaluate the management system**.

The key question is:

> **Is the management system ready to operate effectively without the project team, project manager, and project sponsor?**

Management review is a critical part of the implementation and is required before certification.

It helps determine whether the management system is:

- Suitable
    
- Adequate
    
- Effective
    
- Aligned with organizational objectives
    
- Ready for continual improvement
    

---

## 2. Management Review and Certification

A management review must be performed **before the certification audit**.

It provides top management with an opportunity to evaluate the overall performance of the management system and make decisions about its future.

The project manager should help prepare information covering:

- Implementation progress
    
- Past performance
    
- Current performance
    
- Problems and weaknesses
    
- Improvement opportunities
    
- Future performance
    
- Required resources
    
- Proposed changes
    

---

# 3. Main Outcome — Continual Improvement

The purpose and outcome of management review should be **continual improvement of the management system**.

As the effectiveness and efficiency of the management system improve, the organization's overall performance should also improve.

Management review should identify:

- What is working well.
    
- What needs improvement.
    
- What is no longer suitable.
    
- What is no longer sufficient.
    
- What should change in the next management cycle.
    

---

# 4. Management Review as a Transition Point

Management review can be viewed as a **transition between two management cycles**.

### Looking Back

Evaluate:

- Previous performance.
    
- Objectives achieved.
    
- Problems encountered.
    
- Effectiveness of the management system.
    
- What worked well.
    
- What did not work well.
    

### Looking Forward

Decide:

- Future objectives.
    
- Required improvements.
    
- Changes to the management system.
    
- Resources required.
    
- Priorities for the next cycle.
    

### Simple Concept

**Past cycle → Review → Decisions → New cycle**

---

# 5. Frequency of Management Reviews

Management reviews should be performed **regularly**.

They do not necessarily have to be limited to one annual meeting.

For example, an organization may conduct:

- Monthly ordinary management reviews.
    
- Periodic extraordinary management reviews.
    
- Additional reviews when significant changes occur.
    

The frequency should be appropriate to the organization's needs and context.

---

# 6. Two Types of Management Review

## A. Extraordinary Management Review

The extraordinary review provides a broader evaluation of the management system.

Top management can:

- Review overall performance.
    
- Review the management system design.
    
- Question the strategic direction.
    
- Review objectives.
    
- Change strategic orientation.
    
- Establish priorities for the next management cycle.
    

This is particularly useful during major transitions or significant organizational changes.

---

## B. Ordinary Management Review

Ordinary reviews focus mainly on **ongoing performance**.

They evaluate:

- Whether performance is meeting targets.
    
- Whether objectives are being achieved.
    
- Whether improvements are required.
    
- Whether existing processes remain effective.
    

The strategic direction and objectives are generally **not reconsidered at every ordinary review**.

---

# 7. Be Honest About Problems

A negative finding during management review **does not mean the management system has failed**.

The organization should be honest about:

- Weaknesses.
    
- Failures.
    
- Poor performance.
    
- Nonconformities.
    
- Ineffective controls.
    
- Improvement opportunities.
    

The objective is to identify problems and address them.

### Important Principle

> **Do not hide problems. Identify them, address them, and improve the system.**

---

# 8. Internal Detection vs External Audit Finding

If the organization identifies a problem internally and takes appropriate corrective action, this demonstrates that the management system is working to **detect and improve problems**.

An external auditor is more likely to raise a nonconformity when:

- Problems were hidden.
    
- Known failures were ignored.
    
- Corrective actions were not taken.
    
- The organization failed to address known weaknesses.
    

Therefore:

**Detect → Correct → Improve**

is much better than:

**Hide → Ignore → External auditor discovers**

---

# 9. Common Management Review Myths

## Myth 1: There must be only one annual management review

Not necessarily.

Organizations can conduct **multiple management reviews**, such as monthly reviews, when this is appropriate.

---

## Myth 2: Every topic must be covered in one meeting

Not necessarily.

For effectiveness, different topics can be discussed in **multiple management review meetings** where appropriate.

The important point is that the required management review inputs and outputs are properly addressed.

---

## Myth 3: Management review should be treated as a rigid meeting

The ISO standard should be used as a **guide to ensure the review is complete**, rather than treating it as a rigid recipe.

The organization should design the review process in a way that provides useful information and meaningful management decisions.

---

# 10. Effective Management Review

A good management review should:

- Be objective.
    
- Use actual performance information.
    
- Encourage honest discussion.
    
- Identify weaknesses.
    
- Review improvement opportunities.
    
- Make management decisions.
    
- Consider future performance.
    
- Establish priorities.
    
- Support continual improvement.
    

If a topic requires detailed discussion, it may be better to use **more than one meeting** rather than trying to cover everything superficially in a single meeting.

---

# 11. Project Manager's Role

The project manager should support top management by:

- Preparing implementation information.
    
- Providing past performance data.
    
- Providing current status information.
    
- Highlighting problems and improvement opportunities.
    
- Preparing information about future performance.
    
- Supporting the management review process.
    
- Recording decisions and required actions.
    

The **final management decisions belong to top management**.

---

## Quick Revision

### Management Review

**Past Performance → Current Performance → Evaluate Suitability & Effectiveness → Identify Improvements → Decide Future Direction**

### Two Types

**Extraordinary Review**  
→ Overall performance + strategy + objectives + management system design

**Ordinary Review**  
→ Ongoing performance + targets + improvements

### Key Principle

> **Management review is not about proving that everything is perfect; it is about objectively determining what is working, what is not working, and what needs to improve.**

### Before Certification

**Implementation → Internal Audit → Management Review → Corrective Actions/Improvements → Certification Readiness**
