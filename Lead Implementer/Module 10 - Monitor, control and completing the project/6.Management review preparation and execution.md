
### 1. Purpose of Management Review

Management review is performed by **top management** to objectively evaluate whether the ISMS:

- Is **suitable** for the organization.
    
- Is **adequate** to meet its requirements.
    
- Is **effective** in achieving intended outcomes.
    
- Is aligned with the organization's strategic direction.
    
- Requires improvement or changes.
    
- Has the necessary resources.
    

The purpose is **continual improvement** of the management system.

---

## 2. Preparing for Management Review

There is no single universal format for conducting a management review. The organization should design an approach that:

- Encourages top-management participation.
    
- Makes the meeting meaningful.
    
- Focuses on decisions and actions.
    
- Produces useful results.
    

### Preparation should include:

- Prepare and distribute the **agenda**.
    
- Define the purpose of the meeting.
    
- Identify participants.
    
- Define required inputs.
    
- Assign responsibility for preparing each input.
    
- Share information with participants before the meeting.
    
- Give participants enough time to review the information.
    

---

## 3. Management Review Participants

Participants normally include:

- Top management
    
- Relevant department managers
    
- ISMS manager / information security manager
    
- Project manager, especially during implementation
    
- Other relevant process owners or stakeholders
    

Top management should **lead and own** the management review rather than treating it as another routine meeting.

---

## 4. Management Review Inputs

The agenda should be based on the requirements of the applicable ISO 27001 standard.

Important inputs can include:

### ISMS Performance

- Status of information security objectives.
    
- Whether objectives are being achieved.
    
- Effectiveness of action plans.
    
- Performance of the ISMS.
    
- Trends in information security performance.
    

### Internal Audit Results

Review:

- Main internal audit findings.
    
- Nonconformities.
    
- Corrective actions.
    
- Whether corrective actions were effective.
    

### Previous Management Review

Review:

- Previous decisions.
    
- Previous actions.
    
- Progress against those actions.
    
- Whether previous decisions were effective.
    

### Internal and External Issues

Consider changes affecting the ISMS, such as:

- Organizational changes.
    
- Technology changes.
    
- Business changes.
    
- Legal and regulatory changes.
    
- New threats.
    
- Changes in customer requirements.
    
- Changes in the external environment.
    

### Risks and Opportunities

Review:

- Significant information security risks.
    
- Changes in risk levels.
    
- Risk treatment progress.
    
- New risks.
    
- Opportunities for improving the ISMS.
    

### Resources

Evaluate whether sufficient resources are available, including:

- People
    
- Budget
    
- Technology
    
- Training
    
- Infrastructure
    
- Security tools
    

---

## 5. Management Review Meeting

The meeting should **not simply be a presentation of information**.

The objective is to:

- Analyze important information.
    
- Discuss performance.
    
- Identify problems.
    
- Evaluate effectiveness.
    
- Make decisions.
    
- Assign actions.
    
- Determine required improvements.
    

### Key questions

Management should ask:

- Is the ISMS effective?
    
- Is the ISMS suitable for the organization?
    
- Is the ISMS adequate?
    
- Are security objectives being achieved?
    
- What is working well?
    
- What needs improvement?
    
- Are current risks adequately controlled?
    
- What new risks or opportunities exist?
    
- What resources are required?
    
- Does the security policy need updating?
    
- What should the next security objectives be?
    

---

## 6. Outputs of Management Review

The management review should produce **decisions and actions**.

Typical outputs include:

### Decisions about:

- Improvements to the ISMS.
    
- Changes to information security objectives.
    
- Changes to policies.
    
- Changes to risk treatment.
    
- Required resources.
    
- Changes required because of business or security conditions.
    

### Actions should identify:

- What needs to be done.
    
- Who is responsible.
    
- Target completion date.
    
- Required resources.
    
- Follow-up method.
    

The outputs should be **documented and retained as evidence** of the management review.

---

## 7. First Management Review During Implementation

When the organization is implementing ISO 27001 for the first time, the management review has an additional purpose.

It should evaluate:

- Whether implementation activities are complete.
    
- Whether the ISMS has been implemented as planned.
    
- Results of internal audits.
    
- Outstanding nonconformities.
    
- Effectiveness of implemented controls.
    
- Readiness for certification.
    
- Remaining actions before certification.
    

Top management can then decide whether the **implementation project is complete and ready for formal closure**.

---

## 8. Extraordinary and Ordinary Management Reviews

A practical approach can use two types:

### Extraordinary Management Review

Used for major strategic evaluation.

It may consider:

- Overall ISMS performance.
    
- Strategic direction.
    
- Major organizational changes.
    
- Changes to ISMS objectives.
    
- Major risks and opportunities.
    
- Significant changes to the ISMS.
    

### Ordinary Management Review

Used for regular monitoring.

It may focus on:

- Performance against objectives.
    
- Audit results.
    
- Risk status.
    
- Corrective actions.
    
- Security incidents.
    
- Improvement actions.
    
- Resource requirements.
    

Management reviews do **not necessarily have to be limited to one annual meeting**. Multiple reviews can be performed when useful to the organization.

---

## 9. Management Review and Continual Improvement

Management review should work as a **look-back and look-forward activity**.

### Look back

Evaluate:

- What happened?
    
- What worked?
    
- What failed?
    
- Were objectives achieved?
    
- What did audits and incidents reveal?
    

### Look forward

Decide:

- What needs to change?
    
- What should the next objectives be?
    
- What risks and opportunities need attention?
    
- What improvements are required?
    
- What resources are needed?
    

This supports the **continual improvement of the ISMS**.

---

## 10. Important Principle — Be Honest

Negative findings should **not be hidden**.

If the management review identifies:

- Weak controls
    
- Poor performance
    
- Unmet objectives
    
- Security problems
    
- Ineffective processes
    

they should be openly discussed and addressed through appropriate actions.

Identifying and correcting problems demonstrates that the ISMS is being actively managed.

The real concern is not discovering a problem; it is **hiding or failing to address the problem**.

---

## 11. Management Review Meeting Agenda

A practical agenda can include:

1. Date, time and participants
    
2. Purpose of the management review
    
3. Previous review actions
    
4. ISMS objectives and performance
    
5. Internal audit results
    
6. Security incidents and corrective actions
    
7. Risk and opportunity status
    
8. Changes in internal and external issues
    
9. Interested-party and legal requirements
    
10. ISMS performance and effectiveness
    
11. Resource adequacy
    
12. Improvement opportunities
    
13. Required decisions and actions
    
14. Implementation/certification readiness
    
15. Project closure decision, where applicable
    

---

## 12. Project Manager's Role

During initial ISO 27001 implementation, the project manager should:

- Prepare implementation status information.
    
- Provide information about past performance.
    
- Coordinate required inputs.
    
- Support preparation of the management review agenda.
    
- Highlight outstanding implementation activities.
    
- Present project risks and issues.
    
- Provide information about internal audit results.
    
- Explain certification readiness.
    
- Support management in making decisions.
    
- Record the decision regarding **implementation acceptance and project closure**.
    

The project manager **supports the management review**, but top management owns the review and makes the management decisions.

---

### Key Takeaway

**Management Review = Evaluate + Decide + Improve**

Top management reviews the ISMS's **performance, suitability, adequacy, effectiveness, risks, opportunities, resources and audit results**, then makes decisions about **improvements, objectives, resources and future direction**. For the first management review, it can also determine whether the ISO 27001 implementation is complete and ready for the next stage.
