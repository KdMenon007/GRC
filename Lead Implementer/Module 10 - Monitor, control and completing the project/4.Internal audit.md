
## 1. Purpose of Verification During Implementation

Performing verifications during project implementation helps the project team evaluate:

- Implementation progress.
    
- Whether new practices are actually being followed.
    
- Whether procedures and rules have been adapted to the organization's reality.
    
- Whether the implemented system is producing the expected results.
    

Verification helps identify problems **before the final certification stage**.

---

# 2. Purpose of Internal Audit

An **internal audit** provides an independent and objective assessment of the management system.

It determines:

- To what extent the requirements of the standard have been addressed.
    
- Whether the organization's own policies and procedures are being followed.
    
- Whether the management system is working as intended.
    
- Whether the implemented practices make sense for the organization.
    
- What adjustments or improvements are required.
    

---

# 3. Internal Audit and Certification

If an organization intends to become **ISO 27001 certified**, it must perform internal audits before certification.

The internal audit should evaluate conformity of the management system with:

- ISO 27001 requirements.
    
- The organization's own ISMS requirements.
    
- Applicable policies and procedures.
    

The audit provides confidence that the organization is ready for the certification process.

---

# 4. Project Manager's Role

The project manager is responsible for ensuring that an internal audit is performed during the implementation project.

The project manager should:

- Coordinate with the project sponsor.
    
- Work with the internal ISMS leader where applicable.
    
- Arrange for competent internal or external auditors.
    
- Ensure the audit is performed independently.
    
- Ensure audit findings are reviewed.
    
- Ensure required actions are addressed.
    

### Important

The project manager should **not compromise auditor independence** by having someone audit their own work.

---

# 5. Selecting the Auditor

The audit can be performed by:

- A qualified internal auditor.
    
- An independent employee.
    
- An external auditor or consultant.
    

The important principle is that the auditor should provide an **objective and independent assessment**.

---

# 6. Internal Audit Report

The main output of the internal audit is an **audit report**.

The report should help answer three important questions:

### A. Are we doing it right?

This means checking whether the organization is following its established rules.

**Example:**

> Are employees complying with the approved access control policies and procedures?

---

### B. Are we doing the right thing?

This evaluates whether the implemented processes are actually achieving their intended objectives.

**Example:**

> The organization follows its access control procedure, but does the procedure actually provide effective access protection?

---

### C. What adjustments are necessary?

The audit identifies areas where improvements or changes are needed.

**Example:**

A procedure is being followed correctly, but it creates unnecessary bureaucracy.

The organization may simplify the procedure **without reducing its intended security outcome**.

---

# 7. Internal Audit Is Not About Blame

Internal auditing should **not** be treated as:

- Finding someone to blame.
    
- Punishing employees.
    
- Finger-pointing.
    
- Judging individuals.
    
- Finding mistakes just for the sake of finding mistakes.
    

Instead, internal auditing should support:

- **Prevention**
    
- **Discipline**
    
- **Learning from mistakes**
    
- **Improvement**
    
- **Better implementation**
    
- **Continual improvement**
    

---

# 8. Key Principle

> **Internal audit is not about finding who is wrong; it is about finding what needs to be improved.**

The audit provides an independent view of whether the ISMS is **implemented correctly, achieving its intended results, and suitable for the organization**.

---

## Quick Revision

**Implementation Verification → Internal Audit → Findings → Corrective/Improvement Actions → Better ISMS → Certification Readiness**

### Three Questions of Internal Audit

1. **Are we doing it right?** → Are rules being followed?
    
2. **Are we doing the right thing?** → Are intended results being achieved?
    
3. **What needs adjustment?** → What should be improved or simplified?
