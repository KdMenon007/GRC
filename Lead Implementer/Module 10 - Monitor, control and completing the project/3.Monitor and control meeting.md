
## 1. Purpose of Monitor and Control Meetings

Monitor and control meetings are **periodic meetings between the project manager and project team** to:

- Evaluate the current project status.
    
- Share important project information.
    
- Review progress against the project plan.
    
- Identify problems and deviations.
    
- Make project decisions.
    
- Agree on required changes and actions.
    
- Maintain control over the implementation.
    

These meetings help ensure that the project remains **on schedule, within control, and effective**.

---

## 2. Prepare Before the Meeting

A good practice is to prepare and send the **project status report before the meeting**.

This allows participants to:

- Read the information in advance.
    
- Understand the current project situation.
    
- Think about problems and required decisions.
    
- Prepare questions and recommendations.
    

As a result, the meeting can focus on **decisions and actions rather than simply presenting information**.

---

## 3. Send Meeting Invitation

Based on the **stakeholder list** created during project planning, invite the relevant participants.

The invitation should include:

- Meeting date
    
- Meeting location or online meeting details
    
- Start time
    
- End time
    
- Purpose of the meeting
    
- Agenda
    
- Project status report
    
- Relevant documents or links
    

The status report and agenda should be provided **in advance**.

---

# 4. Meeting Agenda

A clear agenda helps make the meeting **productive and time-controlled**.

### Date, Place and Start Time

Clearly state:

- Date
    
- Location
    
- Start time
    

Participants should know exactly when and where the meeting will take place.

### End Time

Define the expected **end time**.

This is important because participants can plan their other responsibilities and are less likely to view the meeting as an unnecessarily long meeting.

---

## 5. Overall Purpose

Clearly explain **why the meeting is being held**.

For example:

- Review project progress.
    
- Identify deviations.
    
- Decide corrective actions.
    
- Review project risks.
    
- Approve required changes.
    
- Confirm upcoming activities.
    

The participants should understand **what the project needs to achieve by the end of the meeting**.

---

## 6. Meeting Content

For every topic, define:

- **Subject** — What will be discussed?
    
- **Purpose** — Why is it being discussed?
    
- **Responsible person** — Who will present or lead it?
    
- **Time allotted** — How much time is available?
    

This prevents discussions from becoming unfocused.

### Example

|Topic|Purpose|Responsible|Time|
|---|---|---|--:|
|Project progress|Review completed activities|Project Manager|10 min|
|Delayed activities|Identify reasons and actions|Team Lead|10 min|
|Risk review|Review new project risks|Risk Owner|10 min|
|Upcoming activities|Confirm responsibilities|Project Manager|10 min|

---

## 7. Provide Relevant Links

Participants should be given access to the information they need to prepare.

Examples:

- Project plan
    
- Latest status report
    
- Risk register
    
- Implementation checklist
    
- Previous meeting minutes
    
- Project schedule
    
- Relevant procedures or documents
    

This reduces time spent during the meeting searching for information.

---

# 8. Characteristics of an Effective Meeting

An effective monitor and control meeting should be:

- **Planned**
    
- **Focused**
    
- **Time-bound**
    
- **Evidence-based**
    
- **Decision-oriented**
    
- **Action-oriented**
    

The project manager should keep the discussion focused on the project objectives and prevent unnecessary discussions.

---

# 9. Outputs of the Meeting

The meeting should result in clear decisions and actions.

Typical outputs include:

- Decisions made
    
- Corrective actions
    
- New tasks
    
- Assigned responsibilities
    
- Deadlines
    
- Changes to the project plan
    
- Risk actions
    
- Escalation requirements
    
- Follow-up items
    

Every action should have an **owner and expected completion date**.

---

# 10. Project Manager's Role

The project manager should:

- Prepare the status report.
    
- Prepare the agenda.
    
- Invite the correct stakeholders.
    
- Ensure participants receive information beforehand.
    
- Keep the meeting focused.
    
- Review project progress.
    
- Identify deviations and problems.
    
- Facilitate decisions.
    
- Assign actions and responsibilities.
    
- Update the project plan when changes are approved.
    
- Follow up on agreed actions.
    

---

## 11. Key Principle

> **Send the status report before the meeting so that the meeting can focus on evaluation, decisions, and actions.**

The purpose of a monitor and control meeting is **not simply to provide information**. It is to determine whether the project is under control and decide what needs to be done next.

### Simple Flow

**Status Report → Review → Discussion → Decision → Action → Follow-up**

This supports effective **project monitoring and control** throughout the ISO 27001 implementation.
