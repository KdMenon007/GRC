
## 1. Define the Scope of the Project

The first step in preparing for implementation is to **define the scope of the management system**.

The scope establishes the **boundaries of where the management system will apply**.

You need to determine:

- Which parts of the organization are included?
    
- Which processes are included?
    
- Which products and services are included?
    
- Which locations are included?
    
- Which markets or business activities are included?
    

---

## 2. Scope Can Cover the Entire Organization

Normally, the scope may cover:

- The entire organization
    
- All processes
    
- All products
    
- All services
    
- All relevant locations
    

However, an organization may define a more limited scope when appropriate.

### Example

A large organization operating in several countries may decide to include:

- Headquarters
    
- Operations in India
    
- Specific products and services
    

But exclude:

- Operations in other countries
    
- Certain business units
    
- Certain products or services
    

The scope must clearly describe **what is included and what is excluded**.

---

## 3. Factors to Consider When Defining Scope

When defining the scope, consider:

- **Budget**
    
- **Requirements of the standard**
    
- **Legal and regulatory requirements**
    
- **Requirements of interested parties**
    
- **Top management expectations**
    
- **Current state of the organization**
    
- Products and services
    
- Locations
    
- Organizational processes
    
- Business objectives
    

The scope should be realistic and aligned with the organization's needs and available resources.

---

## 4. Gap Analysis

After defining the scope, a **gap analysis** can be performed.

Gap analysis compares:

> **Current state of the organization vs. requirements of the management system**

The purpose is to identify what the organization already has and what still needs to be implemented.

### Gap Analysis Helps Identify

- Existing processes
    
- Existing policies and procedures
    
- Existing controls
    
- Existing documentation
    
- Missing requirements
    
- Weaknesses
    
- Areas requiring improvement
    

---

## 5. Gap Analysis Result

The difference between the current situation and the required management system is called the **gap**.

### Example

**Requirement:** Organization must conduct regular internal audits.

**Current situation:** No formal internal audit program exists.

**Gap:** Internal audit process and audit program need to be established.

The same approach can be applied to all relevant requirements.

---

## 6. Benefits of Gap Analysis

Gap analysis helps the organization:

- Understand its current position.
    
- Identify missing requirements.
    
- Determine implementation activities.
    
- Estimate required resources.
    
- Plan the project more effectively.
    
- Prepare a realistic implementation schedule.
    
- Allocate responsibilities.
    
- Estimate the required budget.
    

---

## 7. Initial Planning

The results of the scope definition and gap analysis provide a foundation for **implementation planning**.

They help management determine:

- What needs to be implemented?
    
- What resources are required?
    
- Who will be responsible?
    
- How much will it cost?
    
- How long will implementation take?
    
- Which activities should be prioritized?
    

### Key Principle

> **Define the scope first, assess the current state through gap analysis, identify the gaps, and then use the results to plan resources, activities, budget, and implementation timelines.**
