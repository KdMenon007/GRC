
Before the implementation starts, after the project has been planned, the organization should organize a **Project Kick-Off Meeting** with the key people involved in or affected by the project.

### 1. Purpose of the Kick-Off Meeting

The main purpose is to communicate:

- The **importance of the project** to the organization.
    
- The **role and contribution** of each participant.
    
- The project **calendar and important milestones**.
    
- The project's **strategic context**.
    
- Why the project is important **at this particular time**.
    
- How the project is **prioritized compared with other projects and normal business activities**.
    

---

### 2. Project Sponsor's Role

The **Project Sponsor should chair and open the kick-off meeting**.

The Sponsor should explain:

- The strategic reason for starting the project.
    
- Why the organization needs the project now.
    
- How the project supports organizational objectives.
    
- The priority given to the project.
    
- The importance of cooperation from the project team and relevant departments.
    

This gives the project **management visibility and authority**.

---

### 3. Participants

Key people who have a close interest in or relevance to the implementation should attend.

This may include:

- Project Sponsor
    
- Project Manager
    
- Project Team
    
- CISO / ISMS Manager
    
- Department representatives
    
- Process owners
    
- Other relevant stakeholders
    

---

### 4. Key Information to Communicate

During the meeting, participants should understand:

**Why → What → Who → When**

- **Why:** Why is the project important?
    
- **What:** What will the project achieve?
    
- **Who:** Who is responsible for each activity?
    
- **When:** What is the schedule and when are the milestones?
    

---

### 5. Importance

The kick-off meeting ensures that everyone starts the implementation with a **common understanding of the project's purpose, priorities, responsibilities, and schedule**.

> **The Project Kick-Off Meeting formally starts the implementation and aligns management, the project team, and key stakeholders.**
