
## Estimate Workload and Project Duration

### 1. Estimate the Required Work

Consider all activities required for implementation and estimate the **effort needed** to complete them.

Consider:

- Number of activities
    
- Complexity of each activity
    
- Required expertise
    
- Number of people involved
    
- Dependencies between activities
    
- Available resources
    
- Organization's deadlines
    

---

### 2. Break Work into Smaller Tasks

Large activities should be divided into smaller, manageable tasks.

Ask:

- Can the work be broken down?
    
- Can different activities run in parallel?
    
- Can the work be shared between two or more people?
    
- Which activities depend on other activities?
    

Breaking work into smaller tasks makes **planning, assignment, and monitoring** easier.

---

### 3. Consider Organization Deadlines

The implementation schedule must consider deadlines imposed by the organization or external parties.

For example:

- Customer requirement
    
- Contractual deadline
    
- Business launch
    
- Regulatory deadline
    
- Target certification date
    

The project schedule should be realistic while still meeting important business requirements.

---

## 4. Typical Implementation Duration

Implementation duration depends primarily on the **size and complexity of the organization**.

|Organization Size|Typical Implementation Duration|
|---|--:|
|Small — up to 50 employees|**Less than 8 months**|
|Medium — up to 500 employees|**8–12 months**|
|Large — 500+ employees|**Around 15 months**|

These are **typical estimates**, not ISO 27001 requirements. Actual duration depends on the organization's scope, complexity, maturity, resources, and existing processes.

---

## 5. Avoid Excessive Project Duration

Projects should not continue indefinitely.

For example:

> A small organization taking more than 12 months for implementation may indicate problems with project management, resources, commitment, or execution.

Long delays can result in:

- Loss of momentum
    
- Reduced employee engagement
    
- Changing business requirements
    
- Changing risks
    
- Loss of management attention
    
- Difficulty completing the project
    

The project should therefore have a **realistic target completion date**.

---

## 6. Estimate Expert/Consultant Workload

Estimate how much time the consultant or expert can realistically provide.

Consider:

- Number of people on the project team
    
- Team capacity
    
- Experience
    
- Authority
    
- Availability
    
- Responsibilities outside the project
    
- Consultant availability
    
- Project duration
    

The workload should match the actual capacity of the people involved.

---

## 7. Define Dedicated Project Time

Whenever possible, establish a minimum amount of dedicated time for the project.

### Example

For a **12-month implementation project**:

> Allocate at least **one day per week** to the project, particularly during the first half of implementation.

This creates consistency and prevents the project from becoming something that is only worked on occasionally.

---

## 8. Maintain Work Discipline

Successful implementation requires **continuous involvement and discipline**.

Team members should not view ISO 27001 implementation as:

> "A non-priority, part-time activity."

Instead, they should understand that their participation is an important part of the organization's implementation project.

Regular dedicated time helps maintain:

- Project momentum
    
- Progress
    
- Accountability
    
- Team involvement
    
- Management visibility
    

---

### Key Principle

> **Estimate the effort realistically, divide large tasks into manageable activities, allocate sufficient people and time, establish a realistic implementation duration, and ensure team members have dedicated time to keep the project moving.**
