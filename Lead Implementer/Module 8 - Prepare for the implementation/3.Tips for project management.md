
## Define Tasks and Control Project Execution

### 1. Define Project Activities

After completing the **gap analysis**, identify all activities required to implement the management system.

Use:

- Gap analysis results
    
- PDCA cycle
    
- ISO 27001 requirements
    
- Organization's objectives
    
- Identified risks and priorities
    

Create a complete list of implementation tasks.

---

## 2. Consider Available Resources

Before scheduling activities, evaluate:

- Available organizational resources
    
- Project team members
    
- Consultant availability
    
- Time constraints
    
- Budget
    
- Required expertise
    
- Organization's target completion date
    

The implementation schedule should be realistic based on these limitations.

---

## 3. Prioritize Activities

Not every activity needs to be performed at the same time.

Prioritize activities based on:

- Importance
    
- Risk
    
- Dependencies
    
- Available resources
    
- Project deadlines
    
- Business requirements
    

Then determine the **pace of implementation**.

---

## 4. Create an Implementation Calendar

Translate the implementation activities into a project calendar.

The calendar should specify:

|Element|Description|
|---|---|
|**Task**|What needs to be done|
|**Responsible person**|Who will perform it|
|**Deadline**|When it must be completed|
|**Resources**|What is required|
|**Milestone**|Important project achievement|
|**Control point**|When progress will be reviewed|

This creates a clear roadmap for project execution.

---

## 5. Establish Control Points

A scheduled plan does not guarantee that the project will actually progress according to the plan.

Therefore, establish **control points** throughout the project.

Control points help determine:

- Is the project progressing according to plan?
    
- Are activities being completed on time?
    
- Are sufficient resources available?
    
- Is the project moving at the expected pace?
    
- Are responsibilities being fulfilled?
    
- Are there implementation problems?
    
- Is management intervention required?
    

---

## 6. Control Project Progress

Monitor whether:

- Tasks are completed according to schedule.
    
- Deadlines are being met.
    
- Resources are available.
    
- People are performing their assigned responsibilities.
    
- Project milestones are being achieved.
    

If the project falls behind, take corrective management action.

---

## 7. Control Quality

It is not enough to complete activities on time.

You must also verify the **quality of the work**.

For example:

- Are policies properly written?
    
- Are procedures actually being followed?
    
- Are controls implemented correctly?
    
- Is documentation appropriate?
    
- Are employees following the defined processes?
    

The objective is not simply to mark tasks as "completed," but to ensure that the implementation is effective.

---

## 8. Monitor Implementation and Effectiveness

The project should monitor both:

### Implementation

> **Has the planned activity actually been implemented?**

### Effectiveness

> **Is the implemented activity achieving the intended result?**

For example:

**Activity:** Security awareness training

**Implementation:** Employees completed the training.

**Effectiveness:** Employees demonstrate improved understanding and follow security requirements in practice.

---

## 9. Monitor Employee Difficulties

During implementation, identify problems such as:

- Employee resistance
    
- Lack of awareness
    
- Lack of resources
    
- Lack of skills
    
- Delays
    
- Communication problems
    
- Conflicting business priorities
    

These issues should be addressed before they significantly affect the project.

---

## 10. Establish Important Milestones

Identify major milestones and create a control point for each one.

Examples:

1. **Policy awareness**
    
2. **Procedure implementation**
    
3. **Security control implementation**
    
4. **Performance monitoring**
    
5. **Internal audit**
    
6. **Management review**
    
7. **Final certification preparation**
    

At each milestone, evaluate whether the project is ready to move to the next stage.

---

## 11. Project Review Meetings

Establish meetings for ongoing evaluation and feedback.

Meetings can be used to:

- Review project progress
    
- Discuss problems
    
- Review resources
    
- Check milestones
    
- Evaluate quality
    
- Make decisions
    
- Assign corrective actions
    
- Obtain feedback from the project team
    

Regular communication with the **project sponsor/top management** is important for major decisions and escalations.

---

## 12. Final Verification Point

Before the certification audit, establish a **final verification point**.

Review the entire implementation from the beginning of the project through certification preparation.

Verify that:

- The defined scope is still appropriate.
    
- ISO 27001 requirements have been addressed.
    
- Required documentation is available.
    
- Controls have been implemented.
    
- Processes are actually being followed.
    
- Records/evidence are available.
    
- Internal audit has been completed.
    
- Nonconformities have been addressed.
    
- Management review has been completed.
    
- The organization is ready for the certification audit.
    

---

### Key Principle

> **Create the implementation plan, assign responsibilities and deadlines, establish milestones and control points, continuously monitor progress and quality, address problems promptly, and perform a final verification before certification.**
