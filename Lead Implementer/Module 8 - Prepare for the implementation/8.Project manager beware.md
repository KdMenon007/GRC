
### 1. What is a Project Risk?

A **risk** is anything that could potentially affect the:

- **Project timeline**
    
- **Project performance**
    
- **Project budget**
    
- **Quality of implementation**
    
- **Successful completion of the project**
    

A risk is something that **may happen in the future**.

If the risk actually occurs, it becomes an **issue** that must be addressed.

**Example:**

> Risk: Key project team member may become unavailable.  
> Issue: The team member is actually unavailable and the assigned work is delayed.

---

### 2. Purpose of Risk Management

**Risk management** is the process of:

1. Identifying risks.
    
2. Categorizing risks.
    
3. Assessing and prioritizing risks.
    
4. Planning how to treat or control them.
    
5. Monitoring risks throughout the project.
    

The objective is to **anticipate problems before they become issues**.

---

### 3. Risk Management is Proactive

Risk management should not only be performed when something goes wrong.

It should be part of the **project planning process**.

The project team should ask:

> **What could go wrong?**

Then determine:

- What could cause the risk?
    
- What could be the impact?
    
- How likely is it to occur?
    
- How can it be prevented?
    
- What should be done if it occurs?
    
- Who is responsible for managing it?
    

---

### 4. Examples of Project Risks

#### Top Management Risk

Top management may:

- Ignore project communications.
    
- Fail to attend important meetings.
    
- Delay important decisions.
    
- Not provide sufficient support.
    

**Impact:** Project delays and lack of resources.

---

#### Scope Risk

Required activities may be missed during the initial **scope definition**.

**Impact:** Important requirements may be discovered later, causing additional work, cost, and delays.

---

#### Estimation Risk

The project team may make inaccurate estimates of:

- Time
    
- Cost
    
- Workload
    
- Resources
    

**Impact:** The project may exceed its planned schedule or budget.

---

#### Resource Risk

The organization may not have enough:

- People
    
- Time
    
- Budget
    
- Technical resources
    

**Impact:** Activities may be delayed or performed inadequately.

---

#### Competence Risk

Project team members may lack sufficient experience or knowledge.

**Impact:** Poor-quality documentation, incorrect implementation, or repeated work.

---

#### Data Protection and Confidentiality Risk

Sensitive organizational information may be exposed during the implementation.

For example:

- Risk assessment information
    
- Employee information
    
- Customer information
    
- Security configurations
    
- Confidential business information
    

**Impact:** Confidentiality breaches, legal problems, or loss of trust.

---

### 5. Continuous Risk Monitoring

Risk identification should continue **throughout the entire project lifecycle**.

New risks can appear because of:

- Organizational changes
    
- Staff changes
    
- Changes in project scope
    
- New technologies
    
- Changes in deadlines
    
- Resource constraints
    
- External requirements
    
- Unexpected project issues
    

Therefore:

> **Risk management is a continuous activity, not a one-time activity at the beginning of the project.**

---

### 6. Simple Risk Management Flow

**Identify → Categorize → Assess → Prioritize → Plan Treatment → Monitor → Review**

This allows the Project Manager and Project Team to anticipate problems and take action before they significantly affect the ISO 27001 implementation project.
