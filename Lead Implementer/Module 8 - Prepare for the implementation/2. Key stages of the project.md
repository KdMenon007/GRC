|Stage|What it means|ISO 27001 example|
|---|---|---|
|**PLAN**|Decide what you want to achieve and how|Define ISMS scope, security objectives, policies, risks|
|**DO**|Implement what you planned|Implement security controls, procedures, and processes|
|**CHECK**|Verify whether it is working|Monitoring, measurement, internal audit, management review|
|**ACT**|Fix problems and improve|Corrective actions, treating nonconformities, continual improvement|

## 1. PLAN — Decide what you're going to do

This is the **foundation**.

You establish:

- **Scope** — What parts of the organization are covered?
    
- **Objectives** — What security outcomes do you want?
    
- **Top-level policy** — What is management committing to?
    
- **Organizational structure** — Who is responsible for what?
    
- **Risk management** — What could go wrong, and how will you handle it?
    

For ISO 27001, this means building the basic structure of your **Information Security Management System (ISMS)**.

**Example:**  
A company decides that its cloud infrastructure, employees, customer data, and internal applications are within the ISMS scope. It identifies risks such as ransomware, unauthorized access, and data leakage.

---

## 2. DO — Execute the plan

Now you stop planning and **actually implement things**.

This phase focuses on **operations**.

For ISO 27001, this could include:

- Access control
    
- Incident management
    
- Backup procedures
    
- Vulnerability management
    
- Security awareness training
    
- Supplier security
    
- Asset management
    
- Authentication controls
    
- Security monitoring
    

The key idea is:

> **PLAN says what you intend to achieve. DO puts those intentions into operation.**

For example:

**Plan:**  
"Only authorized employees should access sensitive customer information."

**Do:**  
Implement MFA, role-based access control, access reviews, and appropriate authentication procedures.

---

## 3. CHECK — Is it actually working?

This is where you **challenge your own assumptions**.

You don't simply say:

> "We implemented the controls, therefore we're secure."

That's not enough.

You need evidence that the system is performing as intended.

ISO management systems use mechanisms such as:

### Monitoring and measurement

Measure whether objectives and processes are performing as expected.

### Internal audit

Independently examine whether the ISMS is:

- implemented,
    
- maintained,
    
- compliant with ISO 27001 requirements, and
    
- operating effectively.
    

### Management review

Top management reviews the ISMS and determines whether it remains suitable, adequate, and effective.

For example:

**Objective:** 100% of privileged accounts must use MFA.

**Check:** Audit the accounts.

**Result:** 95% use MFA.

You've now discovered a **5% gap**.

---

## 4. ACT — Fix the gaps and improve

This is where you respond to what the **CHECK** phase discovered.

You:

- Identify **nonconformities**
    
- Determine their causes
    
- Take **corrective actions**
    
- Verify whether corrective actions worked
    
- Continually improve the ISMS
    

Using our MFA example:

**Check:**  
95% of privileged accounts use MFA.

**Act:**  
Investigate why the remaining 5% don't.

Perhaps those accounts belong to a legacy system that doesn't support MFA.

Then you could:

1. Document the problem.
    
2. Determine the root cause.
    
3. Implement a solution.
    
4. Verify that MFA is now enabled.
    
5. Update the process to prevent recurrence.
    

And then...

**The cycle starts again.**

---

# 🧠 The important concept

PDCA isn't really a **one-time four-step project**.

It's a **continuous loop**:

**PLAN → DO → CHECK → ACT → PLAN → DO → CHECK → ACT...**

That's why ISO talks so much about **continual improvement**.

A useful way to remember it:

> **Plan it → Do it → Prove it → Fix it → Improve it → Repeat.**

For **ISO 27001**, you can think of the four stages as:

**PLAN**  
🎯 Scope + objectives + policy + risks

⬇️

**DO**  
⚙️ Controls + processes + procedures

⬇️

**CHECK**  
🔍 Monitoring + measurement + internal audit + management review

⬇️

**ACT**  
🛠️ Nonconformities + corrective actions + continual improvement

⬇️

🔄 **Back to PLAN**
