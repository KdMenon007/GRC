
A **surveillance audit** is a periodic follow-up audit performed by the certification body after an organization has achieved certification.

### 1. When does it happen?

After successful certification, surveillance audits are conducted periodically during the certificate's validity period. The exact frequency depends on the certification scheme and certification body; commonly, they are **annual**, with some schemes allowing different intervals.

The certification body issues an **audit report** and can raise nonconformities that the organization must address.

---

### 2. How is it different from the certification audit?

|Certification Audit|Surveillance Audit|
|---|---|
|More comprehensive|Based largely on sampling|
|Initial certification assessment|Ongoing maintenance assessment|
|Broader coverage of the management system|Selected processes, locations and activities|
|More extensive audit time|Usually less audit time|
|Determines whether certification can initially be granted|Determines whether certification continues to be maintained|

The surveillance audit does **not normally examine every process in the same depth** as the initial certification audit.

---

### 3. What will auditors typically look at?

Auditors will usually give particular attention to areas that demonstrate whether the management system is actually functioning, such as:

- **Management reviews**
    
- **Internal audits**
    
- **Corrective actions**
    
- **Complaints/customer feedback**
    
- Performance and objectives
    
- Changes affecting the management system
    
- Continual improvement
    
- Selected operational processes
    
- Selected sites or organizational units
    

They then sample other parts of the management system.

---

### 4. What does "sampling" mean?

Suppose an organization has:

- 2 operating activities
    
- 4 locations
    
- 20 major processes
    

The surveillance auditor does **not necessarily need to examine all 4 locations and all 20 processes during one surveillance visit**.

Instead, the auditor selects representative samples.

For example:

> **Management review → Internal audit → Corrective actions → Site A → Selected operational processes**

During the certification cycle, the certification body plans its surveillance activities so that the required coverage of the management system and relevant sites is achieved.

---

### 5. What should the organization do?

Don't prepare for surveillance only when the auditor's arrival is approaching.

Maintain the system continuously:

**Operate → Monitor → Internal Audit → Correct → Management Review → Improve → Surveillance Audit**

The organization should be able to demonstrate that:

- Procedures are actually being followed.
    
- Required records are available.
    
- Internal audits are effective.
    
- Previous nonconformities have been properly addressed.
    
- Risks are reviewed.
    
- Objectives are monitored.
    
- Management is involved.
    
- The ISMS continues to achieve its intended outcomes.
    
- Continual improvement is taking place.
    

**Easy way to remember:**  
**Certification = broad initial assessment**  
**Surveillance = periodic sampled check that the system is still working**
