
A management system must be a **living system**. Organizations constantly change, so policies, procedures, competencies, suppliers, stakeholders, risks, and controls must also be reviewed and updated.

### 1. Review Policies and Procedures

Organizations may introduce:

- New products or services
    
- New customers
    
- New business models
    
- New distribution channels
    
- New technologies
    
- New performance requirements
    

If the management system isn't updated, its documents can quickly become outdated or irrelevant.

### Best practice: Document ownership

Assign an **owner to every important document**.

The document owner should:

1. Review the document periodically.
    
2. Usually review it at least annually.
    
3. Determine whether changes are necessary.
    
4. Recommend or initiate updates.
    
5. Ensure the approved version is implemented.
    

**Remember:**

> **Every important document should have an owner and a review cycle.**

---

# 2. Review Competence Requirements

New activities can create new competency requirements.

For example, an organization introduces a new cloud platform.

The existing employees may not have sufficient knowledge to operate it securely.

The organization may therefore need:

- Training.
    
- New skills.
    
- New roles.
    
- Additional personnel.
    
- External expertise.
    

A living management system should adapt its **competence requirements** to organizational changes.

---

# 3. Monitor Supplier and Outsourcing Performance

Don't select suppliers purely based on:

> **Price + initial evaluation**

Instead, monitor their **actual performance**.

For example:

- Are service levels being met?
    
- Are security requirements being followed?
    
- Are incidents occurring?
    
- Are deliveries reliable?
    
- Are contractual requirements being fulfilled?
    
- Are outsourced services meeting organizational expectations?
    

Supplier and outsourcing-partner performance should be evaluated at planned intervals.

If performance is inadequate, the organization may:

- Work with the supplier to correct the problem.
    
- Strengthen requirements.
    
- Increase monitoring.
    
- Find an alternative supplier.
    

---

# 4. Obtain Stakeholder Feedback

Stakeholders can provide an external perspective on organizational performance.

Don't ask only:

> "Are you satisfied with our service?"

A simple "yes" doesn't provide much actionable information.

Instead, ask questions that identify **specific opportunities for improvement**.

For example:

- What could we improve?
    
- Which part of our service creates difficulties for you?
    
- Are there recurring problems?
    
- What would make our service more valuable?
    
- Are there emerging expectations we should consider?
    

Then use the feedback to drive improvement.

---

# 5. Review Risks When Circumstances Change

Changes in the organization can change its **threats, vulnerabilities and risks**.

For example:

**New cloud service**  
↓  
New technology exposure  
↓  
New threats/vulnerabilities  
↓  
Risk changes  
↓  
Existing controls may no longer be adequate  
↓  
Risk assessment needs updating  
↓  
Additional/modified controls may be required

Therefore, risk assessment should not be treated as a document that is created once and forgotten.

### Risk reviews should occur:

- At planned intervals, typically at least annually where required by the organization's process.
    
- Whenever significant changes occur.
    
- When new threats or vulnerabilities emerge.
    
- When major business or technology changes occur.
    

---

# 6. Update Controls

After reviewing the risks, determine whether existing controls remain appropriate.

You may need to:

- Modify existing controls.
    
- Add new controls.
    
- Remove controls that are no longer relevant.
    
- Change responsibilities.
    
- Strengthen monitoring.
    
- Update procedures.
    

This is particularly important in an **ISO 27001 ISMS**, because changes in the risk landscape can make previously adequate controls insufficient.

---

# 7. Monitor External Requirements

Organizations must also monitor changes outside the organization.

Examples include:

- Laws.
    
- Regulations.
    
- Standards.
    
- Contractual requirements.
    
- Customer requirements.
    
- Industry requirements.
    

The organization should have someone responsible for:

**Identify → Obtain → Review → Assess impact → Implement changes**

For example:

> A new regulatory requirement is published.

The organization should not simply store a copy of the regulation.

It should:

1. Obtain the updated requirement.
    
2. Determine whether it applies.
    
3. Analyse its impact.
    
4. Identify gaps.
    
5. Update policies/procedures/controls if necessary.
    
6. Implement the changes.
    
7. Maintain evidence of compliance.
    

---
### Easy memory framework

**Change → Review → Assess → Update → Implement → Verify**

And for the ISMS specifically:

> **Business changes → Risks change → Controls may change → ISMS must adapt.**
