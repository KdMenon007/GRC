## Maintaining Certification: Audit Findings, Internal Audits and Management Review

After certification, the organization must continue to **operate, monitor, and improve the management system**. Certification is maintained through disciplined follow-up and evidence.

### 1. Close Certification Audit Findings Properly

Any **nonconformities and observations** identified during the certification audit should be handled diligently.

For nonconformities, maintain a complete audit trail showing:

**Finding → Root-cause analysis → Corrective action → Implementation → Evidence → Effectiveness verification → Closure**

Don't simply fix the immediate symptom.

For example:

> **Finding:** Access reviews were not consistently performed.

Weak response:

> "We completed the missing access review."

Better corrective action:

- Determine why reviews were missed.
    
- Identify the root cause.
    
- Define responsibility.
    
- Establish a review schedule.
    
- Complete the required reviews.
    
- Monitor future compliance.
    
- Verify that the corrective action is effective.
    

This gives the certification body evidence that the organization has **learned from the problem and prevented recurrence**.

---

# 2. Use of the Certification Logo

If the organization wants to use a certification logo or certification mark, it must follow the **certification body's rules**.

Don't assume that certification automatically permits unrestricted use of the logo.

Incorrect use can create problems with the certification body.

### Best practice:

**Check the certification body's official rules → Confirm permitted usage → Obtain clarification if uncertain.**

---

# 3. Internal Audits Are More Than Compliance Checks

Internal audits have two important purposes:

### Compliance

> **Are we doing what we said we would do?**

For example:

- Are procedures followed?
    
- Are records maintained?
    
- Are controls implemented?
    

### Effectiveness

> **Is what we're doing actually working?**

For example:

- Is the control reducing the intended risk?
    
- Are security objectives being achieved?
    
- Is the procedure practical?
    
- Is the process producing the expected outcome?
    

Therefore, internal audits support both:

**Discipline + Continual Improvement**

---

# 4. Why Surveillance Auditors Care About Internal Audits

Surveillance audits are generally shorter than the initial certification audit.

Therefore, the auditor may pay close attention to the organization's **internal audit program and reports**.

They want evidence that the organization itself is:

- Monitoring the ISMS.
    
- Identifying weaknesses.
    
- Reporting nonconformities.
    
- Taking corrective actions.
    
- Following up on findings.
    
- Improving the management system.
    

A strong internal audit program demonstrates that the organization is not simply waiting for the external auditor to discover problems.

---

# 5. Management Review Must Continue

Management reviews should be performed at **planned intervals** according to the organization's management-system requirements and arrangements.

They demonstrate:

- Top-management leadership.
    
- ISMS performance evaluation.
    
- Resource decisions.
    
- Review of objectives.
    
- Risk and opportunity consideration.
    
- Corrective-action follow-up.
    
- Continual improvement.
    
- Alignment with business needs.
    

Management review should answer:

> **"Is our management system still suitable, adequate and effective for the organization?"**

---

# 6. Align the ISMS With Business Objectives

This is one of the most important points.

An ISMS should not become a separate bureaucratic activity that exists only to satisfy an auditor.

It should support actual business challenges.

For example:

**Business challenge:** Protect customer data  
↓  
**Security objective:** Reduce data-exposure risk  
↓  
**Controls:** Access control, encryption, monitoring, awareness  
↓  
**Measurement:** Security incidents, access-review results, audit findings  
↓  
**Management review:** Evaluate whether the controls are achieving the intended result  
↓  
**Improvement:** Modify controls where necessary

When the ISMS is connected to business outcomes, management is more likely to see it as a **business-enabling system rather than a burden**.

---
### Quick memory formula

**Findings → Corrective Action → Internal Audit → Management Review → Improvement → Surveillance Audit**

And the key principle:

> **A certification is maintained by operating an effective management system—not by preparing for the auditor only when the audit is approaching.**
