## Monitoring Performance and Continual Improvement

One of the most important characteristics of a good management system is that it can **measure its own performance, identify gaps, and drive improvement**.

### 1. The Basic Improvement Cycle

The process can be understood as:

**Desired performance**  
↓  
**Operate the management system**  
↓  
**Monitor actual performance**  
↓  
**Compare actual vs. desired**  
↓  
**Identify gaps**  
↓  
**Correct / improve**  
↓  
**Monitor again**

This creates a continuous improvement cycle.

---

## 2. Don't Automatically Change the System

A poor result does **not always mean that the management system itself needs to be changed**.

First determine why the performance was poor.

### Example

Suppose production was significantly below target for one month.

After analysing eight months of data, you discover that:

- Seven months were normal.
    
- Only one month was poor.
    
- The poor result was caused by an unusual one-time event.
    

In that case, changing the entire management system may be unnecessary.

A temporary operational action—such as additional working capacity to recover the delay—may be sufficient.

### Therefore:

**Poor result ≠ automatically change the management system.**

First investigate the cause.

---

## 3. Look for Trends, Not Just Numbers

Don't simply maintain tables full of figures.

Use:

- Trends.
    
- Graphs.
    
- Comparisons.
    
- Ratios.
    
- Statistical analysis where appropriate.
    
- Historical performance.
    

The objective is to determine whether the management system is **reliably achieving its objectives**.

For example, suppose customer complaints over six months show:

- 60%+ of complaints come from only two recurring causes.
    

That tells management where improvement effort is likely to produce the greatest benefit.

Instead of trying to fix everything, concentrate resources on the **significant recurring causes**.

---

# 4. Three Possible Outcomes of Monitoring

After monitoring performance, you can generally reach three conclusions.

### A. Performance is acceptable

The system is achieving its intended results.

**Action:** Continue operating and monitoring.

---

### B. Performance is temporarily poor, but the system is still effective

The problem may be caused by an isolated event.

**Action:** Take an operational correction without necessarily changing the management system.

---

### C. The management system itself is not effective

There is evidence of a recurring or systemic problem.

**Action:** Investigate the cause and implement corrective action and/or improve the management system.

---

# 5. Corrective Action Is Not a Band-Aid

A weak corrective action treats only the symptom.

A strong corrective action addresses the **root cause**.

The basic sequence is:

**Identify symptom**  
↓  
**Analyse the problem**  
↓  
**Determine root cause**  
↓  
**Plan corrective action**  
↓  
**Implement action**  
↓  
**Verify effectiveness**

### Example

**Problem:** Repeated unauthorized-access incidents.

Weak response:

> Reset the affected user's password.

That's a correction, but it may not address the underlying cause.

Better investigation:

> Why are unauthorized-access incidents repeatedly occurring?

Possible root cause:

> Access rights are not being reviewed when employees change roles.

Corrective action could therefore include:

- Improve the joiner/mover/leaver process.
    
- Define responsibility for access reviews.
    
- Establish periodic access reviews.
    
- Train responsible personnel.
    
- Monitor compliance.
    
- Verify effectiveness after implementation.
    

---

## ⭐ ISO 27001 Perspective

This principle fits directly with the **PDCA/continual-improvement mindset** of an ISMS.

You don't want an ISMS that merely says:

> "We have policies and controls."

You want an ISMS that can demonstrate:

> **"We measure whether our controls and processes work, identify weaknesses, take action, and verify whether the action improved the result."**
---

### Remember this formula:

**Measure → Compare → Analyse → Correct → Improve → Verify → Repeat**
