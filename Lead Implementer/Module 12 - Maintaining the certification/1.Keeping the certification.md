## Maintaining ISO 27001 Certification

### 1. Certification Is Not a One-Time Event

ISO 27001 certification should not be treated as a **prize that is achieved once and then forgotten**.

Certification provides external recognition that the organization's management system has met the certification requirements. The organization must then continue operating and improving the ISMS to maintain certification.

The best way to maintain certification is simple:

> **Operate the management system effectively every day.**

---

## 2. What Does Operating the ISMS Mean?

It means that the organization actually performs the activities defined in its management system.

For example:

- Follow approved policies and procedures.
    
- Perform required risk assessments and reviews.
    
- Maintain required records.
    
- Conduct internal audits.
    
- Perform management reviews.
    
- Monitor security objectives and performance.
    
- Manage incidents and corrective actions.
    
- Review risks and opportunities.
    
- Maintain competence and awareness.
    
- Continuously improve the ISMS.
    

The key relationship is:

**Document → Implement → Record → Monitor → Improve**

---

## 3. Keep the ISMS Aligned With Reality

A management system should not remain frozen after certification.

During operation, you may discover that:

- A procedure is impractical.
    
- A process has changed.
    
- A record provides little value.
    
- A control needs strengthening.
    
- A responsibility has changed.
    
- A document needs simplification.
    

Don't be afraid to update the system.

> **Changing the management system when reality changes is a sign of a living and effective ISMS—not a failure.**

---

## 4. Maintain an Annual ISMS Calendar

A useful way to maintain discipline is to create an **annual ISMS activity calendar**.

For example:

|Activity|Frequency|
|---|---|
|Internal audits|Planned intervals|
|Management reviews|Planned intervals|
|Risk reviews|Planned intervals / when required|
|Corrective-action reviews|Ongoing|
|Security performance monitoring|Regularly|
|Customer/stakeholder feedback|As applicable|
|Awareness activities|Planned intervals|
|Control reviews|Planned intervals|
|ISMS objectives review|Planned intervals|

This prevents important activities from being forgotten.

---

## 5. Integrate New Employees

New employees are an important part of maintaining the ISMS.

A new employee may have:

- Excellent technical knowledge.
    
- Strong academic qualifications.
    
- Significant previous experience.
    

But they may know **nothing about your organization's ISMS**.

Therefore, onboarding should cover relevant:

- Information security policies.
    
- Procedures.
    
- Roles and responsibilities.
    
- Security requirements.
    
- Reporting processes.
    
- Organizational objectives.
    
- Applicable controls.
    

### Why is this important?

If new employees start working without understanding the organization's established practices, they may introduce different ways of working.

Over time:

**New employee ignores procedure → Others copy the behaviour → Standard practices deteriorate → ISMS discipline weakens**

Therefore, **competence and awareness must be maintained continuously**.

---

## 6. Keep Top Management Engaged

Another important responsibility is maintaining **top-management engagement**.

Don't assume that management will remain interested simply because the organization obtained certification.

The ISMS should speak the **language of business**.

Instead of only saying:

> "We need to comply with ISO 27001."

Connect information security to business outcomes:

- Reduced business risk.
    
- Protection of critical information.
    
- Business continuity.
    
- Customer trust.
    
- Regulatory compliance.
    
- Reduced security incidents.
    
- Protection of revenue.
    
- Competitive advantage.
    
- Achievement of business objectives.
    

Management should see the ISMS as a **business management tool**, not merely an audit requirement.

---
### Remember:

**Certification → Operate → Monitor → Audit → Review → Improve → Maintain**

The core principle is:

> **Don't maintain the certificate; maintain an effective ISMS.**
