

### 1. Give Management Time

- Do not expect top management to understand all the benefits in a **single 20-minute meeting**.
    
- Management may need time to understand:
    
    - Business benefits
        
    - Financial benefits
        
    - Risk reduction
        
    - Customer benefits
        
    - Long-term value
        
- Continue communicating the value of the project through appropriate opportunities.
    

---

### 2. Elevator Speech

An **elevator speech** is a short **30–60 second presentation** used to explain the project when you have a brief opportunity to speak with an important decision-maker.

It should:

- Clearly explain the project.
    
- Explain why it is important.
    
- Focus on business benefits.
    
- Explain how it can help the organization.
    
- Be simple, confident, and convincing.
    

### Example Structure

> **Problem → Proposed Management System → Business Benefit → Expected Outcome**

The speech should be **prepared and rehearsed** so that you can communicate confidently when an unexpected opportunity arises.

---

### 3. Use Important Opportunities

Presenting the project in an important setting can be more effective than relying only on formal meetings.

Possible opportunities include:

- Meeting the CEO
    
- Informal discussions with senior management
    
- Company events
    
- Business meetings
    
- Short conversations with decision-makers
    

The key is to be prepared to explain the project clearly whenever an opportunity arises.

---

### 4. Build Support Among Key People

Identify people who are:

- Close to top management.
    
- Interested in the project.
    
- Able to influence management decisions.
    
- Able to explain the benefits of the management system.
    

These people can help:

- Provide additional insight.
    
- Explain the project's value to management.
    
- Build internal support.
    
- Help bring the project to the **top management agenda**.
    

---

### 5. Involve the CFO

The **Chief Financial Officer (CFO)** can be an important stakeholder.

The CFO may be particularly interested in:

- Cost savings
    
- Investment justification
    
- Audit-related costs
    
- Operational efficiency
    
- Financial risks
    
- Return on investment
    

For example, if certification can reduce the number of separate customer audits, the potential **cost savings** can be presented to the CFO.

---

### 6. Prepare Before Presenting

Before presenting the project:

- Understand the organization's business.
    
- Know the main challenges.
    
- Identify the expected benefits.
    
- Prepare your key messages.
    
- Prepare a short elevator speech.
    
- Identify influential stakeholders.
    
- Be ready to explain the financial and business value.
    

---

### 7. If You Are an Insider

If you are already working inside the organization:

- Use your existing knowledge of the organization.
    
- Identify relevant decision-makers.
    
- Build internal support.
    
- Communicate the project's benefits to management.
    
- Use appropriate internal opportunities to present the proposal.
    

The objective is to **build enough support for the project to reach and gain approval from top management**.
