### 1. Purpose of the Proposal

After a successful sales meeting and convincing top management, the consultant prepares a **project proposal**.

The proposal should not be treated as the tool that wins the business. Ideally, the client should already be interested and convinced before receiving it.

The proposal should:

- Confirm what was discussed.
    
- Clearly define the project.
    
- Explain what the client will receive.
    
- Establish responsibilities and conditions.
    
- Define timeline and milestones.
    
- Specify pricing and payment terms.
    

---

### 2. Focus on Client Outcomes

The proposal should focus on **what the organization will achieve**, rather than focusing mainly on the consultant.

Explain:

- What the client will get.
    
- What results they can expect.
    
- How the project will benefit the business.
    
- How the management system will help achieve their objectives.
    

### Example

Instead of focusing on:

> "We provide ISO 27001 consulting services."

Focus on:

> "The project will help the organization establish an effective ISMS, address information security risks, prepare required documentation, implement relevant controls, and prepare for certification."

---

### 3. Define Project Conditions

Clearly state what is required from both sides.

#### Consultant provides:

- Consulting expertise
    
- Project guidance
    
- Documentation support
    
- Training
    
- Implementation support
    
- Audit preparation
    

#### Organization provides:

- Required personnel
    
- Access to relevant information
    
- Internal resources
    
- Management involvement
    
- Timely decisions and approvals
    
- Required infrastructure/resources
    

Also identify any **constraints or assumptions** that could affect the project.

---

### 4. Project Duration and Milestones

The proposal should specify:

- Overall project duration
    
- Start date
    
- Expected completion date
    
- Major milestones
    
- Important deliverables
    

### Example milestones

1. Initial assessment
    
2. ISMS planning
    
3. Risk assessment
    
4. Risk treatment
    
5. Documentation
    
6. Control implementation
    
7. Internal audit
    
8. Management review
    
9. Certification audit preparation
    

---

### 5. Pricing and Payment

Clearly state the financial terms, including:

- Total project price
    
- Payment schedule
    
- Payment milestones
    
- Invoice conditions
    
- Taxes
    
- Travel expenses
    
- Other reimbursable expenses
    
- Any additional costs
    

This avoids misunderstandings later.

---

### 6. Keep the Proposal Short

A good proposal should be:

- Clear
    
- Concise
    
- Easy to understand
    
- Focused on the client
    
- Relevant to the agreed project
    

Avoid unnecessary information.

Do not make the proposal mainly about:

- Your company history
    
- Your personal achievements
    
- Excessive technical details
    
- Unnecessary marketing content
    

The proposal should provide the information the client needs to make the project clear.

---

### 7. Follow Up After Sending

After sending the proposal:

- Set a reminder to follow up.
    
- Confirm that the client received it.
    
- Ask whether they have questions.
    
- Clarify any concerns.
    
- Show continued interest.
    
- Avoid being overly persistent or annoying.
    

Follow-up demonstrates **professionalism and commitment**.

---

### Key Principle

> **A good consulting proposal confirms the client's desired outcomes, clearly defines responsibilities, timeline, deliverables, and costs, and makes it easy for the organization to move forward with the project.**
