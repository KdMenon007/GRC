

When presenting an ISO 27001 project, the objective is to position yourself as a **reliable partner** who can help the organization achieve its business goals—not simply as someone who implements an ISO standard.

---

## 1. Introduce Yourself

If you are a consultant meeting a prospect for the first time, briefly explain:

- Your name
    
- Your professional experience
    
- Your ISO 27001 experience
    
- Your relevant expertise
    
- Your previous contributions
    
- What you do
    
- How you can help the organization
    

### Example

> "I help organizations implement and improve their Information Security Management Systems and prepare for ISO 27001 certification."

Keep the introduction focused on **value**, rather than giving a long description of your background.

---

## 2. Explain What You Can Help Them Achieve

Don't focus only on your services.

Explain the **outcomes the organization can obtain**.

Possible outcomes include:

- ISO 27001 certification
    
- Reduced information-security risks
    
- Improved customer confidence
    
- Better security processes
    
- Improved compliance
    
- Reduced security incidents
    
- Better risk management
    
- Improved business processes
    

### Key Principle

> **Focus on what the client will get, not just what you will do.**

---

## 3. Do Your Homework

Before the meeting, research the organization.

Understand:

- What the organization does.
    
- Its products and services.
    
- Its customers.
    
- Its industry/sector.
    
- Its business model.
    
- Its current situation.
    
- Potential security challenges.
    
- Regulatory or customer requirements.
    
- Possible reasons for pursuing ISO 27001.
    

This allows you to create a **tailored approach** rather than giving the same presentation to every prospect.

---

## 4. Tailor Your Presentation

Use your research to explain how ISO 27001 could help with the organization's specific situation.

### Example

If the organization is losing business because customers require ISO 27001:

❌ "ISO 27001 has 93 Annex A controls."

✅ "Certification could help you satisfy customer security requirements and qualify for contracts where ISO 27001 is a prerequisite."

The second approach connects the project to a **business objective**.

---

## 5. Understand Why They Need Help

Before proposing your solution, understand their situation.

Important questions include:

### Why now?

> **Why are you looking for help at this time?**

Possible reasons:

- Customer requirement
    
- Regulatory requirement
    
- Upcoming audit
    
- Security incident
    
- New market opportunity
    
- Contract requirement
    
- Management initiative
    

---

## 6. Understand Their Expected Outcome

Ask:

> **What do you want to achieve from this project?**

Possible objectives:

- Obtain ISO 27001 certification.
    
- Establish an ISMS.
    
- Improve existing security.
    
- Meet customer requirements.
    
- Prepare for certification audit.
    
- Improve risk management.
    

---

## 7. Understand How Success Will Be Measured

Ask:

> **How will you measure the success of the project?**

Possible measurements:

- Certification achieved.
    
- Number of security risks reduced.
    
- Customer requirements satisfied.
    
- Audit findings reduced.
    
- Security processes implemented.
    
- ISMS operating effectively.
    

This helps ensure that both sides have the **same definition of success**.

---

## 8. Understand Why They Chose You

Ask:

> **Why do you think external consulting support is needed?**

Also understand:

- What do they expect from you?
    
- What expertise are they missing?
    
- What problems do they expect you to solve?
    
- Why are they considering your services?
    

Your services are an **instrument for delivering value**, not the ultimate objective.

---

## 9. Discuss Project Timing

Determine:

- When they want to start.
    
- Target completion date.
    
- Target certification date.
    
- Important deadlines.
    
- Major milestones.
    

### Example

**Project Start → Gap Assessment → Risk Assessment → ISMS Implementation → Internal Audit → Management Review → Certification Audit**

---

## 10. Discuss Resources

Understand what resources the organization expects to allocate.

These may include:

- Internal employees
    
- CISO/security team
    
- IT team
    
- HR
    
- Legal/compliance
    
- Management involvement
    
- Budget
    
- Technology
    
- Training resources
    
- Time available from employees
    

A successful ISO 27001 project requires **management commitment and organizational resources**, not only a consultant.

---

## 11. Ask What Happens Next

Before ending the meeting, clarify:

- Who makes the final decision?
    
- What additional information is required?
    
- When will they make a decision?
    
- Is a proposal required?
    
- Are there other stakeholders who need to be involved?
    
- When should the next meeting happen?
    

This prevents the opportunity from becoming inactive after the first meeting.

---

# Follow-Up After the Meeting

After the meeting, send a short follow-up email.

It should include:

- Thank you for the meeting.
    
- Key points discussed.
    
- Organization's objectives.
    
- Problems/challenges identified.
    
- Proposed next steps.
    
- Agreed actions.
    
- Timeline, if discussed.
    
- Proposal or quotation, if appropriate.
    

### Example Follow-Up Structure

**Meeting → Summary → Agreed Requirements → Proposed Approach → Next Steps → Proposal**

---

# Consultant Meeting Flow

**1. Introduce yourself**  
↓  
**2. Explain your relevant experience**  
↓  
**3. Understand the organization**  
↓  
**4. Ask why they need ISO 27001 now**  
↓  
**5. Identify their goals**  
↓  
**6. Understand success criteria**  
↓  
**7. Discuss timeline and resources**  
↓  
**8. Explain how you can help achieve their goals**  
↓  
**9. Agree on next steps**  
↓  
**10. Send follow-up and proposal**

---

## Key Takeaways

- Present yourself as a **reliable partner**.
    
- Research the prospect before the meeting.
    
- Tailor the presentation to their business.
    
- Focus on **client outcomes**, not your services.
    
- Ask why they need ISO 27001 **now**.
    
- Understand their objectives and success criteria.
    
- Clarify timeline and available resources.
    
- Identify what they expect from you.
    
- Always agree on the next step.
    
- Follow up promptly with a summary and proposal when appropriate.
    

### Core Principle

> **Don't sell ISO 27001 consulting. Sell the business outcome that ISO 27001 will help the organization achieve.**
