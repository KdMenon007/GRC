## Certification Decision

### 1. Purpose of the Certification Decision

When most of the management system has been implemented and is working, the organization reaches an important milestone:

> **Should the organization proceed with certification?**

Implementing a management system **does not automatically mean that certification is required**.

The decision should be taken by **top management**, with information and recommendations provided by the **project manager and project sponsor**.

---

## 2. Benefits of Certification

### Marketing and Competitive Advantage

Certification can help the organization:

- Demonstrate credibility to customers.
    
- Qualify for certain tenders.
    
- Win new customers.
    
- Remain competitive when competitors are certified.
    
- Demonstrate commitment to information security.
    

### Compliance

Certification can support compliance by providing:

- Structured processes.
    
- Formal controls.
    
- Documented procedures.
    
- Evidence of systematic management.
    

It can make it easier for an organization to demonstrate that security requirements are being systematically managed.

### Cost Reduction

Certification may reduce certain costs when customers or partners previously required separate audits.

For example:

- Customers may conduct their own supplier audits.
    
- Multiple external assessments may be required.
    
- Certification may reduce the need for some of these individual assessments.
    

### Internal Pressure and Deadline

A certification audit provides a clear external deadline.

This can:

- Increase urgency.
    
- Keep implementation activities on schedule.
    
- Encourage management and employees to complete outstanding work.
    
- Prevent the project from continuing indefinitely.
    

### Maintaining Discipline

Certification is **not a one-time event**.

The certified organization must continue maintaining its management system and undergo surveillance/recertification activities.

This can provide continued pressure to:

- Follow procedures.
    
- Maintain controls.
    
- Monitor performance.
    
- Correct weaknesses.
    
- Prevent the gradual deterioration of security practices.
    

### Benchmarking and Improvement

Certification audits can provide an opportunity to obtain feedback from experienced auditors.

The organization can use audit findings and observations to:

- Identify weaknesses.
    
- Improve processes.
    
- Benchmark its practices.
    
- Strengthen the management system.
    

---

## 3. Disadvantages of Certification

The major disadvantage is **additional cost**.

Costs can include:

- Certification audit fees.
    
- Surveillance audits.
    
- Recertification.
    
- Consultant costs, where applicable.
    
- Employee time.
    
- Preparation and maintenance activities.
    
- Corrective-action activities.
    

Therefore:

> If customers, tenders, regulators, or business strategy do not require certification, the organization should evaluate whether the benefits justify the additional cost.

---

## 4. Top Management Decision

After evaluating the advantages and disadvantages, **top management decides whether to proceed with certification**.

### Possible decisions

**Go for certification**

Proceed with planning for the certification audit.

**Do not pursue certification**

Continue operating and improving the management system without formal certification.

The management system can still provide significant business and security benefits even without certification.

---

## 5. If the Decision Is "Go"

Once management decides to proceed with certification, several important questions must be answered.

### Management System Readiness

Determine when all relevant requirements will be implemented.

This should include:

- Required policies and procedures.
    
- Applicable controls.
    
- Risk assessment and treatment.
    
- Statement of Applicability.
    
- Internal audit.
    
- Corrective actions.
    
- Management review.
    
- Required operational evidence.
    

---

## 6. Certification Audit Date

Select an appropriate date for the certification audit.

The organization should allow sufficient time to:

- Complete implementation.
    
- Operate the controls.
    
- Collect evidence.
    
- Complete the internal audit cycle.
    
- Close significant findings.
    
- Conduct management review.
    
- Address remaining gaps.
    

Avoid scheduling the certification audit before the ISMS has had enough time to demonstrate that it is **implemented and operating effectively**.

---

## 7. Historical Evidence

The organization should determine what **historical evidence and records** will be available for the certification audit.

Examples include:

- Risk assessment records.
    
- Risk treatment records.
    
- Security incident records.
    
- Internal audit results.
    
- Corrective actions.
    
- Security monitoring results.
    
- Training and awareness records.
    
- Access reviews.
    
- Backup testing records.
    
- Management review records.
    
- Performance measurements.
    

The organization should ensure that evidence demonstrates not only that controls were designed, but that they have been **implemented and operated**.

---

## 8. Selecting a Certification Body

The organization should establish criteria for selecting a suitable **certification body**.

Consider factors such as:

- Accreditation.
    
- Experience with the organization's industry.
    
- Auditor competence.
    
- Reputation.
    
- Availability.
    
- Certification audit methodology.
    
- Cost.
    
- Geographic coverage.
    
- Experience with organizations of similar size and complexity.
    

The certification body should be appropriately **accredited** where accreditation is required or expected by the organization.

---

## 9. Project Manager's Role

The project manager should:

- Present the pros and cons of certification.
    
- Provide management with implementation status.
    
- Identify remaining gaps.
    
- Confirm readiness requirements.
    
- Help determine a realistic certification timeline.
    
- Identify required historical evidence.
    
- Support selection of a certification body.
    
- Coordinate preparation for the certification audit.
    

However, the **final decision to pursue certification belongs to top management**.

---

## 10. Certification Decision Flow

**Management system mostly implemented**  
↓  
**Evaluate benefits and disadvantages**  
↓  
**Project manager + project sponsor prepare recommendation**  
↓  
**Top management decision**  
↓

### If NO

**Continue operating and improving the management system**

### If YES

↓  
**Complete remaining requirements**  
↓  
**Complete internal audit cycle**  
↓  
**Close relevant nonconformities**  
↓  
**Conduct management review**  
↓  
**Determine required historical evidence**  
↓  
**Select certification body**  
↓  
**Agree certification audit date**  
↓  
**Prepare for certification audit**

### Key Takeaway

**Certification is a management decision, not an automatic requirement of implementing ISO 27001.**

The organization should compare the **business benefits and additional costs** and then decide whether certification supports its strategic objectives, customer requirements, competitive position, compliance needs, and business goals.
