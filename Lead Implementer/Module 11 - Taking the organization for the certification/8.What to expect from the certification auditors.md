## What Will Certification Auditors Look For?

For an ISO 27001 certification audit, the auditor is primarily checking **conformity, implementation, and effectiveness**.

### 1. What auditors will assess

They will generally verify three important relationships:

**Standard requirements → Your documented system → Actual implementation**

They will check whether:

1. **Mandatory documented information exists**
    
    - Required policies, processes, records, and other documented information are maintained as applicable.
        
2. **Your documented information meets the standard**
    
    - Your ISMS documentation addresses the applicable ISO 27001 requirements.
        
3. **Actual activities follow your documented processes**
    
    - What employees actually do should be consistent with your policies and procedures.
        
4. **The ISMS achieves its intended outcomes**
    
    - This is particularly important.
        
    - Having documents alone is not enough.
        
    - The auditor wants evidence that the ISMS is actually working and supporting information security objectives.
        

### Simple example

Suppose your procedure says:

> "Security incidents must be reported through the incident management process."

The auditor may:

- Ask an employee how they report an incident.
    
- Examine the incident procedure.
    
- Review actual incident records.
    
- Check whether incidents were investigated and handled according to the process.
    

So the auditor is essentially checking:

**What you say → What you document → What you do → What results you achieve**

---

# What Can Annoy Auditors?

Auditors need cooperation to perform their work effectively.

Avoid:

- Wasting the auditor's time.
    
- Deliberately avoiding questions.
    
- Hiding information.
    
- Providing misleading information.
    
- Giving answers that are not supported by evidence.
    
- Arguing simply because you dislike a finding.
    

Most importantly:

> **Never lie to an auditor.**

If you don't know the answer, say that you don't know and find the appropriate information.

---

# What Can a Certification Auditor Do?

Within the agreed certification scope, the auditor can generally:

### Interview people

The auditor can speak with employees, managers, process owners, and other relevant personnel.

### Examine documented information

The auditor can request relevant:

- Policies
    
- Procedures
    
- Records
    
- Logs
    
- Reports
    
- Risk assessments
    
- SoA
    
- Audit records
    
- Management review records
    
- Corrective-action records
    

### Visit relevant areas

The auditor can inspect areas covered by the certification scope and follow audit trails to obtain objective evidence.

---

# What Is the Auditor NOT?

A certification auditor is **not your consultant**.

Their role is to independently assess whether your management system conforms to the applicable requirements.

They should not:

- Design your ISMS for you.
    
- Write your procedures.
    
- Tell you exactly which solution to implement.
    
- Implement corrective actions on your behalf.
    

They may, however, provide limited clarification or general guidance about addressing a nonconformity without becoming your consultant.

---

# When Can an Auditor Raise a Nonconformity?

A nonconformity should be based on **objective audit evidence**.

There should be:

**1. A requirement**

The requirement must come from an applicable source, such as:

- ISO 27001
    
- Applicable legal/contractual requirements
    
- Your organization's own documented requirements
    

**2. Evidence of nonconformity**

The auditor must have evidence showing that the requirement has not been fulfilled.

### Example

If your procedure says:

> "Access reviews shall be performed quarterly."

And the auditor finds that the required quarterly reviews were not performed, there is a clear basis:

**Requirement:** Quarterly access review  
**Evidence:** Required review was not performed  
**Conclusion:** Potential nonconformity

---

# What If You Disagree With the Auditor?

You should **professionally challenge the finding when you have objective evidence**.

For example, suppose an auditor says:

> "You must maintain this record in paper form."

You can ask:

> "Could you please identify the specific requirement that requires this record to be maintained in paper format?"

If the requirement does not specify paper, the organization may be able to maintain the information electronically, provided the applicable requirements are satisfied.

Similarly, if an auditor concludes from one isolated example that a process is **never** performed, you can provide objective evidence demonstrating that the process is actually performed.

The key is:

> **Don't argue based on opinion. Challenge the finding using the requirement and objective evidence.**

## ⭐ Interview / Exam Summary

|Question|Key Answer|
|---|---|
|What does the auditor check?|Conformity, implementation and effectiveness|
|Is documentation alone sufficient?|No|
|What is the main focus?|Whether the management system achieves intended outcomes|
|Can the auditor interview employees?|Yes, within the certification scope|
|Can the auditor examine documents?|Yes, relevant documented information|
|Can the auditor inspect premises?|Yes, within the audit scope|
|Is the auditor a consultant?|No|
|Can the auditor implement solutions?|No|
|What is required for a nonconformity?|Applicable requirement + objective evidence of nonconformity|
|Should you lie to an auditor?|Never|
|Can you challenge an auditor's finding?|Yes, professionally, using requirements and objective evidence|

### The golden rule

**Don't prepare employees to "pass the audit." Prepare the organization to demonstrate what it actually does.**

That distinction is critical in ISO 27001: **a well-written ISMS that isn't implemented will not withstand a Stage 2 audit.**
