
### Certification audit flow

**1. Stage 1 — Readiness & documentation review**

- Review ISMS documentation against ISO/IEC 27001 requirements.
    
- Confirm the **scope of certification** is clear.
    
- Review whether **internal audits and management reviews** are planned/performed.
    
- Determine whether the organization is ready for Stage 2.
    
- Any Stage 1 nonconformities should be addressed before Stage 2.
    
- If the organization is not ready, Stage 2 may be postponed.
    

**2. Stage 2 — Implementation & effectiveness**  
The auditor now verifies whether the ISMS actually works in practice.

Typical audit techniques:

- Interviews with management and employees.
    
- Examination of records and evidence.
    
- Site/department visits.
    
- Following audit trails.
    
- Checking whether policies and procedures are actually implemented.
    
- Evaluating whether the ISMS achieves its intended outcomes.
    

The key distinction is:

> **Stage 1 asks: “Are you ready?”**  
> **Stage 2 asks: “Are you actually doing it, and does it work?”**

### Stage 2 findings

|Finding|Meaning|Certification impact|
|---|---|---|
|**Major nonconformity**|Significant breakdown/failure of the ISMS|Certification cannot proceed until appropriately addressed|
|**Minor nonconformity**|Limited failure that does not represent a major system breakdown|Certification may proceed subject to the certification body's process|
|**Observation**|Potential opportunity for improvement|Not necessarily a nonconformity|

### 3. Certificate issuance

After successful completion of the certification process and resolution of applicable findings, the certification body can issue the certificate.

The certificate is generally valid for **three years**, subject to successful ongoing surveillance.

### 4. Surveillance audits

During the three-year certification cycle:

- Surveillance audits are conducted periodically.
    
- The organization must continue operating and maintaining the ISMS.
    
- The certification body checks whether the ISMS remains conforming and effective.
    

Certification is therefore **not a one-time achievement**.

### 5. Recertification

Before the three-year certificate expires, the organization undergoes a **recertification audit**.

The auditor considers:

- Performance during the previous certification cycle.
    
- Previous audit findings and corrective actions.
    
- Trends in nonconformities.
    
- Continued conformity with ISO/IEC 27001.
    
- Evidence of continual improvement.
    
- Effectiveness of the overall ISMS.
    

### Easy interview answer

If asked **“Explain the ISO 27001 certification process”**, you can answer:

> “The ISO 27001 certification process starts with selecting and contracting an accredited certification body. The certification audit normally has two stages. Stage 1 evaluates the ISMS documentation, scope, readiness, internal audits and management review. Stage 2 evaluates actual implementation and effectiveness through interviews, records, site visits and audit trails. If major nonconformities are identified, certification cannot proceed until they are appropriately addressed. Once the certification requirements are satisfied, the certificate is issued, generally for a three-year cycle. During that cycle, surveillance audits are performed periodically, followed by a recertification audit before the certificate expires.”

### Overall picture

**Implementation → Internal Audit → Management Review → Certification Decision → Stage 1 → Corrective Actions → Stage 2 → Certification → Surveillance → Recertification**

This is the practical lifecycle you should remember for your **ISO 27001 implementation/project-manager interview**.
