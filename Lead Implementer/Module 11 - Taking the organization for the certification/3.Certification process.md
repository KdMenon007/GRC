## ISO 27001 Certification Process

### 1. Overview

The certification of an ISO 27001 management system is performed through a series of stages.

The typical certification process is:

**Offer & Contract → Audit Preparation → Stage 1 Audit → Stage 2 Audit → Certificate Issuance → Surveillance Audits → Recertification**

---

## 2. Offer and Contract Phase

After selecting a certification body:

- The certification body provides an offer/proposal.
    
- The organization reviews the scope and audit arrangements.
    
- Audit costs and conditions are agreed.
    
- A formal contract is established.
    

The agreed **ISMS scope** is an important part of the certification arrangement.

---

## 3. Audit Preparation

Before the certification audit, the certification body prepares for the audit based on information provided by the organization.

This may include reviewing:

- Organization size
    
- Number of employees
    
- Locations
    
- ISMS scope
    
- Business activities
    
- Complexity of operations
    
- Shift patterns
    
- Temporary sites
    
- Applicable certification requirements
    

The organization should also ensure that the ISMS is ready for the certification audit.

---

## 4. Optional Pre-Audit

An organization may request an **optional pre-audit** before the formal certification process.

The purpose is to:

- Identify potential gaps.
    
- Obtain feedback about conformity.
    
- Understand areas requiring improvement.
    
- Improve readiness for the formal certification audit.
    

A pre-audit is **optional** and is separate from the formal certification audit.

---

# 5. Stage 1 Audit

Stage 1 is the **initial certification audit preparation and readiness assessment**.

A major purpose is to evaluate the organization's documented ISMS and determine whether it is ready to proceed to Stage 2.

The auditor reviews areas such as:

- ISMS scope
    
- Organizational context
    
- Information security policy
    
- Risk assessment methodology
    
- Risk assessment results
    
- Risk treatment approach
    
- Statement of Applicability
    
- Information security objectives
    
- ISMS documentation
    
- Understanding of applicable requirements
    
- Readiness for Stage 2
    

The auditor also evaluates whether the organization is sufficiently prepared for the **Stage 2 audit**.

### Stage 1 Key Question

> **Is the organization ready for the Stage 2 certification audit?**

---

# 6. Stage 2 Audit

Stage 2 is the main **certification audit**.

The auditor evaluates whether the ISMS:

- Has been implemented.
    
- Is operating effectively.
    
- Conforms to ISO 27001 requirements.
    
- Meets the organization's own ISMS requirements.
    
- Has implemented applicable controls.
    
- Is achieving intended outcomes.
    

The auditor gathers objective evidence through activities such as:

- Interviews
    
- Document review
    
- Observation
    
- Sampling
    
- System and process verification
    
- Examination of records
    
- Checking implemented controls
    

### Stage 2 Key Question

> **Is the ISMS implemented and operating effectively in practice?**

---

## 7. Audit Findings

During Stage 1 and Stage 2, auditors may identify findings such as:

- Nonconformities
    
- Areas requiring correction
    
- Observations or improvement opportunities
    

The organization may need to:

- Analyze the finding.
    
- Determine the cause.
    
- Take corrective action.
    
- Provide evidence of correction/corrective action.
    

The certification decision depends on the results of the certification audit and resolution of applicable findings according to the certification body's rules.

---

# 8. Certificate Issuance

After successful completion of the certification process and the certification decision, the certification body issues the **ISO 27001 certificate**.

The certificate identifies information such as:

- Certified organization
    
- Applicable ISO 27001 standard
    
- ISMS certification scope
    
- Certification validity
    
- Certification body
    

Certification demonstrates that the organization's ISMS has been independently assessed against the applicable certification requirements.

---

# 9. Surveillance Audits

Certification does not mean that the organization can stop maintaining the ISMS.

After certification, the organization undergoes **surveillance audits** during the certification cycle.

These audits verify that the organization continues to:

- Maintain the ISMS.
    
- Operate its controls.
    
- Meet ISO 27001 requirements.
    
- Address identified issues.
    
- Continually improve the system.
    

---

# 10. Recertification

At the end of the certification cycle, the organization undergoes a **recertification audit** to continue its certification.

The organization must demonstrate that its ISMS continues to be:

- Suitable
    
- Adequate
    
- Effective
    
- Maintained
    
- Continually improved
    

---

## 11. Remote Auditing

Certification audits are commonly performed at the organization's relevant locations, but **some audit activities may be performed remotely**, depending on the certification body's audit arrangements and applicable requirements.

Examples may include:

- Document review
    
- Interviews
    
- Review of electronic records
    
- Verification of certain information
    

The certification body determines the appropriate audit approach.

---

## Certification Process — Simple Flow

**Select Certification Body**  
↓  
**Offer & Contract**  
↓  
**Audit Preparation**  
↓  
**Optional Pre-Audit**  
↓  
**Stage 1 Audit**  
_Readiness + ISMS documentation/evidence review_  
↓  
**Stage 2 Audit**  
_Implementation + effectiveness assessment_  
↓  
**Certification Decision**  
↓  
**ISO 27001 Certificate**  
↓  
**Surveillance Audits**  
↓  
**Recertification**

### Key Takeaway

**Stage 1 = Readiness and ISMS review**

**Stage 2 = Full implementation and effectiveness assessment**

**Certification = Successful audit + certification decision**

After certification, the organization must continue operating and improving its ISMS through the ongoing surveillance and recertification cycle.
