## Certification Audit: Closing Meeting, Findings, and Corrective Actions

At the end of the certification audit, the audit team formally communicates its findings and determines whether the organization can proceed toward certification.

### 1. Audit Report

The audit team prepares an **audit report** containing:

- Audit findings.
    
- Nonconformities.
    
- Classification of nonconformities:
    
    - **Major**
        
    - **Minor**
        
- Observations or opportunities for improvement.
    
- Overall audit conclusions.
    
- Certification recommendation, where applicable.
    

---

## 2. Closing Meeting

The certification body conducts a **closing meeting** with relevant management and organizational representatives.

The auditors:

- Present the audit results.
    
- Explain the findings.
    
- Explain any nonconformities.
    
- Clarify their significance.
    
- Communicate the next steps.
    
- State whether the organization is recommended for certification, subject to the certification body's formal decision process.
    

A useful principle is:

> **The closing meeting should not be the first time management hears about a serious problem.**

Significant findings should normally have been discussed with the relevant people during the audit.

---

# 3. Major vs. Minor Nonconformity

### Major Nonconformity

A major nonconformity represents a **significant failure of the management system**.

Examples include:

### Complete failure to meet a requirement

For example:

> The organization completely fails to perform a required management review.

### Significant breakdown of a process

Suppose the organization's procedure requires daily backups, but backups are performed only randomly a few times per month.

That may indicate that the backup process is not effectively implemented.

### Multiple related minor nonconformities

Several apparently minor findings can indicate a **systemic failure**.

For example, within HR:

- Training records are missing.
    
- Required employees have not received training.
    
- Employees cannot demonstrate required competence.
    

Individually these might appear limited, but together they may demonstrate a significant breakdown in the competence process.

### Certification impact

A major nonconformity generally prevents certification from being granted until the certification body is satisfied that the nonconformity has been appropriately addressed.

---

# 4. Minor Nonconformity

A minor nonconformity is a **limited failure that does not amount to a major breakdown** of the management system.

Example:

> A required backup was performed every day except one day during the month, with evidence showing that the backup process is otherwise operating effectively.

The exact classification always depends on the applicable certification rules and the auditor's evaluation of the evidence.

---

# 5. Corrective Action

After receiving nonconformities, the organization must determine appropriate corrective actions.

The key point is:

> **Don't just fix the symptom. Address the cause.**

A good corrective-action process is:

**Nonconformity identified**  
↓  
**Contain/correct the immediate problem**  
↓  
**Determine root cause**  
↓  
**Implement corrective action**  
↓  
**Verify effectiveness**  
↓  
**Provide evidence to certification body**

### Example

**Finding:** An employee's access was not removed after leaving the organization.

Weak response:

> "Remove that account."

Better corrective action:

1. Disable the account.
    
2. Investigate why the account-removal process failed.
    
3. Identify the root cause.
    
4. Improve the joiner/mover/leaver process.
    
5. Establish appropriate notifications and responsibilities.
    
6. Verify that subsequent leavers have their access removed correctly.
    
7. Provide evidence to the certification body.
    

---

# 6. Submit Evidence

Once corrective actions have been implemented, the organization should provide the certification body with appropriate evidence.

Examples:

- Revised procedure.
    
- Completed records.
    
- Access-review evidence.
    
- Training records.
    
- Updated system configuration.
    
- Internal verification results.
    
- Root-cause analysis.
    
- Corrective-action records.
    

The certification body evaluates whether the evidence demonstrates that the nonconformity has been appropriately addressed.

---

# 7. Additional Verification

For significant/major nonconformities, the certification body may require **additional verification**, potentially including another audit activity, to confirm that:

- Corrective actions were implemented.
    
- The underlying cause was addressed.
    
- The corrective actions are effective.
    

For minor nonconformities, the certification body's process may allow verification through submitted evidence and/or subsequent surveillance activity.

---

# 8. After Certification

Once the certification requirements have been successfully satisfied, the certification body issues the certificate.

But certification is **not permanent**.

The certification cycle generally lasts **three years**, with surveillance activities during the cycle and recertification before expiry.

A significant failure discovered during surveillance can put the certification status at risk, including possible suspension depending on the certification body's process.

---
### Remember this sequence

**Audit → Findings → Closing Meeting → Corrective Action → Evidence → Verification → Certification → Surveillance → Recertification**

The most important lesson is:

> **Certification is not the end of ISO 27001. It is the beginning of maintaining and continually improving the ISMS.**
