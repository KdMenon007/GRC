
### 1. Purpose

Choosing a certification body should **not be based only on price**.

The organization should evaluate several factors to select a certification body that is:

- Credible
    
- Competent
    
- Appropriate for the organization's industry
    
- Properly accredited
    
- Experienced
    
- Suitable for the organization's needs
    

---

## 2. Selection Criteria

### Price

Price is an important factor, but it should **not be the only criterion**.

The organization should:

- Request quotations from multiple certification bodies.
    
- Compare what is included in each proposal.
    
- Compare audit fees and other associated costs.
    
- Consider the overall value provided.
    

---

### Reputation

Choose a certification body with a **strong and credible reputation**.

A reputable certification body should:

- Be recognized in the industry.
    
- Conduct meaningful and professional audits.
    
- Maintain credibility of its certificates.
    
- Provide confidence to customers and other interested parties.
    

If certification will be used as a **marketing or competitive advantage**, the reputation of the certification body becomes especially important.

---

### Accreditation

Check whether the certification body is **properly accredited** to issue accredited ISO 27001 certificates.

Accreditation provides confidence that the certification body operates according to recognized certification requirements.

> **Certification body ≠ Accreditation body**

The certification body performs the audit and issues the certificate, while an accreditation body assesses the competence and conformity of certification bodies.

---

### Industry Specialization

Consider whether the certification body has experience in the organization's **industry or sector**.

For example:

- A bank should preferably select a certification body with financial-sector experience.
    
- A healthcare organization should consider experience with healthcare.
    
- A manufacturing company may benefit from an auditor experienced in manufacturing.
    

Industry experience helps the auditor understand the organization's:

- Processes
    
- Risks
    
- Regulatory environment
    
- Business model
    
- Information security challenges
    

---

### Auditor Experience

The experience and competence of the auditor are important.

An inexperienced auditor may appear attractive because the organization expects an easier audit, but this can be a disadvantage.

An experienced auditor can provide valuable insights about:

- Information security risks.
    
- Industry practices.
    
- Control effectiveness.
    
- Potential weaknesses.
    
- Opportunities for improvement.
    

The objective should be to select a **competent and experienced auditor**, not simply the auditor who is easiest to pass.

---

### Language

Language should also be considered.

An auditor who speaks the organization's working language can:

- Understand documentation more easily.
    
- Communicate effectively with employees.
    
- Conduct interviews more efficiently.
    
- Reduce misunderstandings.
    
- Build better communication during the audit.
    

Although translation services may be available, having a common language can make the audit smoother.

---

## 3. Shortlist Certification Bodies

After defining the selection criteria:

1. Identify potential certification bodies.
    
2. Evaluate their reputation.
    
3. Check accreditation.
    
4. Check industry specialization.
    
5. Review auditor experience.
    
6. Consider language requirements.
    
7. Compare pricing.
    
8. Shortlist approximately **2–3 suitable certification bodies**.
    

---

## 4. Request for Quotation (RFQ)

Send a **Request for Quotation (RFQ)** to the shortlisted certification bodies.

The certification body will require information about the organization to determine the audit effort and price.

Typical information includes:

### Organization Size

- Number of employees.
    
- Relevant personnel involved in the ISMS.
    

### Industry / Activity

The certification body may request the organization's **industry/activity code** used for certification purposes.

### Locations

Provide information about:

- Number of fixed sites.
    
- Multiple offices or locations.
    
- Scope of activities at each location.
    

### ISMS Scope

Clearly define what will be covered by the certification.

For example:

> "Information security management covering the IT services and supporting processes of the organization's Bangalore operations."

### Shift Work

Inform the certification body if the organization operates:

- Multiple shifts.
    
- 24×7 operations.
    
- Night shifts.
    

### Temporary Sites

Mention any temporary or changing locations relevant to the certification scope.

Examples:

- Project sites
    
- Temporary production locations
    
- Customer locations
    
- Construction or field sites
    

---

## 5. Final Selection

After comparing the proposals, the organization should select the certification body that provides the **best overall fit**, rather than simply choosing the cheapest option.

Consider:

|Criterion|What to Evaluate|
|---|---|
|Price|Overall audit and certification cost|
|Reputation|Industry credibility|
|Accreditation|Ability to issue accredited certificates|
|Specialization|Experience in your industry|
|Experience|Auditor competence and experience|
|Language|Effective communication|
|Scope|Ability to audit the required ISMS scope|
|Availability|Suitable audit dates|
|Value|Overall quality and usefulness|

---

## 6. Application

Once the certification body has been selected:

- Submit the certification application.
    
- Provide the requested organizational information.
    
- Confirm the ISMS scope.
    
- Agree on certification arrangements.
    
- Provide information required for audit planning.
    
- Schedule the certification audit.
    

---

### Key Takeaway

**Choosing a certification body = Price + Accreditation + Reputation + Industry Experience + Auditor Competence + Language + Overall Fit**

The goal is not to find the **cheapest certification body** or the one that gives the easiest audit. The goal is to select a **credible, accredited and competent certification body** that understands the organization's industry and can provide a trustworthy assessment of the ISO 27001 ISMS.
