# 1. What is ISO/IEC 27001?

## Definition

**ISO/IEC 27001** is an international standard that defines requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an **Information Security Management System (ISMS)** within an organization.

It provides a **systematic, structured, and risk-based approach** for managing information security risks and protecting organizational information assets from:

- Unauthorized access
    
- Data breaches
    
- Cyber attacks
    
- Accidental data loss
    
- Information misuse
    
- Operational disruptions
    

### Simple Definition

> **ISO 27001 is a framework that helps organizations identify information security risks, implement appropriate security controls, and continuously improve their security posture.**

---

## ISO 27001 is a Management System Standard

ISO/IEC 27001 is not only a technology or cybersecurity standard.

It does not specify:

- Which firewall product to purchase
    
- Which antivirus solution to deploy
    
- Which SIEM platform to use
    

Instead, it defines **how an organization should manage information security systematically.**

ISO 27001 combines:

- People
    
- Processes
    
- Technology
    
- Governance
    
- Risk Management
    

to protect organizational information assets.

# 3. Purpose of ISO 27001

The primary purpose of ISO 27001 is to help organizations:

- Protect confidential information
    
- Ensure information accuracy and reliability
    
- Maintain availability of critical information
    
- Reduce cybersecurity risks

ISO 27001 focuses on protecting information through the three fundamental principles of information security:

- Confidentiality

# 4. CIA Triad in ISO 27001

Information security is based on three fundamental security principles.

## 1. Confidentiality

### Definition

Ensures information is accessible only to authorized users.

### Objective

Prevent unauthorized disclosure of information.

### Examples:

- Access control
    
- Encryption
    
- Identity and Access Management (IAM)
    
- Data classification
    

### Example:

Customer account information should only be accessible by authorized banking employees.

---

## 2. Integrity

### Definition

Ensures information remains accurate, complete, and protected from unauthorized modification.

### Objective

Prevent unauthorized alteration or destruction of information.

### Examples:

- Hashing
    
- Digital signatures
    
- File integrity monitoring
    
- Change management
    

### Example:

Prevent unauthorized modification of financial transaction records.

---

## 3. Availability

### Definition

Ensures information and systems are available when required by authorized users.

### Objective

Maintain reliable access to critical services.

### Examples:

- Backup systems
    
- Disaster recovery
    
- High availability solutions
    
- Business continuity planning
    

### Example:

Ensuring banking applications remain operational 24/7.

# 5. What is ISMS?

## Information Security Management System

An **ISMS is a structured management system used to manage information security risks within an organization.**

It provides a framework to:

- Identify security risks
    
- Implement security controls
    
- Monitor effectiveness
    
- Improve security continuously
    

---

## Components of ISMS

An ISMS includes:

- Information security policies
    
- Risk assessment processes
    
- Security controls
    
- Roles and responsibilities
    
- Procedures
    
- Monitoring activities
    
- Internal audits
    
- Continual improvement processes
    

---

## ISMS Example

A bank manages:

- Customer account information
    
- Transaction data
    
- Employee records
    

ISMS helps the bank to:

1. Identify risks
    
2. Analyze threats
    
3. Implement controls
    
4. Monitor security effectiveness
    
5. Improve security continuously

# 2. R – Risk View

## Definition

Risk management identifies, analyzes, evaluates, and treats security risks that may impact information assets.

---

## Asset Protection Requirements

Organizations identify critical assets:

Examples:

- Customer databases
    
- Servers
    
- Applications
    
- Intellectual property
    
- Business systems
    

---

## Risk Exposure

Risk is influenced by:

```
Risk = Threat × Vulnerability × Impact
```

### Example:

**Asset:**

Customer database

**Threat:**

SQL Injection attack

**Vulnerability:**

Unpatched application

**Impact:**

Customer data theft

**Risk:**

High

---

## Risk Appetite

Risk appetite defines the amount of risk an organization is willing to accept.

### Example:

Bank:

- Low risk appetite
    
- Cannot accept customer data exposure
    

Startup:

- Higher risk appetite
    
- May accept some operational risks
    

---

## Risk Management Process

ISO 27001 follows:

```
Identify Risk
      ↓
Analyze Risk
      ↓
Evaluate Risk
      ↓
Treat Risk
      ↓
Monitor Risk
```

---

## Risk Treatment Options

Organizations can:

1. Avoid risk
    
2. Reduce/Mitigate risk
    
3. Transfer risk
    
4. Accept risk

# 7. ISO 27001 Risk-Based Approach

ISO 27001 follows a **risk-based approach**.

Organizations do not implement controls blindly.

Controls are selected based on:

- Business requirements
    
- Asset criticality
    
- Risk level
    
- Compliance requirements
    

---

       ↓

# 9. ISO 27001 Structure (ISO/IEC 27001:2022)

ISO 27001 consists of two major parts:

## 1. Main Standard Requirements (Clauses 0–10)

Defines mandatory requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and improving an ISMS.

## 2. Annex A Security Controls

Contains security controls used to manage information security risks.

ISO 27001:2022 includes:

- 93 controls
    
- 4 control categories
    

---

# ISO 27001 Clauses 0–10

|Clause|Title|Purpose|
|---|---|---|
|Clause 0|Introduction|Overview of ISO 27001|
|Clause 1|Scope|Defines applicability|
|Clause 2|Normative References|Related standards|
|Clause 3|Terms and Definitions|Defines terminology|
|Clause 4|Context of Organization|Business environment and ISMS scope|
|Clause 5|Leadership|Management commitment and governance|
|Clause 6|Planning|Risk assessment and objectives|
|Clause 7|Support|Resources, competence, awareness, communication, documentation|
|Clause 8|Operation|ISMS implementation and operation|
|Clause 9|Performance Evaluation|Monitoring, measurement, audits, reviews|
|Clause 10|Improvement|Corrective actions and continual improvement|
