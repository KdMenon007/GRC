
## 1. Where We Are in the Risk Management Process

The complete flow is:

```text
1. Define Risk Assessment Methodology
                ↓
2. Risk Identification
                ↓
3. Risk Analysis
                ↓
4. Risk Evaluation
                ↓
5. Risk Treatment
                ↓
6. Statement of Applicability (SoA)
                ↓
7. Risk Treatment Plan
                ↓
8. Implement Controls
```

You have already covered **Risk Identification**.

Now we move to:

```text
Risk Identification
        ↓
Risk Analysis
        ↓
Risk Evaluation
```

---

# 2. What Is Risk Analysis?

**Risk analysis** determines the level or significance of an identified risk.

It generally considers two major factors:

1. **Impact**
    
2. **Likelihood**
    

### Simple formula

A commonly used approach is:

```text
Risk Level = Impact × Likelihood
```

However, ISO/IEC 27001 does **not prescribe one mandatory mathematical formula**.

An organization must define its methodology and criteria.

---

# 3. Impact

**Impact** means:

> What would happen to the organization if the risk actually materialized?

The assessment should consider the effect on:

- **Confidentiality**
    
- **Integrity**
    
- **Availability**
    

These are the three fundamental information security objectives — **CIA**.

---

# 4. Confidentiality Impact

Confidentiality is about preventing unauthorized disclosure of information.

### Example

Suppose an employee's confidential salary document is accessed by an unauthorized person.

```text
Asset:
Employee salary information

Threat:
Unauthorized access

Impact:
Confidentiality compromised
```

If the information is highly sensitive, the confidentiality impact could be **5/5**.

Possible consequences include:

- Privacy violations
    
- Regulatory penalties
    
- Reputation damage
    
- Loss of customer trust
    

---

# 5. Integrity Impact

Integrity means ensuring that information is:

- Accurate
    
- Complete
    
- Correct
    
- Not improperly modified
    

### Example

An attacker modifies transaction information in a banking database.

```text
Asset:
Transaction database

Threat:
Unauthorized modification

Impact:
Integrity compromised
```

If incorrect information could cause major financial or operational consequences, the integrity impact could be very high.

---

# 6. Availability Impact

Availability means information and systems are accessible when required.

### Example

A production server goes offline.

```text
Asset:
Production server

Threat:
Power outage

Vulnerability:
No UPS

Risk:
Temporary server unavailability

Impact:
Availability
```

The information may still be intact and confidential, but users cannot access it.

Therefore:

> **The primary security impact is on Availability.**

---

# 7. CIA Impact Assessment

A risk can affect one, two, or all three CIA objectives.

For example:

|Risk|Confidentiality|Integrity|Availability|
|---|--:|--:|--:|
|Unauthorized database access|High|Low|Low|
|Data manipulation|Medium|High|Low|
|Server outage|Low|Low|High|
|Ransomware|High|High|High|
|Lost unencrypted laptop|High|Low|Medium|

This helps the organization understand **what type of damage the risk could cause**.

---

# 8. Impact Scales

The organization decides how impact will be measured.

For example:

### 3-Level Scale

|Rating|Meaning|
|---|---|
|1|Low|
|2|Medium|
|3|High|

Or:

### 5-Level Scale

|Rating|Meaning|
|---|---|
|1|Very Low|
|2|Low|
|3|Medium|
|4|High|
|5|Very High|

An organization could also use a **1–10 scale**.

There is no requirement that every organization use the same scale.

The important point is:

> **The organization must define its assessment criteria in its risk assessment methodology and apply them consistently.**

---

# 9. Likelihood

The second major component of risk analysis is **likelihood**.

Likelihood asks:

> **How likely is it that this risk will actually occur?**

The organization can consider factors such as:

- Historical incidents
    
- Frequency of previous events
    
- Existing controls
    
- Threat intelligence
    
- Exposure of the asset
    
- Vulnerability level
    
- Attack activity
    
- Environmental conditions
    

---

## Example Likelihood Scale

A 1–5 scale could be:

|Rating|Likelihood|
|--:|---|
|1|Very unlikely|
|2|Unlikely|
|3|Possible|
|4|Likely|
|5|Very likely|

Again, the organization determines the appropriate scale.

---

# 10. Example — Electricity Outage

Let's use the same example from your material.

### Asset

**Server**

### Threat

**Electricity outage**

### Vulnerability

**No UPS**

### Risk

> Temporary unavailability of the server due to electricity outage.

Now we analyze the risk.

### Impact

The main impact is on:

**Availability**

Suppose the organization assigns:

```text
Impact = 4
```

Why?

Because the server is important, but the outage is expected to be **temporary**.

---

### Likelihood

Suppose historical data shows that electricity outages don't happen very frequently.

Therefore:

```text
Likelihood = 2
```

---

# 11. Calculating the Risk Level

The organization must define how impact and likelihood are combined.

For example:

```text
Risk = Impact + Likelihood
```

Therefore:

```text
Impact     = 4
Likelihood = 2

Risk Level = 4 + 2

Risk Level = 6
```

So the resulting risk level is:

> **6**

---

# 12. Another Common Formula

Another organization might use:

```text
Risk = Impact × Likelihood
```

For the same values:

```text
Impact     = 4
Likelihood = 2

Risk = 4 × 2

Risk = 8
```

Notice something important:

**The same risk produces different numerical results depending on the methodology.**

Therefore, the organization must clearly define:

- Impact scale
    
- Likelihood scale
    
- Risk calculation method
    
- Risk classification
    
- Risk acceptance criteria
    

in its **Risk Assessment Methodology**.

---

# 13. Risk Matrix

A very common approach is a risk matrix.

For example, using:

```text
Risk = Impact × Likelihood
```

we could have:

|Impact \ Likelihood|1|2|3|4|5|
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
|**1**|1|2|3|4|5|
|**2**|2|4|6|8|10|
|**3**|3|6|9|12|15|
|**4**|4|8|12|16|20|
|**5**|5|10|15|20|25|

The organization can then define categories such as:

```text
1–5    → Low
6–10   → Medium
11–15  → High
16–25  → Very High
```

These thresholds are only examples. Each organization defines its own criteria.

---

# 14. What Is Risk Evaluation?

After calculating the risk level, we move to **risk evaluation**.

Risk evaluation asks:

> **Is this risk acceptable to the organization?**

The organization compares the calculated risk against its predefined **risk acceptance criteria**.

For example:

```text
Acceptable Risk ≤ 6

Unacceptable Risk ≥ 7
```

Suppose we have:

```text
Risk Level = 6
```

Then:

```text
6 ≤ 6
```

Therefore:

> **The risk is acceptable.**

But if the risk were:

```text
Risk Level = 8
```

then:

```text
8 > 6
```

Therefore:

> **The risk is unacceptable and must be treated.**

---

# 15. Risk Acceptance Criteria

This is a very important concept.

An organization cannot realistically eliminate every possible risk.

Therefore, it needs to define:

> **What level of risk are we willing to accept?**

For example:

```text
Risk Level 1–6
       ↓
Acceptable

Risk Level 7–25
       ↓
Unacceptable
       ↓
Risk Treatment Required
```

The actual threshold depends on the organization's:

- Business requirements
    
- Risk appetite
    
- Regulatory obligations
    
- Customer requirements
    
- Financial situation
    
- Criticality of information
    
- Business impact
    

---

# 16. Example With Multiple Risks

Suppose an organization identifies these risks:

|Risk|Impact|Likelihood|Risk Level|Evaluation|
|---|--:|--:|--:|---|
|Server outage|4|2|6|Acceptable|
|Customer data disclosure|5|3|8|Unacceptable|
|Ransomware|5|4|9|Unacceptable|
|Printer failure|2|1|3|Acceptable|

Assuming:

```text
Risk = Impact + Likelihood

Acceptable ≤ 6
Unacceptable ≥ 7
```

Then:

### Server outage

```text
4 + 2 = 6
```

Acceptable.

### Customer data disclosure

```text
5 + 3 = 8
```

Unacceptable.

### Ransomware

```text
5 + 4 = 9
```

Unacceptable.

### Printer failure

```text
2 + 1 = 3
```

Acceptable.

---

# 17. What Happens to Unacceptable Risks?

This is where **risk treatment** begins.

```text
Risk Analysis
      ↓
Risk Evaluation
      ↓
Is risk acceptable?
      |
   +--+--+
   |     |
  YES    NO
   |     |
Accept   Risk
risk     treatment
          ↓
      Controls
```

For unacceptable risks, the organization selects an appropriate treatment option.

The common options are:

1. **Avoid the risk**
    
2. **Modify/reduce the risk**
    
3. **Share/transfer the risk**
    
4. **Retain/accept the risk**
    

---

# 18. Difference Between Analysis and Evaluation

This is a common interview question.

### Risk Analysis

Determines:

> **How much risk do we have?**

It considers:

- Impact
    
- Likelihood
    
- Risk level
    

### Risk Evaluation

Determines:

> **Is that level of risk acceptable?**

It compares:

```text
Calculated Risk
       ↓
Risk Acceptance Criteria
       ↓
Acceptable / Unacceptable
```

### Easy memory

> **Analysis = Calculate**  
> **Evaluation = Decide**

# 19. Complete Example

Let's combine everything from identification through evaluation.

### Step 1 — Asset

```text
Production Server
```

### Step 2 — Threat

```text
Electricity outage
```

### Step 3 — Vulnerability

```text
No UPS
```

### Step 4 — Risk

```text
Temporary unavailability of the server
```

### Step 5 — Impact

Availability is primarily affected.

```text
Impact = 4
```

### Step 6 — Likelihood

Based on historical frequency:

```text
Likelihood = 2
```

### Step 7 — Risk Calculation

Using the organization's defined formula:

```text
Risk = Impact + Likelihood

Risk = 4 + 2

Risk = 6
```

### Step 8 — Risk Evaluation

Suppose:

```text
Acceptable Risk ≤ 6
```

Therefore:

```text
6 = Acceptable
```

The organization may decide to **retain/accept the risk**, subject to its methodology and approval requirements.

# 20. What Must Be Documented?

ISO/IEC 27001 requires the organization to retain **documented information about the results of the information security risk assessments**.

The organization's methodology should establish things such as:

- How impact is assessed
    
- How likelihood is assessed
    
- How risk levels are calculated
    
- How risks are classified
    
- Risk acceptance criteria
    
- How consistently the assessment is performed
    

A risk register might therefore contain:

|Asset|Threat|Vulnerability|Risk|CIA Impact|Impact|Likelihood|Risk Level|Evaluation|Owner|
|---|---|---|---|---|--:|--:|--:|---|---|
|Server|Power outage|No UPS|Temporary server unavailability|A|4|2|6|Acceptable|IT Manager|

---

# 21. Very Important ISO 27001 Interview Point

Don't say:

> "ISO 27001 requires Impact × Likelihood."

That is **not correct**.

Instead say:

> **ISO 27001 requires the organization to establish and apply a risk assessment process with defined criteria, but it does not prescribe one specific scoring formula. The organization may use a numerical scale, qualitative scale, risk matrix, multiplication, addition, or another justified methodology, provided it is defined and applied consistently.**

---

# 22. Risk Assessment vs Risk Treatment

Another important distinction:

### Risk Assessment

```text
Identify
   ↓
Analyze
   ↓
Evaluate
```

### Risk Treatment

```text
Select treatment
       ↓
Select controls
       ↓
Prepare SoA
       ↓
Create Risk Treatment Plan
       ↓
Implement controls
```

So:

> **Risk assessment tells us which risks exist and how serious they are.**

> **Risk treatment tells us what we are going to do about those risks.**
