# Statement of Applicability (SoA) – ISO/IEC 27001:2022

The **Statement of Applicability (SoA)** is a **mandatory documented information** required by ISO/IEC 27001. It is prepared as part of the **risk treatment process** and provides a clear overview of which information security controls the organization has selected and why.

## 1. Purpose of the SoA

The SoA describes the organization's **information security control profile**.

It answers four key questions:

1. **Which controls are applicable?**
    
2. **Why are they applicable?**
    
3. **How are they implemented?**
    
4. **Why are any controls excluded?**
    

The SoA also provides evidence that the organization has considered the relevant controls when determining its risk treatment.

---

## 2. What Does the SoA Contain?

The SoA generally contains:

|SoA Information|Explanation|
|---|---|
|**Control**|The selected Annex A control|
|**Applicability**|Whether the control is applicable|
|**Justification**|Why the control is included or excluded|
|**Implementation Status**|Implemented, partially implemented, or planned|
|**Implementation Details**|How the control is implemented|
|**Related Risks**|Risks addressed by the control|
|**Other Requirements**|Customer, legal, regulatory, contractual, or business requirements supporting applicability|

---

## 3. Annex A and the SoA

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A contains **93 controls**.

The organization does **not automatically have to implement all 93 controls**.

Instead, the organization determines which controls are applicable based on:

- Risk assessment
    
- Risk treatment decisions
    
- Legal and regulatory requirements
    
- Contractual requirements
    
- Customer requirements
    
- Business requirements
    
- Interested-party requirements
    
- Management decisions
    

Therefore:

> **Annex A provides the reference controls; the SoA documents the organization's decision about their applicability.**

---

## 4. Applicable Controls

If a control is applicable, the organization should explain **why it is required** and **how it is implemented**.

### Example

**Control:** Information classification

**Applicable:** Yes

**Justification:**  
The organization processes confidential customer and business information, so information must be classified according to its sensitivity.

**Implementation:**  
The organization uses an information classification policy and classification labels such as Public, Internal, Confidential, and Restricted.

**Related Risk:**  
Unauthorized disclosure of confidential information.

---

## 5. Excluded Controls

If a control is considered **not applicable**, the organization must provide a **justification for the exclusion**.

### Example

**Control:** Physical security perimeter

**Applicable:** No

**Justification:**  
The organization operates its production infrastructure entirely through cloud service providers and does not operate its own data center or server room within the ISMS scope.

The important point is:

> **A control should not simply be marked "Not Applicable." The organization should explain why it is not applicable.**

---

## 6. Controls Outside Annex A

The organization can also implement **additional controls that are not listed in Annex A**.

For example:

- A customer-specific security requirement
    
- A specialized fraud detection control
    
- An industry-specific security requirement
    
- An internal security requirement
    

These additional controls can also be included in the SoA.

---

## 7. SoA and Risk Treatment

The relationship can be remembered as:

```text
Risk Assessment
       ↓
Identify & Evaluate Risks
       ↓
Risk Treatment
       ↓
Select Appropriate Controls
       ↓
Statement of Applicability (SoA)
       ↓
Implement Controls
       ↓
Monitor & Improve
```

The SoA therefore acts as a **bridge between risk treatment and control implementation**.

---

## 8. Simple SoA Example

|Control|Applicable?|Justification|Implementation|
|---|---|---|---|
|Information classification|Yes|Required to protect sensitive information|Information Classification Policy|
|Access control|Yes|Required to prevent unauthorized access|RBAC, MFA, access reviews|
|Backup|Yes|Required to maintain availability and recover data|Automated daily backups|
|Physical security|No|No physical data center within scope; infrastructure is cloud-hosted|Not applicable|
|Supplier security|Yes|Critical services are provided by external suppliers|Supplier security assessments|

---

## 9. Why Is the SoA Important?

The SoA is important because it:

- Documents the organization's security control decisions.
    
- Demonstrates that controls were selected systematically.
    
- Links controls to identified risks and requirements.
    
- Provides justification for excluded controls.
    
- Shows the implementation status of controls.
    
- Helps management understand the organization's security posture.
    
- Provides important evidence during the **ISO 27001 certification audit**.
