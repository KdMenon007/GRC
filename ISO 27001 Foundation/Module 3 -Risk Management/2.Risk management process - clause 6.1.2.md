
# ISO/IEC 27001 Risk Management Process

The overall flow can be remembered as:

```text
1. Define Risk Assessment Methodology
                 ↓
2. Perform Risk Assessment
                 ↓
3. Select Risk Treatment Options
                 ↓
4. Create Statement of Applicability (SoA)
                 ↓
5. Create Risk Treatment Plan (RTP)
                 ↓
        Implement Controls
                 ↓
       Monitor & Improve
```

# 1. Define the Risk Assessment Methodology

Before assessing risks, the organization must decide **how risks will be assessed**.

The purpose is to make sure that different risks are assessed **consistently and objectively**.

## What should the methodology define?

It should establish:

- How risks are identified
    
- How risk likelihood is determined
    
- How impact/consequence is determined
    
- How risk levels are calculated
    
- How risks are evaluated
    
- What constitutes an acceptable risk
    
- What constitutes an unacceptable risk
    
- How risk owners are assigned
    
- How risks are reviewed
    

### Example risk matrix

A company could use:

**Risk = Likelihood × Impact**

|Likelihood|Score|
|---|--:|
|Rare|1|
|Unlikely|2|
|Possible|3|
|Likely|4|
|Almost Certain|5|

And:

|Impact|Score|
|---|--:|
|Insignificant|1|
|Minor|2|
|Moderate|3|
|Major|4|
|Critical|5|

Therefore:

> **Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

For example:

**Likelihood = 4**  
**Impact = 5**

Risk score:

**4 × 5 = 20**

The organization might classify 20 as **High/Critical Risk** requiring treatment.

The exact scoring system is chosen by the organization; ISO 27001 does not prescribe one universal numerical formula.

# 2. Define Risk Acceptance Criteria

An organization cannot eliminate every possible risk.

Therefore, it must define:

> **How much risk is the organization willing to accept?**

This is called the organization's **risk acceptance criteria** or, more broadly, its risk appetite/tolerance.

For example:

```text
0–5     → Low       → Accept
6–10    → Medium    → Management review
11–15   → High      → Treatment required
16–25   → Critical  → Immediate treatment
```

These numbers are only an example. Each organization establishes its own criteria.

### Example

Suppose a small consulting company could tolerate losing four hours of non-critical working data because backups are available.

For that company:

> Four hours of data loss may fall within its accepted risk.

But for a telecommunications company processing thousands of transactions every hour:

> Four hours of data loss could have a major financial and operational impact.

Therefore:

**The same event can represent different levels of risk for different organizations.**

This is why risk assessment must be based on the organization's **context, objectives, requirements, and risk criteria**.

---

# 3. Perform the Information Security Risk Assessment

Once the methodology has been established, the organization performs the actual risk assessment.

The objective is to:

1. Identify information-security risks
    
2. Analyze those risks
    
3. Evaluate whether they are acceptable
    

A simple model is:

```text
Asset / Information
        ↓
Threat
        ↓
Vulnerability
        ↓
Potential Event
        ↓
Impact
        ↓
Likelihood
        ↓
Risk Level
```

### Example

**Asset:** Customer database

**Threat:** Cyber attacker

**Vulnerability:** Weak access control

**Potential event:** Unauthorized access

**Impact:** Customer information disclosed

**Likelihood:** Likely

**Risk:** High

The organization then compares the calculated risk against its predefined risk acceptance criteria.

---

# 4. Select Risk Treatment Options

After assessing the risks, the organization decides **what to do with unacceptable risks**.

The common risk treatment options are:

|Option|Meaning|
|---|---|
|**Avoid**|Stop the activity causing the risk|
|**Reduce/Modify**|Implement controls to reduce the risk|
|**Transfer/Share**|Share the risk with another party, e.g. insurance or contractual arrangements|
|**Accept/Retain**|Knowingly retain the risk within accepted criteria|

### Example

Risk:

> Customer database could be compromised because of weak authentication.

Possible treatment:

**Reduce the risk** by implementing:

- MFA
    
- Strong password policies
    
- Privileged access management
    
- Access reviews
    
- Monitoring and alerting
    

The organization then evaluates the **residual risk** after treatment.

---

# 5. Create the Statement of Applicability

The **Statement of Applicability (SoA)** is a key ISO 27001 document.

It connects:

**Risk assessment → Risk treatment → Security controls**

The SoA identifies the controls that are:

- Necessary
    
- Applicable
    
- Implemented or planned
    
- Justified when included or excluded
    

### Example

|Control|Applicable?|Status|Justification|
|---|---|---|---|
|Access control|Yes|Implemented|Required to protect systems|
|MFA|Yes|Planned|Reduces authentication risk|
|Supplier security|Yes|Implemented|Critical suppliers exist|
|Physical security|Yes|Implemented|Data center requires protection|

### Important point

**Annex A contains 93 controls, but that does not mean every organization automatically implements all 93 controls.**

Controls are selected based on the organization's risk treatment and requirements.

The SoA provides the rationale for the organization's decisions.

---

# 6. Create the Risk Treatment Plan

After deciding what needs to be done, the organization creates the **Risk Treatment Plan (RTP)**.

The RTP answers:

> **Who will do what, by when, and with what resources?**

A typical RTP could contain:

|Risk|Treatment|Control/Action|Owner|Deadline|Status|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|Weak authentication|Reduce|Implement MFA|IAM Team|Q2|In progress|
|Ransomware|Reduce|Deploy EDR|SOC|Q2|Planned|
|Data leakage|Reduce|DLP implementation|Security Team|Q3|Planned|
|Supplier risk|Reduce|Supplier assessment|Procurement|Q2|Completed|

The RTP therefore turns the risk-treatment decisions into **actual implementation activities**.

---

# 7. Residual Risk and Risk Acceptance

After controls are implemented, the risk may not disappear completely.

For example:

```text
Initial Risk
    ↓
Implement Controls
    ↓
Residual Risk
    ↓
Evaluate
    ↓
Accept / Further Treatment
```

### Example

Before MFA:

> Risk = High

After implementing MFA and other controls:

> Risk = Medium

The remaining **residual risk** may be acceptable according to the organization's criteria.

If it is still unacceptable, additional treatment may be required.

---

# 8. Why Risk Management Comes First

This is one of the most important concepts for ISO 27001.

An organization should **not start by blindly implementing every security control**.

Instead:

```text
Business Context
       ↓
Security Requirements
       ↓
Risk Assessment
       ↓
Risk Treatment
       ↓
Control Selection
       ↓
SoA
       ↓
Risk Treatment Plan
       ↓
Control Implementation
```

Therefore:

> **Risk determines which security controls are needed.**

This prevents organizations from spending money and resources on controls that provide little value while potentially ignoring more important risks.

# 9. Example from a SOC Perspective

Imagine you are working as a **SOC Analyst**.

Your organization identifies this risk:

> Attackers may compromise privileged accounts and gain unauthorized access to critical servers.

### Risk assessment

- Asset: Critical servers
    
- Threat: Credential theft
    
- Vulnerability: No MFA for privileged accounts
    
- Likelihood: High
    
- Impact: Critical
    
- Overall risk: Critical
    

### Treatment

Reduce the risk.

### Controls/actions

- Implement MFA
    
- Privileged Access Management
    
- Monitor privileged logins
    
- Detect impossible travel
    
- Alert on abnormal administrative activity
    
- Perform periodic access reviews
    

### SoA

Document the applicable controls and their status/justification.

### RTP

Define:

- Control/action
    
- Responsible owner
    
- Implementation deadline
    
- Required resources
    
- Status
    

### SOC monitoring

After implementation, monitor:

- Failed privileged logins
    
- Suspicious authentication
    
- Privilege escalation
    
- Abnormal administrative activity
    

This demonstrates how **ISO 27001 risk management connects directly to SOC operations**.

# 10. The Five Steps – Easy Exam Memory

Remember:

> **M-A-T-S-P**

### **M – Methodology**

Define how risk will be assessed and accepted.

### **A – Assessment**

Identify, analyze, and evaluate risks.

### **T – Treatment**

Decide how unacceptable risks will be treated.

### **S – SoA**

Document applicable controls and their justification.

### **P – Plan**

Create the Risk Treatment Plan to implement the selected treatments/controls.

```text
M → A → T → S → P
│    │    │    │    │
│    │    │    │    └── Risk Treatment Plan
│    │    │    └─────── Statement of Applicability
│    │    └──────────── Treatment decision
│    └───────────────── Risk Assessment
└────────────────────── Methodology
```
