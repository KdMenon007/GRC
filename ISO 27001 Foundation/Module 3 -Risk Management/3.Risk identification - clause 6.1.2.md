
## 1. What is Risk Identification?

Risk identification is the first part of the **information security risk assessment**.

The objective is to identify:

- **What needs to be protected?** → Asset
    
- **What could go wrong?** → Threat
    
- **Why could it happen?** → Vulnerability
    
- **What would be the consequence?** → Risk
    
- **Who is responsible for managing it?** → Risk Owner
    

![[Pasted image 20260808105638.png]]

### Simple flow

```text
Asset
  ↓
Threat
  ↓
Vulnerability
  ↓
Risk
  ↓
Risk Owner
```

For example:

```text
Server
  ↓
Electricity outage
  ↓
No UPS
  ↓
Server becomes unavailable
  ↓
IT Infrastructure Manager
```

# 2. Asset Identification

An **asset** is something valuable that needs protection.

Assets can include:

### Hardware

- Servers
    
- Laptops
    
- Routers
    
- Switches
    
- Firewalls
    
- Storage devices
    
- Data-center equipment
    

### Software

- Operating systems
    
- Applications
    
- Databases
    
- Security tools
    
- Business applications
    

### Information

- Customer information
    
- Employee information
    
- Financial records
    
- Intellectual property
    
- Business documents
    
- Security logs
    

Information may exist in:

- Electronic form
    
- Paper form
    

### Infrastructure

- Data centers
    
- Offices
    
- Network infrastructure
    
- Power systems
    
- Communication infrastructure
    

### People

- Employees
    
- Administrators
    
- Developers
    
- Security analysts
    
- Key personnel
    

### Example

|Asset|Why valuable?|
|---|---|
|Customer database|Contains sensitive customer information|
|Domain Controller|Controls authentication and access|
|Production server|Supports critical business applications|
|Firewall|Protects the network|
|Employee laptop|Contains business information|

---

# 3. Threat Identification

A **threat** is something that can potentially cause harm to an asset.

Examples:

|Asset|Possible Threat|
|---|---|
|Server|Electricity outage|
|Server|Fire|
|Database|Malware|
|Laptop|Theft|
|Network|DDoS|
|Employee|Social engineering|
|Application|Cyberattack|
|Data center|Flood|
|Key employee|Employee departure|

A threat does **not necessarily mean that an incident has already happened**.

It represents something that **could happen** and cause damage.

---

# 4. Vulnerability Identification

A **vulnerability** is a weakness that can allow a threat to cause harm.

For example:

### Threat:

Electricity outage

### Vulnerability:

No UPS

### Result:

Server becomes unavailable.

Another example:

### Threat:

Virus/malware

### Vulnerability:

No endpoint protection

### Result:

Malware infects the system.

# 5. Creating the Risk

Once the asset, threat, and vulnerability are identified, we can describe the **risk**.

A useful way to formulate it is:

> **Risk = Threat exploiting a vulnerability and causing an unwanted consequence to an asset.**

### Example 1 — Server Availability

**Asset:** Server

**Threat:** Electricity outage

**Vulnerability:** No UPS

**Risk:** Temporary unavailability of the server due to an electricity outage.

---

### Example 2 — Customer Database

**Asset:** Customer database

**Threat:** Unauthorized access

**Vulnerability:** Excessive user privileges

**Risk:** Unauthorized disclosure of customer information.

---

### Example 3 — Employee Laptop

**Asset:** Employee laptop

**Threat:** Theft

**Vulnerability:** Full-disk encryption is not enabled

**Risk:** Confidential business information may be exposed if the laptop is stolen.

---

# 6. Risk Owner

Every identified risk should have a **risk owner**.

The risk owner is the person who has the authority and responsibility to manage that particular risk.

For example:

|Risk|Risk Owner|
|---|---|
|Server unavailable due to power failure|IT Infrastructure Manager|
|Customer data exposure|Data Owner / Business Owner|
|Vulnerable web application|Application Owner|
|Employee phishing risk|HR / Security Management|
|Data-center fire|Facilities Manager|

### Important distinction

The **risk owner does not necessarily implement the control personally**.

For example:

```text
Risk Owner
    ↓
IT Manager

Treatment:
Install UPS

Implementation:
Infrastructure Team
```

The IT Manager may own the risk, while the infrastructure team implements the treatment.

# 8. Risk Identification vs Risk Analysis vs Risk Evaluation

This distinction is very important for ISO 27001 interviews.

ISO/IEC 27001 risk assessment consists of:

```text
Risk Assessment
       |
       +------------------+
       |                  |
Risk Identification   Risk Analysis
                           |
                           ↓
                    Risk Evaluation
```

More practically:

### Step 1 — Risk Identification

Ask:

> **What risks exist?**

Identify:

- Assets
    
- Threats
    
- Vulnerabilities
    
- Consequences
    
- Risk owners
    

---

### Step 2 — Risk Analysis

Ask:

> **How significant is the risk?**

Usually consider:

- Likelihood
    
- Impact
    

For example:

```text
Likelihood = 4/5
Impact     = 5/5

Risk Score = 20
```

The exact calculation method depends on the organization's documented risk assessment methodology.

---

### Step 3 — Risk Evaluation

Ask:

> **Is this risk acceptable?**

Compare the calculated risk against the organization's **risk acceptance criteria**.

For example:

```text
Risk Score: 20
Risk Acceptance Threshold: 10

20 > 10
      ↓
Risk is unacceptable
      ↓
Risk treatment required
```

# 9. Is Asset-Based Risk Assessment Mandatory?

This is an important point from your material.

An **asset-based approach** is a common and practical method for identifying information security risks, and it aligns well with guidance such as ISO/IEC 27005.

However, **ISO/IEC 27001:2022 does not mandate a specific asset-based risk assessment methodology**.

The organization must define and apply a risk assessment process that produces **consistent, valid, comparable results**, but it has flexibility in how it identifies and analyzes risks.

So for an interview:

> **Asset-based risk assessment is a commonly used approach, but ISO/IEC 27001:2022 does not require organizations to use only this methodology.**

---

# 10. Why Risk Identification Is Important

Risk identification is the foundation of the entire ISO 27001 risk management process.

If you fail to identify a risk:

```text
Risk not identified
       ↓
Risk not analyzed
       ↓
Risk not evaluated
       ↓
No treatment selected
       ↓
No appropriate control
       ↓
Security exposure remains
```

Therefore:

> **You cannot properly treat a risk that you haven't identified.**

---

# 11. Connection to ISO 27001 Implementation

Risk identification eventually leads to control selection.

The overall process looks like:

```text
Understand Organization
        ↓
Identify Risks
        ↓
Analyze Risks
        ↓
Evaluate Risks
        ↓
Identify Unacceptable Risks
        ↓
Select Risk Treatment
        ↓
Select Appropriate Controls
        ↓
Statement of Applicability (SoA)
        ↓
Risk Treatment Plan
        ↓
Implement Controls
        ↓
Monitor Effectiveness
        ↓
Continual Improvement
```

This is one of the most important flows to remember for an **ISO 27001/SOC interview**.

**A → T → V → R → O**

> **Asset → Threat → Vulnerability → Risk → Owner**

And then:

> **Identify → Analyze → Evaluate → Treat → Monitor**
