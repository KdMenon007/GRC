
## 1. Where Risk Treatment Fits

The complete risk management flow is:

```text
Understand Context
       ↓
Define Risk Assessment Methodology
       ↓
Identify Risks
       ↓
Analyze Risks
       ↓
Evaluate Risks
       ↓
Identify Unacceptable Risks
       ↓
Select Risk Treatment Options
       ↓
Select Controls
       ↓
Statement of Applicability (SoA)
       ↓
Risk Treatment Plan
       ↓
Implement Controls
       ↓
Monitor Effectiveness
```

So the important sequence is:

> **Risk Assessment tells us what risks we have.**  
> **Risk Treatment tells us what we will do about those risks.**

---

# 2. What Is Risk Treatment?

**Risk treatment** is the process of selecting and implementing appropriate measures for dealing with identified risks.

After risk evaluation, the organization knows which risks are:

- Acceptable
    
- Unacceptable
    

The unacceptable risks require a treatment decision.

For example:

```text
Risk Level = 15
       ↓
Risk Acceptance Level = 10
       ↓
15 > 10
       ↓
Unacceptable
       ↓
Risk Treatment Required
```

---

# 3. Why Is Risk Treatment Important?

Risk treatment is one of the most important parts of an ISO 27001 implementation because this is where the organization decides:

- Which security measures to implement
    
- How much money to spend
    
- Which risks to reduce
    
- Which risks to accept
    
- Which activities to stop
    
- Which risks can be shared with third parties
    

Therefore, risk treatment must make sense from **both security and business perspectives**.

The goal is not:

> **"Eliminate every risk."**

The goal is:

> **Reduce information security risks to an acceptable level in a way that is appropriate for the organization.**

---

# 4. Four Common Risk Treatment Options

The four common options are:

```text
                Risk Treatment
                     |
       +-------------+-------------+
       |             |             |
    Reduce         Accept        Avoid
       |
    Transfer
```

Or remember:

> **Reduce – Accept – Avoid – Transfer**

# 5. Option 1 — Reduce the Risk

This is often called **risk modification/reduction**.

The organization implements controls to reduce:

- Likelihood
    
- Impact
    
- Or both
    

### Example: Server Power Failure

### Risk

```text
Asset:
Production server

Threat:
Electricity outage

Vulnerability:
No backup power

Risk:
Server becomes unavailable
```

### Treatment

Implement:

- UPS
    
- Power generator
    
- Redundant power supply
    
- Power monitoring
    

Result:

```text
Before:
Electricity outage
       ↓
Server unavailable
       ↓
High risk

After:
Electricity outage
       ↓
UPS / Generator
       ↓
Server continues operating
       ↓
Lower risk
```

This is an example of **risk reduction**.

---

# 6. Risk Reduction Does Not Necessarily Mean Zero Risk

This is important.

Suppose:

```text
Initial Risk = 16
```

After implementing controls:

```text
Residual Risk = 6
```

If the organization's acceptable level is:

```text
Acceptable ≤ 10
```

then:

```text
Residual Risk = 6
        ↓
Acceptable
```

The organization does not necessarily need to eliminate the remaining risk.

The remaining risk is called:

> **Residual Risk**

---

# 7. Option 2 — Accept the Risk

Sometimes the organization decides that the risk is acceptable.

This can happen when:

- The risk is already within the acceptance criteria.
    
- Treatment costs more than the potential damage.
    
- Further reduction is not practical.
    
- The business decides the remaining risk is acceptable.
    

### Example

Suppose:

```text
Potential loss = ₹50,000
```

But implementing a sophisticated control would cost:

```text
Control cost = ₹5,00,000
```

The organization may determine that reducing the risk further is not economically justified.

It may therefore decide to **accept the risk**, subject to its risk acceptance rules and appropriate authorization.

### Important

Risk acceptance should **not** simply mean:

> "We don't want to spend money."

It should be a **deliberate business decision based on defined risk acceptance criteria**.

---

# 8. Option 3 — Avoid the Risk

Risk avoidance means:

> **Stop or change the activity that creates the risk.**

Instead of trying to control the risk, the organization eliminates the source of the risk.

### Example: Remote Working

Suppose an organization identifies several risks associated with remote access:

- Unauthorized access
    
- Insecure networks
    
- Endpoint compromise
    
- Data leakage
    

The organization could implement controls such as:

- VPN
    
- MFA
    
- Endpoint security
    
- Device management
    

But suppose management determines that the business activity itself is not sufficiently valuable to justify the risk and cost.

The organization could decide:

> Stop remote access/teleworking.

The activity generating the risk is removed.

Therefore:

```text
Remote Working
      ↓
Associated Risks
      ↓
Stop Remote Working
      ↓
Risk Avoided
```

### Important

Avoidance does not always mean "do nothing."

It means **changing or stopping the activity so that the risk no longer exists or is significantly changed**.

---

# 9. Option 4 — Transfer the Risk

Risk transfer means sharing the financial or operational consequences of a risk with another party.

Examples include:

- Insurance
    
- Outsourcing
    
- Contracts
    
- Third-party services
    

### Example — Fire

Suppose a company faces the risk of:

> Fire destroying its office and equipment.

The organization might purchase property/business insurance.

If a fire occurs:

```text
Fire
 ↓
Property destroyed
 ↓
Insurance
 ↓
Financial compensation
```

The financial consequences may be partly transferred.

---

# 10. Very Important: You Cannot Completely Transfer the Risk

This is an important interview point.

Suppose the company has insurance.

A fire occurs.

The insurance company may compensate the organization financially.

But:

- The building may still be destroyed.
    
- Equipment may still be damaged.
    
- Information may still be lost.
    
- Employees may still be unable to work.
    
- Business operations may still stop.
    
- Customers may still be affected.
    
- Reputation may still suffer.
    

Therefore:

> **Insurance transfers or shares some consequences, particularly financial consequences; it does not make the underlying risk disappear.**

---

# 11. Comparing the Four Options

|Treatment|What happens?|Example|
|---|---|---|
|**Reduce**|Implement controls|UPS for server|
|**Accept**|Keep the risk|Accept low-level risk|
|**Avoid**|Stop/change risky activity|Stop remote access|
|**Transfer**|Share consequences with another party|Insurance|

### Easy memory

**R-A-A-T**

> **Reduce → Accept → Avoid → Transfer**

# 12. Selecting Controls

If the organization chooses to **reduce a risk**, it needs to select appropriate controls.

This is where **Annex A** becomes important.

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A contains:

> **93 reference information security controls**

The organization considers these controls when deciding how to treat its risks.

---

# 13. Example — Risk to Control

Suppose the organization identifies:

```text
Risk:
Unauthorized access to customer database
```

Possible treatment:

> Reduce the risk.

Possible controls could include:

- Access control
    
- Identity management
    
- Authentication
    
- Privileged access management
    
- Logging and monitoring
    
- Access reviews
    

The organization selects controls that are appropriate for the **specific risk**.

The objective is not to implement controls simply because they appear in Annex A.

Instead:

```text
Risk
 ↓
Treatment Decision
 ↓
Control Selection
 ↓
Risk Reduction
```

---

# 14. Are Annex A Controls Mandatory?

This is one of the most important points.

**No.**

The 93 Annex A controls are a **reference set of controls**.

The organization selects controls based on its:

- Risk assessment
    
- Risk treatment decisions
    
- Business requirements
    
- Legal requirements
    
- Contractual requirements
    
- Security requirements
    

---

# 15. Can We Use Controls Outside Annex A?

**Yes.**

An organization is not restricted to only the 93 Annex A controls.

It may implement:

- Additional technical controls
    
- Organizational controls
    
- Industry-specific controls
    
- Customer-required controls
    
- Regulatory controls
    
- Controls from other frameworks or standards
    
- Internally developed controls
    

For example, an organization could use a specific security technology or procedure that isn't directly listed as an Annex A control.

The important thing is that the control supports the organization's risk treatment.

---

# 16. Statement of Applicability — SoA

After determining the controls needed for risk treatment, the organization prepares the:

> **Statement of Applicability (SoA)**

The SoA is a key ISO 27001 document.

It documents which Annex A controls are:

- Applicable
    
- Not applicable
    
- Implemented
    
- Planned
    
- Justified for exclusion where appropriate
    

### Simplified example

|Control|Applicable?|Status|Reason|
|---|---|---|---|
|Access control|Yes|Implemented|Required to protect systems|
|Supplier security|Yes|Planned|Third-party services used|
|Physical security|Yes|Implemented|Data center requires protection|
|Specific control|No|Not applicable|Relevant condition does not exist|

---

# 17. Risk Treatment Plan

After selecting the treatment and controls, the organization needs to plan **how those controls will actually be implemented**.

This is where the:

> **Risk Treatment Plan (RTP)**

becomes important.

A treatment plan can contain:

|Risk|Treatment|Control|Owner|Deadline|Status|
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
|Server outage|Reduce|UPS|IT Manager|30 Sept|In progress|
|Phishing|Reduce|Awareness training|CISO|15 Oct|Planned|
|Data leakage|Reduce|DLP|Security Manager|30 Nov|Planned|

# 18. Risk Treatment Example — End-to-End

Let's use the server example.

### Risk Identification

```text
Asset:
Production Server

Threat:
Electricity outage

Vulnerability:
No UPS

Risk:
Temporary server unavailability
```

### Risk Analysis

```text
Impact = 4
Likelihood = 3
Risk Level = 12
```

### Risk Evaluation

Suppose:

```text
Risk Acceptance Level = 6
```

Therefore:

```text
12 > 6
↓
Unacceptable
```

### Risk Treatment

Management chooses:

> **Reduce the risk**

### Controls

Implement:

- UPS
    
- Generator
    
- Power monitoring
    

### SoA

Document applicable controls.

### Risk Treatment Plan

```text
Owner: IT Infrastructure Manager
Deadline: 30 September
Budget: Approved
Status: Planned
```

### After Implementation

Suppose the residual risk becomes:

```text
Residual Risk = 5
```

Since:

```text
5 ≤ 6
```

the residual risk is acceptable according to the organization's criteria.

---

# 19. Initial Risk vs Residual Risk

This is another important concept.

### Initial/Inherent Risk

Risk **before** applying additional treatment controls.

```text
Risk = 12
```

### Treatment

Implement:

- UPS
    
- Generator
    
- Power monitoring
    

### Residual Risk

Risk remaining **after treatment**.

```text
Residual Risk = 5
```

So:

```text
Initial Risk
     ↓
Risk Treatment
     ↓
Controls
     ↓
Residual Risk
     ↓
Risk Acceptance Decision
```

# 20. Risk Treatment Documentation

ISO/IEC 27001 requires documented information relating to the **information security risk treatment process/results**.

In practice, organizations can use:

- Risk register
    
- Risk treatment sheets
    
- Risk management software
    
- Risk treatment plan
    
- SoA
    
- Project plans
    
- Control implementation records
    

The exact format is not prescribed.

The important thing is that the organization can demonstrate:

> **What risk was identified → what treatment was selected → which controls were selected → who is responsible → how treatment is implemented → what residual risk remains.**

---

# 21. Important Difference: SoA vs Risk Treatment Plan

These are frequently confused.

### Statement of Applicability

Answers:

> **Which controls are applicable and why?**

### Risk Treatment Plan

Answers:

> **How will we implement the selected risk treatments and controls?**

Simple memory:

```text
SoA
 ↓
WHAT controls apply?

RTP
 ↓
HOW will we implement them?
```
