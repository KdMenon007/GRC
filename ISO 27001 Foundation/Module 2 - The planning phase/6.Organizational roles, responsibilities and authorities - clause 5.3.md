
## 1. Overview

An effective **Information Security Management System (ISMS)** requires clearly defined:

- Roles
    
- Responsibilities
    
- Authorities
    
- Competencies
    
- Resources
    
- Reporting relationships
    

ISO/IEC 27001:2022 requires the organization to determine and assign the roles and responsibilities necessary for the **establishment, implementation, maintenance, and continual improvement** of the ISMS.

The organization must also ensure that the people performing ISMS-related activities have the **necessary competence, education, training, or experience**.

### Simple concept

> **The right people must have clearly defined responsibilities, sufficient authority, adequate resources, and the required competence to perform their information security responsibilities effectively.**

---

# 2. ISO 27001 Requirements

The main requirements are associated with:

- **Clause 5.3 – Organizational roles, responsibilities and authorities**
    
- **Clause 7.1 – Resources**
    
- **Clause 7.2 – Competence**
    

### Clause 5.3

The organization must ensure that responsibilities and authorities for relevant information security roles are:

- Defined
    
- Assigned
    
- Communicated
    
- Understood
    

### Clause 7.1

The organization must determine and provide the resources needed for establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving the ISMS.

### Clause 7.2

The organization must ensure that people performing work affecting information security are competent based on:

- Education
    
- Training
    
- Skills
    
- Experience
    

Evidence of competence should be retained where appropriate.

---

# 3. Why Roles and Responsibilities Are Important

Without clearly defined responsibilities, organizations may face:

- Unclear ownership of risks
    
- Delayed security decisions
    
- Duplication of work
    
- Missed security activities
    
- Lack of accountability
    
- Conflicts between departments
    
- Poor incident response
    
- Ineffective implementation of controls
    

For example, if nobody is explicitly responsible for reviewing privileged access, the activity may be overlooked.

Therefore:

> **Every important ISMS activity should have a clearly identified owner or responsible role.**

---

# 4. Typical ISMS Roles

ISO/IEC 27001 provides a framework but does **not prescribe one fixed organizational structure**.

The exact roles depend on:

- Organization size
    
- Business model
    
- ISMS scope
    
- Industry
    
- Regulatory requirements
    
- Complexity of IT environment
    

Typical roles include:

|Role|Main Responsibility|
|---|---|
|**Top Management**|Overall accountability, direction and resources|
|**CISO / ISB**|Information security management and ISMS coordination|
|**ISMS Manager**|Day-to-day ISMS operation|
|**Risk Owner**|Owns and manages identified risks|
|**Asset Owner**|Responsible for protection of assigned assets|
|**DPO**|Data protection and privacy responsibilities|
|**Internal Auditor**|Independently evaluates ISMS conformity and effectiveness|
|**Employees**|Follow security policies and procedures|
|**IT/Security Teams**|Implement and operate technical security measures|

---

# 5. Top Management

Top management has overall responsibility for ensuring that information security is properly governed.

Key responsibilities include:

- Providing strategic direction
    
- Approving the Information Security Policy
    
- Establishing information security objectives
    
- Providing necessary resources
    
- Assigning responsibilities and authorities
    
- Supporting the ISMS
    
- Reviewing ISMS performance
    
- Promoting information security throughout the organization
    
- Supporting continual improvement
    

### Example

Management provides budget for:

- SOC operations
    
- Security awareness
    
- Vulnerability management
    
- Security tools
    
- Employee training
    

This demonstrates management commitment to the ISMS.

---

# 6. CISO / Information Security Officer

The **Chief Information Security Officer (CISO)** or **Information Security Officer (ISB)** is commonly responsible for coordinating information security activities.

Typical responsibilities include:

- Maintaining the ISMS
    
- Coordinating risk management
    
- Supporting security strategy
    
- Monitoring security performance
    
- Coordinating security policies
    
- Advising management
    
- Coordinating security incidents
    
- Supporting audits
    
- Reporting security risks to management
    
- Driving continual improvement
    

The CISO should be capable of translating between:

> **Business requirements ↔ Information security risks ↔ Security measures**

This is an important practical competency.

For example:

**Business requirement:**  
Online banking must remain available.

**Security risk:**  
DDoS attack could disrupt banking services.

**Security measure:**  
DDoS protection, redundancy, monitoring and incident response.

---

# 7. Risk Owner

A **risk owner** is the person responsible for managing a particular information security risk.

Responsibilities may include:

- Understanding the risk
    
- Evaluating treatment options
    
- Approving risk treatment
    
- Accepting residual risk where appropriate
    
- Monitoring the risk
    
- Ensuring appropriate actions are completed
    

### Example

Risk:

> Unauthorized access to customer database.

Risk owner:

> Head of Customer Data / Business Application Owner.

Treatment:

> Implement stronger access controls, MFA and periodic access reviews.

---

# 8. Asset Owner

An asset owner is responsible for an organization's particular information asset.

Assets may include:

- Databases
    
- Applications
    
- Servers
    
- Business processes
    
- Customer information
    
- Intellectual property
    

Responsibilities can include:

- Defining protection requirements
    
- Classifying information
    
- Ensuring appropriate access
    
- Supporting risk assessments
    
- Reviewing access rights
    
- Ensuring appropriate security controls
    

---

# 9. Data Protection Officer (DPO)

The **DPO** is responsible for applicable data protection and privacy obligations.

The DPO role is different from the CISO role.

### CISO focus

Primarily concerned with:

> Information security and protection of information systems.

### DPO focus

Primarily concerned with:

> Privacy and protection of personal data.

There can be situations where the responsibilities of the CISO and DPO overlap.

---

# 10. CISO and DPO – Potential Conflict of Interest

An organization may sometimes assign both CISO and DPO responsibilities to the same person.

This can provide certain advantages, such as:

- Better information flow
    
- Better understanding of security and privacy
    
- More coordinated controls
    
- Reduced organizational complexity
    

However, potential conflicts of interest must be carefully assessed.

### Example: Security Monitoring

The CISO may want:

> Extensive collection and analysis of logs and network traffic to detect threats.

The DPO may need to consider:

> Privacy requirements, lawful processing, purpose limitation and protection of personal data.

Therefore, the organization must determine whether the combined roles can be performed without creating unacceptable conflicts of interest.

### Important principle

> **Combining CISO and DPO roles is not automatically prohibited, but potential conflicts of interest must be carefully assessed and managed.**

The organization should also verify that the individual has sufficient:

- Time
    
- Competence
    
- Independence where required
    
- Authority
    
- Resources
    

to perform both functions effectively.

---

# 11. Segregation of Duties (SoD)

**Segregation of Duties (SoD)** means separating conflicting responsibilities between different people or roles.

The objective is to prevent:

- Fraud
    
- Abuse of privileges
    
- Unauthorized changes
    
- Conflicts of interest
    
- Lack of independent oversight
    

### Example

A single employee should ideally not:

1. Request privileged access
    
2. Approve the access
    
3. Implement the access
    
4. Review their own access
    

Instead, responsibilities can be separated.

---

# 12. RACI Model

Organizations can use a **RACI matrix** to clearly define responsibilities.

|RACI|Meaning|
|---|---|
|**R – Responsible**|Performs the activity|
|**A – Accountable**|Ultimately accountable for the outcome|
|**C – Consulted**|Provides expertise/input|
|**I – Informed**|Kept informed|

### Example – Vulnerability Management

|Role|RACI|
|---|---|
|Security Team|**R – Responsible**|
|CISO|**A – Accountable**|
|Application Owner|**C – Consulted**|
|Management|**I – Informed**|

RACI helps avoid ambiguity about who performs, approves, advises on, or receives information about an activity.

# 13. Competence Requirements

ISO 27001 requires organizations to ensure that people performing work affecting information security are competent.

Competence may come from:

- Formal education
    
- Professional certifications
    
- Technical training
    
- Practical experience
    
- Internal training
    
- On-the-job experience
    

### Examples

A SOC Analyst may require knowledge of:

- SIEM
    
- Incident response
    
- Network security
    
- Windows/Linux security
    
- Threat detection
    
- MITRE ATT&CK
    

A CISO may additionally require:

- Leadership
    
- Risk management
    
- Communication
    
- Governance
    
- Business understanding
    
- Regulatory knowledge
    

---

# 14. Evidence of Competence

Organizations should be able to demonstrate that personnel possess the required competence.

Evidence can include:

- Training certificates
    
- Professional certifications
    
- Training records
    
- Personnel competency records
    
- Experience records
    
- Skills assessments
    
- Performance evaluations
    
- Training history
    

### Example

For a SOC analyst:

> Security training completed → certificate retained → competency recorded in training history.

This provides evidence during an audit.

# 15. Important Competencies for a CISO / ISB

The source particularly emphasizes that technical knowledge alone is not sufficient.

A successful CISO should demonstrate:

### Technical competence

- Information security
    
- Risk management
    
- Security controls
    
- Security architecture
    

### Leadership

- Ability to lead teams
    
- Decision-making
    
- Strategic thinking
    

### Communication

- Communicating with management
    
- Explaining technical risks in business terms
    
- Communicating security requirements to employees
    

### Integrity

The CISO frequently deals with sensitive information and difficult security decisions.

### Conflict management

Security controls can sometimes conflict with:

- Business convenience
    
- Operational requirements
    
- Budget constraints
    
- Privacy requirements
    

The CISO must be able to resolve these conflicts appropriately.

---

# 16. Business and Security Translation

One of the most important competencies of the CISO/ISB is the ability to distinguish between:

**Business objectives and processes**

and

**Information security risks and controls.**

The CISO should translate technical security issues into business impact.

### Example

Instead of telling management:

> "The organization has an outdated VPN with a critical vulnerability."

The CISO should explain:

> "The vulnerable VPN could allow unauthorized access to the corporate network, potentially affecting customer information and business operations. Immediate remediation is recommended."

This makes the security risk understandable to decision-makers.

---

# 17. CISO Position in the Organization

The CISO/ISB should have sufficient:

- Authority
    
- Independence
    
- Visibility
    
- Access to management
    
- Resources
    

The role should have a status that allows the CISO to effectively communicate security risks and influence business decisions.

The CISO should not merely function as a technical administrator.

The role should connect:

```text
Business Strategy
       ↓
Information Security Strategy
       ↓
Risk Management
       ↓
Security Controls
       ↓
Security Operations
       ↓
Measurement & Improvement
```

# 21. Cooperation Between Security and Other Functions

The ISMS does not operate independently.

The security function may need to work closely with:

- HR
    
- Legal
    
- Privacy/DPO
    
- IT
    
- Risk Management
    
- Compliance
    
- Internal Audit
    
- Procurement
    
- Facilities
    
- Business Units
    

The organization should define how these functions cooperate.

### Example

For employee onboarding:

```text
HR
 ↓
Employee information
 ↓
IT / IAM
 ↓
Account creation
 ↓
Security
 ↓
Access controls
 ↓
Manager
 ↓
Access approval
```

Clear responsibilities reduce the risk of unauthorized access.

# 22. Practical Example – Incident Management

Consider a ransomware incident.

|Role|Responsibility|
|---|---|
|SOC Analyst|Detect and investigate alert|
|Incident Response Team|Contain and investigate incident|
|CISO|Coordinate security response|
|IT Team|Isolate/recover systems|
|Risk Owner|Assess business risk|
|Legal|Assess legal obligations|
|DPO|Assess personal-data impact|
|Top Management|Make strategic decisions|
|Communications|Manage external communication|

This demonstrates why clearly defined responsibilities are essential.

# 23. Key Success Factors

For an effective ISMS, organizations should:

1. Clearly define ISMS roles.
    
2. Assign responsibilities and authorities.
    
3. Communicate responsibilities to relevant personnel.
    
4. Provide adequate resources.
    
5. Select appropriately qualified personnel.
    
6. Maintain evidence of competence.
    
7. Define reporting relationships.
    
8. Use RACI where useful.
    
9. Apply segregation of duties where necessary.
    
10. Identify and manage conflicts of interest.
    
11. Ensure the CISO/ISB has sufficient authority.
    
12. Establish effective cooperation between security, privacy, risk, HR, IT and other functions.
    
13. Regularly review whether roles and competencies remain appropriate.

# 24. ISO 27001 Audit Perspective

An auditor may ask:

### Question 1

**Who is responsible for the ISMS?**

You should be able to demonstrate the assigned responsibilities and authorities.

### Question 2

**Who owns information security risks?**

You should have documented risk ownership.

### Question 3

**How do you demonstrate employee competence?**

Provide:

- Training records
    
- Certifications
    
- Competency assessments
    
- Experience records
    

### Question 4

**How are responsibilities communicated?**

Examples:

- Job descriptions
    
- RACI matrices
    
- Policies
    
- Appointment letters
    
- Organizational charts
    

### Question 5

**How do you prevent conflicts of interest?**

Show:

- Segregation of duties
    
- Defined authorities
    
- Independent review
    
- Conflict-of-interest assessments

# 25. Key Takeaways

> **Clause 5.3 → Define and assign roles, responsibilities and authorities.**

> **Clause 7.1 → Provide the resources needed for the ISMS.**

> **Clause 7.2 → Ensure people are competent and retain appropriate evidence.**

The most important principle is:

```text
Right People
     ↓
Clear Roles
     ↓
Defined Responsibilities
     ↓
Adequate Authority
     ↓
Required Competence
     ↓
Sufficient Resources
     ↓
Effective ISMS
```

### Interview Answer

If asked **"Why are roles, responsibilities and competencies important in ISO 27001?"**, a strong answer is:

> **"ISO 27001 requires an organization to clearly define and communicate the roles, responsibilities and authorities needed for an effective ISMS. The organization must also provide adequate resources and ensure that personnel have the required competence through education, training or experience. This establishes accountability, prevents gaps and conflicts in security responsibilities, and ensures that the ISMS can be effectively implemented, maintained and continually improved."**
