
## 1. Overview

The **Information Security Policy (IS Policy)** is a high-level document that expresses the organization's strategic commitment to information security and the implementation, operation, and continual improvement of its **Information Security Management System (ISMS)**.

It is established by **top management** and provides the overall direction for information security within the defined ISMS scope.

In simple terms:

> **The Information Security Policy tells the organization why information security is important, what the organization is committed to achieving, and the overall direction that must be followed.**

The policy establishes the foundation for more detailed security policies, procedures, standards, guidelines, and work instructions.

---

# 2. Purpose of the IS Policy

The primary purposes of the Information Security Policy are to:

- Demonstrate management's commitment to information security.
    
- Establish the strategic direction for the ISMS.
    
- Define the organization's overall information security principles.
    
- Support the organization's business objectives.
    
- Commit the organization to fulfilling applicable information security requirements.
    
- Commit to continual improvement of the ISMS.
    
- Communicate the importance of information security to employees.
    
- Establish a foundation for more detailed security requirements.
    
- Clarify high-level roles and responsibilities.
    
- Provide a consistent basis for information security decision-making.
    

The policy therefore acts as an **anchor document** for the organization's information security framework.

---

# 3. Management Commitment

The Information Security Policy must be established by the **top management responsible for the organization or the defined ISMS scope**.

Top management should demonstrate that information security is a business responsibility rather than merely an IT responsibility.

Management commitment should include:

- Approving the Information Security Policy.
    
- Providing necessary resources.
    
- Supporting ISMS implementation.
    
- Establishing information security objectives.
    
- Ensuring information security requirements are integrated into business processes.
    
- Communicating the importance of information security.
    
- Supporting risk management.
    
- Promoting security awareness.
    
- Supporting continual improvement.
    

This is closely connected to **Clause 5 – Leadership**.

# 5. Commitment to Continual Improvement

The IS Policy should include a commitment to **continually improve the ISMS**.

This means the organization should not treat ISO 27001 implementation as a one-time project.

Instead:

```text
Implement ISMS
      ↓
Operate ISMS
      ↓
Monitor Performance
      ↓
Identify Weaknesses
      ↓
Correct / Improve
      ↓
Review
      ↓
Improve ISMS
```

Improvement can result from:

- Internal audits
    
- Security incidents
    
- Risk assessments
    
- Management reviews
    
- Changes in business requirements
    
- New threats
    
- Changes in technology
    
- Regulatory changes
    
- Lessons learned
    

---

# 6. Suitability for the Organization

The Information Security Policy must be **appropriate to the organization's purpose and context**.

A policy should not simply be copied from another organization.

For example, the information security needs of:

- A bank
    
- A software company
    
- A hospital
    
- A manufacturing company
    
- A government organization
    

can be very different.

Therefore, the policy should reflect:

- Business objectives
    
- Business processes
    
- Risk profile
    
- Information assets
    
- Regulatory environment
    
- Industry requirements
    
- Organizational structure
    
- Technology environment
    

---

# 7. IS Policy and Business Objectives

The Information Security Policy should be aligned with the organization's business objectives.

### Example

### Business Objective

> Provide reliable digital banking services.

### Information Security Direction

> Protect customer information and maintain the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of critical banking services.

### Supporting activities

- Access control
    
- Encryption
    
- Security monitoring
    
- Incident response
    
- Business continuity
    
- Vulnerability management
    

This ensures that information security supports the business instead of becoming an isolated activity.

# 8. IS Policy and Information Security Objectives

The policy provides the **high-level direction**, while information security objectives define the specific results the organization wants to achieve.

The relationship is:

```text
Business Objectives
        ↓
Information Security Policy
        ↓
Information Security Objectives
        ↓
Risk Management
        ↓
Security Controls
        ↓
Procedures / Work Instructions
```

### Example

**Policy statement:**

> The organization is committed to protecting information assets against security threats.

**IS objective:**

> Reduce critical vulnerabilities by 80% within six months.

**Implementation measures:**

- Vulnerability scanning
    
- Patch management
    
- Risk prioritization
    
- Remediation verification

# 10. IS Policy Should Not Become a Security Manual

One important practical point is that the IS Policy should not contain excessive implementation details.

### IS Policy

Should explain:

> **What the organization is committed to and the overall direction.**

### Detailed Security Policy / Standard

Explains:

> **Specific requirements for a particular security topic.**

### Procedure

Explains:

> **How a process is performed.**

### Work Instruction

Explains:

> **Detailed steps an employee must follow.**

For example:

```text
Information Security Policy
        ↓
Access Control Policy
        ↓
Access Control Standard
        ↓
User Provisioning Procedure
        ↓
Detailed Work Instruction
```

This creates a **chain of requirements**.

---

# 11. Example: Password Requirements

### IS Policy

> The organization shall protect information through appropriate access control mechanisms.

### Access Control Policy

> Access to information systems shall be restricted based on business need and authorization.

### Password Standard

> Passwords must meet defined complexity and authentication requirements.

### Procedure

> User accounts must be created, modified, and disabled through the approved identity management process.

The IS Policy does not need to contain every technical password requirement.

# 12. IS Policy as an "Anchor" Document

The implementation guide describes the IS Policy as potentially serving as a powerful **anchor or starting point** for information security.

This means the policy can provide the foundation from which more detailed documents are developed.

For example:

```text
                 IS POLICY
                     │
       ┌─────────────┼─────────────┐
       ↓             ↓             ↓
 Access Control   Incident      Risk Management
    Policy        Policy          Policy
       │             │             │
       ↓             ↓             ↓
 Procedures      Procedures      Procedures
       │             │             │
       ↓             ↓             ↓
 Work Instructions / Standards
```

This creates consistency throughout the ISMS documentation.

---

# 13. IS Policy Should Be Concise and High-Level

The policy should be sufficiently comprehensive to communicate management's direction, but it should not become unnecessarily large.

It should allow employees and stakeholders to understand:

- Why information security matters.
    
- What the organization is committed to.
    
- What their responsibilities are.
    
- Where detailed requirements can be found.
    

The policy should therefore balance:

> **Completeness + Clarity + Practicality**

# 20. IS Policy vs Scope Document vs SoA

These documents should not be confused.

|Document|Main Purpose|
|---|---|
|**IS Policy**|Defines high-level information security direction and commitments|
|**ISMS Scope**|Defines where the ISMS applies|
|**Risk Assessment**|Identifies and evaluates information security risks|
|**Risk Treatment Plan**|Defines how identified risks will be treated|
|**SoA**|Documents applicable Annex A controls and justification|
|**Procedures**|Define how processes are performed|
|**Standards**|Define specific security requirements|

### Easy way to remember

> **Policy = Direction**  
> **Scope = Boundaries**  
> **Risk Assessment = What can go wrong**  
> **Risk Treatment = What we will do about it**  
> **SoA = Which controls apply and why**  
> **Procedure = How we perform the activity**

# 23. RACI and Segregation of Duties

The organization should clearly define who is:

- **Responsible**
    
- **Accountable**
    
- **Consulted**
    
- **Informed**
    

A **RACI matrix** can be used to clarify responsibilities.

Segregation of Duties (**SoD**) can also be used to avoid conflicts of interest.

### Example

A person who requests privileged access should not necessarily be the same person who approves that access.

This helps reduce the possibility of:

- Fraud
    
- Unauthorized activity
    
- Conflicts of interest
    
- Abuse of privileges
    

---

# 24. Avoid Conflicts of Interest

Responsibilities should be assigned carefully.

For example:

```text
User requests access
        ↓
Manager approves business need
        ↓
Security / IAM validates
        ↓
Administrator implements access
        ↓
Access is periodically reviewed
```

Separating these responsibilities provides stronger control.

---

# 25. Avoid Copying Generic Templates

The implementation guide strongly emphasizes that organizations should create an IS Policy that reflects their **own organization**.

Templates can be useful for:

- Ideas
    
- Structure
    
- Possible content
    
- Document organization
    

But blindly copying a generic template is not ideal.

The policy should visibly reflect:

- Company's business objectives
    
- Organization's risks
    
- Information security requirements
    
- Organizational structure
    
- Industry
    
- ISMS scope
    
- Relevant stakeholders
    

Employees are more likely to identify with a policy that clearly relates to their actual work.

---

# 26. Example of a Weak IS Policy

❌

> "The company is committed to information security and follows all applicable standards."

Problems:

- Too generic
    
- Does not explain management commitment
    
- Does not establish clear direction
    
- Does not reflect organizational context
    
- Does not clearly communicate continual improvement
    

---

# 27. Example of a Better IS Policy Statement

A stronger high-level policy might state that the organization:

> Is committed to protecting the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of its information and information systems; managing information security risks in alignment with business objectives; fulfilling applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, and organizational requirements; providing appropriate resources and responsibilities; communicating information security requirements to relevant personnel; and continually improving the effectiveness of the ISMS.

This is still high-level while communicating the essential commitments.

# 28. Review and Updating of the IS Policy

The policy should be reviewed periodically and whenever significant changes occur.

Possible triggers include:

- Major organizational changes
    
- New business activities
    
- Significant security incidents
    
- New regulatory requirements
    
- Changes in risk
    
- Major technology changes
    
- Changes to the ISMS scope
    
- Changes in business strategy
    
- Audit findings
    

### Example

A company expands into a new country.

This may introduce:

- New regulatory requirements
    
- New contractual obligations
    
- New data protection requirements
    
- New security risks
    

The IS Policy may therefore need to be reviewed.

# 30. IS Policy and ISO 27001 Requirements

The IS Policy is particularly connected to **ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 5.2 – Information Security Policy**.

The policy should:

- Be appropriate to the organization's purpose.
    
- Provide a framework for setting information security objectives.
    
- Include a commitment to satisfy applicable information security requirements.
    
- Include a commitment to continual improvement of the ISMS.
    
- Be available as documented information.
    
- Be communicated within the organization.
    
- Be available to interested parties as appropriate.

# Final Professional Summary

The **Information Security Policy** is the organization's **high-level strategic statement for information security**. It is approved by top management and establishes the direction, principles, and commitments that guide the ISMS.

A good IS Policy should:

1. Be **appropriate to the organization's purpose and context**.
    
2. Be approved by **top management**.
    
3. Demonstrate **management commitment**.
    
4. Support the organization's **business objectives**.
    
5. Provide a framework for establishing **IS objectives**.
    
6. Include commitment to fulfilling **applicable information security requirements**.
    
7. Include commitment to **continual improvement of the ISMS**.
    
8. Be maintained as **documented information**.
    
9. Be properly **controlled and periodically reviewed**.
    
10. Be **communicated to relevant employees**.
    
11. Make security responsibilities understandable.
    
12. Provide a foundation for detailed **policies, standards, procedures, and work instructions**.
    
13. Be customized to the organization's actual **risks, requirements, scope, and business environment**.
    
14. Avoid unnecessary technical and operational details.
    

### One-line interview answer

> **"The ISO 27001 Information Security Policy is a top-management-approved, high-level document that establishes the organization's strategic direction and commitment to information security, compliance with applicable requirements, achievement of security objectives, and continual improvement of the ISMS."**
