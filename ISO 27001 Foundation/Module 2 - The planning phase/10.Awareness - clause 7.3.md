
## 1. Purpose of Clause 7.3

Clause 7.3 of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 deals with **Awareness**.

The organization must ensure that people working under its control are aware of the organization's information security requirements and understand how their actions affect information security and the ISMS.

In simple terms:

> **Competence means knowing how to perform a task; awareness means understanding what you are expected to do, why it matters, and what can happen if you do not follow the requirements.**

---

# 2. What Should Employees Be Aware Of?

People working for or on behalf of the organization should be aware of:

### 1. Information Security Policy

Employees should know that the organization has an information security policy and understand the security principles relevant to their work.

Example:

An employee should know that company information must not be shared with unauthorized people.

---

### 2. Their Contribution to the ISMS

Employees should understand:

- Their individual security responsibilities
    
- How their activities affect information security
    
- How they contribute to the effectiveness of the ISMS
    
- The importance of following security policies and procedures
    

For example, an employee who reports a suspicious phishing email contributes to the organization's ability to detect and respond to security incidents.

---

### 3. Consequences of Non-Compliance

Employees should understand what can happen if information security requirements are not followed.

Possible consequences include:

- Security incidents
    
- Data leakage
    
- Unauthorized access
    
- Malware infection
    
- Business disruption
    
- Regulatory violations
    
- Financial losses
    
- Damage to organizational reputation
    
- Disciplinary consequences, where applicable
    

The objective is not simply to tell employees **what not to do**, but to help them understand **why the requirements exist**.

---

# 3. Competence vs Awareness

This distinction is very important.

|Competence|Awareness|
|---|---|
|Ability to perform a task|Understanding security responsibilities|
|Focuses on skills and capability|Focuses on understanding and behavior|
|"Can I do it?"|"Do I know what I should do and why?"|
|Clause 7.2|Clause 7.3|
|Example: SOC analyst knows how to investigate an alert|Example: Employee knows that suspicious activity must be reported|

### Example

A person may be technically competent enough to use a company system.

However, they may not be aware that:

> "I must not share my credentials with another employee."

Therefore, competence alone does not guarantee secure behavior.

---

# 4. Why Awareness Is Important

An organization can have:

- Strong security policies
    
- Detailed procedures
    
- Advanced security technologies
    
- Effective security controls
    
- A well-designed ISMS
    

But if employees do not understand the security requirements, the ISMS can still fail.

### Example

An organization has a policy stating:

> "Employees must report suspected phishing emails to the SOC."

But employees do not know:

- What phishing looks like
    
- Where to report it
    
- Why reporting is important
    

As a result, phishing emails may remain undetected.

Therefore:

> **Security policies are effective only when people understand and follow them.**

# 6. Security Awareness Plan

ISO 27001 does not prescribe a particular awareness program format.

However, a structured **Information Security Awareness Plan** is a useful practical approach.

The plan can define:

### Awareness Topics

For example:

- Phishing
    
- Password security
    
- MFA
    
- Data classification
    
- Secure remote working
    
- Social engineering
    
- Incident reporting
    
- Physical security
    
- Acceptable use
    
- Data protection
    

---

### Target Audience

Different audiences may require different awareness activities.

|Audience|Example Awareness|
|---|---|
|All employees|Phishing and password security|
|Developers|Secure coding|
|IT administrators|Privileged access security|
|SOC analysts|Incident handling|
|Executives|Cybersecurity risks|
|HR|Protection of employee information|

---

### Frequency

Activities can be scheduled at different frequencies.

For example:

```text
Weekly
   ↓
Security tips / alerts

Monthly
   ↓
Security awareness campaign

Quarterly
   ↓
Security workshop

Annually
   ↓
Mandatory security awareness training
```

The actual frequency should be based on organizational needs and risks.

# 8. Example – Phishing Awareness Program

Suppose the organization identifies phishing as a significant risk.

### Objective

> Improve employee ability to identify and report phishing emails.

### Awareness activities

1. Send phishing awareness material.
    
2. Conduct an awareness presentation.
    
3. Provide an online training module.
    
4. Run a phishing simulation.
    
5. Explain how to report suspicious emails.
    
6. Provide feedback to employees.
    
7. Repeat training where necessary.
    

### Possible measurement

The organization could monitor indicators such as:

- Phishing simulation click rate
    
- Phishing reporting rate
    
- Training completion rate
    
- Number of reported suspicious emails
    

This connects **awareness activities with security objectives and performance monitoring**.

---

# 9. Awareness Is Not Just Training

Awareness should not be interpreted as simply conducting one annual training session.

A strong awareness program can include:

```text
Training
   +
Communication
   +
Practical Exercises
   +
Security Campaigns
   +
Reminders
   +
Management Participation
   +
Feedback
```

The objective is to develop a **security-aware organizational culture**.

---

# 10. Documentation

A useful distinction:

> **Clause 7.3 does not specifically require documented information proving that awareness activities were performed.**

However, organizations may choose to document awareness activities as good practice and as evidence of effective ISMS operation.

Examples include:

- Awareness plan
    
- Training calendar
    
- Attendance records
    
- Training completion reports
    
- Awareness materials
    
- Phishing simulation results
    
- Security campaign records
    
- Employee acknowledgements
    
- Awareness assessment results
    

Documentation can make it easier to demonstrate that awareness activities are planned and implemented.

# 12. Competence + Awareness + Resources

Clauses 7.1, 7.2, and 7.3 work together.

```text
7.1 Resources
      ↓
Provide people, time, budget and technology
      ↓
7.2 Competence
      ↓
Ensure people have the required skills
      ↓
7.3 Awareness
      ↓
Ensure people understand their responsibilities
      ↓
Effective ISMS
```

### Example: SOC Analyst

**7.1 – Resources**

Provide:

- SIEM
    
- EDR
    
- Analyst staffing
    
- Training budget
    
- Investigation time
    

**7.2 – Competence**

Ensure the analyst knows:

- SIEM queries
    
- Log analysis
    
- Incident response
    
- Threat detection
    

**7.3 – Awareness**

Ensure the analyst understands:

- SOC procedures
    
- Information security policy
    
- Escalation responsibilities
    
- Impact of failing to follow procedures
