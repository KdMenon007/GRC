
## 1. Purpose of Clause 7.2

Clause 7.2 of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 deals with **Competence**.

The organization must ensure that people performing work that affects information security are **competent to perform their assigned responsibilities**.

In simple terms:

> **The organization must identify the skills and knowledge required for information security activities and ensure that the people performing those activities have the necessary competence through education, training, or experience.**

This is important because an ISMS depends not only on policies and technology but also on the people who implement and operate them.

---

# 2. What Does Competence Mean?

Competence is the combination of the **knowledge, skills, training, education, and experience** required to perform a particular activity effectively.

For example, a SOC analyst may need competence in:

- Networking
    
- Windows and Linux security
    
- SIEM
    
- EDR/XDR
    
- Incident response
    
- Threat detection
    
- Log analysis
    
- MITRE ATT&CK
    
- Malware analysis
    
- Threat intelligence
    

The organization should determine what competence is required for each relevant role.

---

# 3. Identifying Required Competencies

The organization should first determine:

> **What knowledge and skills are required to perform this role effectively?**

Examples:

|Role|Required Competence|
|---|---|
|SOC Analyst|SIEM, log analysis, incident investigation|
|Security Engineer|Network security, firewalls, EDR, security architecture|
|Risk Manager|Risk assessment, risk treatment, risk methodology|
|Internal Auditor|Audit methodology, ISO 27001 requirements|
|CISO/ISMS Manager|Security governance, risk management, leadership|
|System Administrator|Secure configuration, patching, access control|

The required competence should be appropriate to the person's responsibilities.

---

# 4. How Can an Organization Achieve Competence?

If an employee does not initially possess the required competence, the organization should take appropriate action.

Possible methods include:

### 1. External Training

Employees can attend external courses or professional training.

Examples:

- ISO 27001 Lead Implementer
    
- ISO 27001 Lead Auditor
    
- Security certifications
    
- SIEM training
    
- Incident response training
    

---

### 2. Internal Training

The organization can provide training internally.

Examples:

- Security awareness sessions
    
- SOC investigation workshops
    
- Secure coding training
    
- Incident response exercises
    
- Internal ISO 27001 training
    

---

### 3. Mentoring

An experienced employee can guide a less experienced employee.

Example:

A junior SOC analyst works with a senior analyst to learn:

- Alert investigation
    
- Log analysis
    
- Incident triage
    
- Escalation procedures
    

---

### 4. Experience

Competence can also be developed through practical experience.

For example:

A SOC analyst may become competent in incident response through repeated participation in real incidents and incident-response exercises.

---

### 5. Reassignment

If appropriate, an employee may be moved to a role that better matches their existing competencies while they develop additional skills.

---

# 5. Competence Gap

Organizations should identify gaps between:

**Required competence**

and

**Existing competence**

For example:

```text
Required Skill
      ↓
Incident Response
      ↓
Employee Current Skill
      ↓
Basic Knowledge
      ↓
Competence Gap
      ↓
Training / Mentoring / Experience
      ↓
Competent Employee
```

### Example

Required:

> Advanced SIEM investigation skills

Current capability:

> Basic SIEM knowledge

Gap:

> Advanced investigation capability

Action:

> Provide advanced SIEM training and practical mentoring.

---

# 6. Training Is Not the Same as Competence

This is an important distinction.

Completing training does **not automatically prove that someone is competent**.

For example:

An employee attends a three-day incident response course.

That proves:

> The employee attended the training.

It does not necessarily prove:

> The employee can independently perform incident response effectively.

Therefore, organizations should consider whether the training, education, or experience has actually resulted in the required competence.

Possible evidence includes:

- Examination results
    
- Practical assessments
    
- Certifications
    
- Performance evaluations
    
- Supervisor assessments
    
- Successful completion of practical exercises
    
- Demonstrated work experience
    

---

# 7. Documenting Competence

ISO/IEC 27001 requires the organization to **retain appropriate documented information as evidence of competence**.

Examples include:

- Training certificates
    
- Professional certifications
    
- Training attendance records
    
- Examination results
    
- Qualification records
    
- Education records
    
- Experience records
    
- Training history
    
- Competency assessments
    
- Performance evaluation records
    

### Example

For an ISO 27001 internal auditor:

|Evidence|Example|
|---|---|
|Training|ISO 27001 Internal Auditor course|
|Certification|Lead Auditor certification|
|Experience|Previous audit experience|
|Assessment|Internal auditor competency assessment|
|Training record|HR training history|

# 8. Human Resources Competence Procedure

A good practice is to establish a **human resources competence or training management procedure**.

The procedure can define:

### Step 1 – Identify Training Needs

Determine what skills are required for each role.

### Step 2 – Identify Competence Gaps

Compare required skills with existing employee capabilities.

### Step 3 – Develop Training Plan

Define:

- Training required
    
- Employee responsible
    
- Training provider
    
- Target date
    
- Budget
    
- Expected outcome
    

### Step 4 – Provide Training

Training may be:

- Internal
    
- External
    
- Online
    
- Classroom-based
    
- Mentoring
    
- Practical exercises
    

### Step 5 – Evaluate Effectiveness

Determine whether the employee acquired the required competence.

### Step 6 – Maintain Records

Keep evidence of:

- Training
    
- Qualifications
    
- Certifications
    
- Assessments
    
- Experience

- Linux logs
    
- SIEM queries

# 10. Why Competence Is Critical to Information Security

An organization can have:

- Excellent policies
    
- Strong security controls
    
- Advanced security technologies
    
- Detailed procedures
    
- A well-designed ISMS
    

But security can still fail if employees do not know how to use those controls correctly.

For example:

> A company may have an excellent incident response procedure, but if SOC analysts do not know how to investigate and escalate incidents, the procedure will not provide the expected level of protection.

Therefore:

**Effective Security = People + Processes + Technology**

Competent people are essential for converting policies, processes, and controls into effective security outcomes.

---

# 11. Competence vs Awareness

Do not confuse **Clause 7.2 – Competence** with **Clause 7.3 – Awareness**.

|Competence|Awareness|
|---|---|
|Ability to perform a task effectively|Understanding the importance of security|
|Role-specific|Applies more broadly|
|Based on education, training, skills, experience|Based on understanding and knowledge|
|Example: SOC analyst can investigate alerts|Example: Employee knows how to report phishing|
|Clause **7.2**|Clause **7.3**|

### Simple memory:

> **Competence = "Can you do the job?"**

> **Awareness = "Do you understand your security responsibilities?"**

---

# 12. Annex A Connection

Human-resource-related security is also addressed in **Annex A** of ISO/IEC 27001:2022.

However, there is an important distinction:

- **Clause 7.2** establishes the ISMS requirement for ensuring competence.
    
- **Annex A** provides relevant information security controls that can be selected and implemented based on the organization's risk assessment.
    

Therefore, Clause 7.2 is part of the **management-system requirements**, while relevant Annex A controls support the organization's information security risk treatment.
