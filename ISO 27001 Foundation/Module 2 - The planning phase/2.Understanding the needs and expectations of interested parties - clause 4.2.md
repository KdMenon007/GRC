Clause 4.2 – Understanding Interested Parties

## Purpose

The organization must identify the **interested parties** that are relevant to the ISMS and determine their relevant requirements.

An interested party is a person, organization, authority, or other entity that can affect, be affected by, or have requirements concerning the organization's information security.


---

# 1. Examples of Interested Parties

|Interested Party|Possible Information Security Requirements|
|---|---|
|Customers|Protection of customer information|
|Employees|Secure working environment|
|Suppliers|Secure information exchange|
|Service Providers|Security requirements in contracts|
|Regulators|Compliance with applicable regulations|
|Legislators|Compliance with laws|
|Shareholders|Protection of business information|
|Board/Supervisory Board|Effective risk management|
|Works Council|Employee-related requirements|
|Subcontractors|Security requirements|
|Strategic Partners|Secure collaboration|

# 2. Types of Requirements

Interested-party requirements can come from several sources.

## 2.1 Legal Requirements

Examples:

- Data protection laws
    
- Privacy regulations
    
- Cybersecurity laws
    

---

## 2.2 Regulatory Requirements

Requirements imposed by regulatory authorities.

Examples:

- Banking regulations
    
- Financial-sector cybersecurity requirements
    
- Government requirements
    

---

## 2.3 Contractual Requirements

Requirements agreed with customers, suppliers, or partners.

Examples:

- Customer security requirements
    
- Security audit rights
    
- Incident notification requirements
    
- Data protection obligations
    

---

## 2.4 Internal Requirements

The organization itself may establish requirements through:

- Internal policies
    
- Corporate security standards
    
- Group-level directives
    
- Business objectives
    

A parent or higher-level organization may also have decision-making or directive authority over the organization implementing the ISMS.

---

# 3. Requirements Analysis

A **requirements analysis** provides management with a clear overview of:

- Who the interested parties are
    
- What requirements they have
    
- Which requirements affect information security
    
- Which requirements affect the ISMS
    

Sources used to identify requirements may include:

- Business plans
    
- Contracts
    
- Laws
    
- Regulations
    
- Customer agreements
    
- Supplier agreements
    
- Internal policies
    
- Regulatory requirements
    
- Industry requirements
    

In practice, a **Compliance or IT Compliance function** may assist with collecting and maintaining these requirements.

---

# 4. Requirements Register

A practical organization can maintain an interested-party and requirements register.

Example:

|Interested Party|Requirement|Type|Relevant to ISMS|
|---|---|---|---|
|Customer|Protect customer data|Contractual|Yes|
|Regulator|Meet cybersecurity requirements|Regulatory|Yes|
|Employee|Security awareness|Internal|Yes|
|Supplier|Secure information exchange|Contractual|Yes|
|Shareholder|Protect company assets|Business|Yes|

This provides evidence that Clause 4.2 has been addressed systematically.
