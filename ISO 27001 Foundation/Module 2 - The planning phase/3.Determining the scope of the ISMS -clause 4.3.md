Clause 4.3 – Determining the Scope of the ISMS

## Purpose

The ISMS scope defines the **boundaries and applicability** of the Information Security Management System.

It answers:

> **"Which parts of the organization and which information assets are covered by the ISMS?"**

The scope must be **documented**.

---

# 1. What Can Be Included in the ISMS Scope?

The scope can cover:

### Organizational Units

- Departments
    
- Business units
    
- Subsidiaries
    

### Locations

- Headquarters
    
- Branch offices
    
- Data centers
    
- Remote locations
    

### Processes

- Customer service
    
- IT operations
    
- Payment processing
    
- Software development
    

### Technology

- Applications
    
- Servers
    
- Networks
    
- Cloud platforms
    
- Databases
    

### Information Assets

- Customer information
    
- Financial information
    
- Intellectual property
    
- Employee information
    

---

# 2. Scope Boundaries

The scope should clearly establish what is:

### Included

and what is:

### Outside the Scope

Example:

**Included:**

- Corporate IT infrastructure
    
- Customer-facing applications
    
- Cloud infrastructure
    
- Customer databases
    
- Supporting IT processes
    

**Excluded:**

- An independent subsidiary not controlled by the organization
    
- A business unit outside the defined ISMS boundary
    

Any exclusions should be clearly understood and justifiable.

---

# 3. Example of an ISMS Scope Statement

> **"The ISMS covers the organization's customer-facing applications, supporting IT infrastructure, cloud environments, customer information, and associated IT operations at the defined organizational locations."**

A good scope should be sufficiently detailed to allow stakeholders to understand exactly what the ISMS covers.

---

# 4. Why Scope Definition Is Important

Scope definition is one of the **first and most important activities** in an ISO 27001 implementation.

An incorrect or vague scope can result in:

- Important assets being excluded
    
- Incomplete risk assessments
    
- Incorrect control selection
    
- Misleading certification claims
    
- Unexpected customer requirements
    
- Increased audit issues
    

Therefore, scope definition should be performed carefully.

---

# 5. Scope and ISO 27001 Certification

An important practical point:

> **An organization having an ISO 27001 certificate does not necessarily mean that every part of the organization is covered by that certificate.**

A certificate applies to the **defined ISMS scope**.

For example:

```text
Company
│
├── Business Unit A ─── Included in ISMS
├── Business Unit B ─── Included in ISMS
├── Business Unit C ─── Outside ISMS
└── Subsidiary D ────── Outside ISMS
```

Therefore, when evaluating a supplier, customer, or service provider, it is important to review the **actual certification scope**, not just the existence of an ISO 27001 certificate.

---

# 6. Scope Document vs Security Policy vs SoA

These documents have different purposes.

|Document|Purpose|Typical Audience|
|---|---|---|
|**ISMS Scope**|Defines ISMS boundaries|Internal and relevant external stakeholders|
|**Information Security Policy**|Defines security direction and commitment|Mainly internal|
|**Statement of Applicability**|Documents control applicability and justification|Mainly internal|

The scope document may be made available to stakeholders upon request when appropriate.

The security policy and SoA are generally treated as internal documents, although their contents may need to be considered when dealing with service providers and supplier audits.

---

# 7. Service Provider Consideration

Scope becomes especially important when dealing with:

- Cloud providers
    
- Managed service providers
    
- Outsourced IT
    
- Security service providers
    
- SaaS providers
    

A supplier may claim:

> "We are ISO 27001 certified."

But the important question is:

> **"Does the ISO 27001 certification scope cover the specific service being provided?"**

For example:

```text
Supplier ISO 27001 Certificate
             ↓
      Check Certification Scope
             ↓
Does it cover the required service?
             ↓
          Yes / No
```

This prevents organizations from relying on a certificate that does not actually cover the relevant service or process.

# 8. Environment Analysis

## Purpose

The environment analysis integrates the ISMS into the broader organizational environment.

It considers:

- Organizational interfaces
    
- Technical interfaces
    
- Internal management systems
    
- Business processes
    
- External relationships
    
- Industry-specific conditions
    
- Location-specific conditions
    

---

## Internal Environment Analysis

Consider:

- Organizational structure
    
- Existing management systems
    
- Risk management
    
- HR
    
- Data protection
    
- Facility management
    
- Internal audit
    
- Legal
    
- IT operations
    

---

## External Environment Analysis

Consider:

- Suppliers
    
- Service providers
    
- Strategic partners
    
- Customers
    
- Regulators
    
- Industry conditions
    
- External threats
    

---

# 9. Interface Agreements

A useful practical document is an **interface agreement** between the ISMS area and internal departments that support the ISMS.

The purpose is to ensure that cooperation between departments meets:

- ISO/IEC 27001 requirements
    
- Organizational security requirements
    
- Defined responsibilities
    

### Example: HR Interface Agreement

HR may be responsible for:

- Employee onboarding
    
- Employee offboarding
    
- Security awareness
    
- Background verification where applicable
    
- Employee records
    

The ISMS/security function may require HR to:

- Notify security about employee termination
    
- Support access revocation
    
- Maintain training records
    

This relationship can be formally documented through an interface agreement or similar arrangement.

---

# 10. Why Context Analysis Is Critical

Understanding the organizational context influences almost every subsequent ISMS activity.

```text
Context Analysis
       ↓
Scope Definition
       ↓
Risk Assessment
       ↓
Risk Treatment
       ↓
Control Selection
       ↓
Security Objectives
       ↓
ISMS Implementation
```

Therefore, an incorrect understanding of the context can affect the entire ISMS.

---
