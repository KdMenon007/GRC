
## 1. Overview

**Leadership and Commitment** is a fundamental requirement for establishing and operating an effective Information Security Management System (ISMS).

ISO/IEC 27001 follows a **top-down approach**, meaning that information security must be driven and supported by management rather than being treated as only an IT or cybersecurity responsibility.

The fundamental idea is:

> **Top management establishes direction, provides resources, makes decisions, and demonstrates through its actions that information security is a business priority.**

An effective ISMS creates a connection between:

```text
Business Objectives
        ↓
Information Security Objectives
        ↓
Risk Management
        ↓
Security Controls
        ↓
Protection of Business Processes
```

---

# 2. Why Leadership Is Important

Information security affects the entire organization.

It involves:

- Business processes
    
- Employees
    
- IT systems
    
- Suppliers
    
- Customers
    
- Legal requirements
    
- Financial resources
    
- Risk management
    

Therefore, security cannot be implemented successfully by the IT/security department alone.

Management support is required when the organization needs to:

- Change organization-wide processes
    
- Allocate budgets
    
- Assign responsibilities
    
- Accept or mitigate risks
    
- Establish policies
    
- Implement security controls
    
- Change employee behavior
    
- Integrate security into business processes
    

Without management commitment, security processes may not have sufficient **authority, resources, or organizational acceptance**.

---

# 3. Top-Down Approach

ISO 27001 implementation should follow a top-down approach.

```text
                 TOP MANAGEMENT
                       ↓
              Security Strategy
                       ↓
              Security Objectives
                       ↓
               Risk Management
                       ↓
              Security Processes
                       ↓
                Security Controls
                       ↓
                  Employees
```

Top management establishes the direction, and the rest of the organization implements and operates the required processes and controls.

---

# 4. Business and Information Security Alignment

One of the main responsibilities of leadership is to establish a connection between **business objectives and information security objectives**.

Security should support business objectives rather than operate independently.

### Example

### Business Objective

> Provide online banking services 24/7.

### Information Security Objective

> Maintain high availability and resilience of critical banking systems.

### Security Measures

- High-availability infrastructure
    
- Disaster recovery
    
- Backup
    
- Monitoring
    
- Incident response
    

Therefore:

```text
Business Goal
     ↓
Security Objective
     ↓
Risk Assessment
     ↓
Security Controls
```

# 5. Understanding Business Objectives and Requirements

Before management can establish an effective ISMS, it must understand:

- Business objectives
    
- Business processes
    
- Stakeholder requirements
    
- Information security risks
    
- Legal requirements
    
- Regulatory requirements
    
- Contractual obligations
    

This information allows management to determine the appropriate security strategy.

# 6. Organizational Conditions

Management must create appropriate organizational conditions for the ISMS.

This may include:

- Establishing risk management processes
    
- Defining security responsibilities
    
- Creating security policies
    
- Establishing reporting structures
    
- Allocating resources
    
- Integrating security into business processes
    
- Integrating security into project management
    

---

# 7. "Tone from the Top"

A key concept associated with leadership and commitment is:

> **Tone from the Top**

This means that management demonstrates through its **actions and behavior** that information security is important.

Management should not merely say:

> "Security is important."

It should demonstrate this by:

- Following security requirements itself
    
- Providing adequate resources
    
- Attending security events
    
- Reviewing security performance
    
- Taking security risks seriously
    
- Responding to nonconformities
    
- Supporting continual improvement
    

Employees are more likely to follow security requirements when management acts as a role model.

---

# 8. Overall Responsibility for Information Security

Top management must **demonstrably assume overall responsibility for information security** within the organization and for the effectiveness of the ISMS.

This does not mean that top management personally performs every security activity.

Instead, management is responsible for ensuring that:

- Appropriate responsibilities are assigned
    
- Resources are available
    
- Security objectives are established
    
- Risks are managed
    
- The ISMS is effective
    
- Security requirements are communicated
    
- Performance is reviewed
    

---

# 9. Communication of ISMS Importance

Management must communicate the importance of:

- Effective information security
    
- The ISMS
    
- Compliance with ISMS requirements
    
- Security responsibilities
    

Communication can occur through:

- Information security policy
    
- Security guidelines
    
- User policies
    
- Awareness programs
    
- Management presentations
    
- Training
    
- Internal communications
    
- Leadership messages
    

### Example

The CEO delivers a short video message explaining:

> "Protecting customer information is a business priority, and every employee is responsible for following our information security requirements."

This is an example of visible management commitment.

---

# 10. Central Responsibilities of Top Management

The major responsibilities can be summarized as follows.

---

## 10.1 Assume Overall Responsibility

Management must take overall responsibility for information security and the ISMS.

This means management cannot treat security as solely the responsibility of:

- IT
    
- SOC
    
- CISO
    
- Security team
    

Security is an organizational responsibility.

---

# 10.2 Define the Information Security Strategy

Management establishes the organization's information security direction.

The strategy should align with:

- Business strategy
    
- Risk appetite
    
- Stakeholder requirements
    
- Regulatory requirements
    

---

# 10.3 Define Information Security Objectives

Management establishes concrete security objectives.

Examples:

- Reduce critical vulnerabilities
    
- Improve incident response
    
- Increase security awareness
    
- Improve backup reliability
    
- Reduce security incidents
    

Objectives should be measurable where appropriate.

### Example

❌ Weak objective:

> Improve cybersecurity.

✅ Better objective:

> Reduce the number of critical vulnerabilities remaining open for more than 30 days by 80%.

---

# 10.4 Establish Risk Assessment and Treatment Principles

Management should establish decision criteria and principles for:

- Risk assessment
    
- Risk evaluation
    
- Risk treatment
    
- Risk acceptance
    

Management determines how much risk the organization is willing to accept.

This is closely related to the organization's **risk appetite**.

---

# 10.5 Integrate Security into Business Processes

Information security should be integrated into normal business operations.

Examples:

### HR Process

Security requirements during:

- Employee onboarding
    
- Employee transfer
    
- Employee termination
    

### Procurement Process

Security requirements for:

- Vendor selection
    
- Supplier contracts
    
- Third-party assessments
    

### Project Management

Security requirements during:

- Project planning
    
- System development
    
- System deployment
    

---

# 10.6 Integrate Security into Project Management

Security should be considered from the beginning of projects rather than after implementation.

Example:

A company develops a customer application.

Security should be considered during:

```text
Project Planning
      ↓
Requirements
      ↓
Design
      ↓
Development
      ↓
Testing
      ↓
Deployment
```

rather than only after the application goes live.

---

# 10.7 Conduct Management Reviews

Top management must periodically review the ISMS.

The management review evaluates whether the ISMS remains:

- Suitable
    
- Adequate
    
- Effective
    

Management reviews can consider:

- Audit results
    
- Security incidents
    
- Risk status
    
- Security objectives
    
- Control effectiveness
    
- Nonconformities
    
- Corrective actions
    
- Changes affecting the ISMS
    
- Improvement opportunities
    

This is addressed under **Clause 9.3 – Management Review**.

---

# 10.8 Provide Human and Financial Resources

Management must provide sufficient resources to establish, operate, and improve the ISMS.

### Human Resources

Examples:

- CISO
    
- ISMS manager
    
- SOC analysts
    
- Risk specialists
    
- Auditors
    

### Financial Resources

Examples:

- Security tools
    
- Training
    
- Consulting
    
- Certification
    
- Security assessments
    

### Technical Resources

Examples:

- SIEM
    
- EDR
    
- Vulnerability scanners
    
- Identity management
    
- Backup infrastructure
    

---

# 10.9 Be Visible in Security Awareness Activities

Management should actively participate in security awareness activities.

Examples:

- CEO security message
    
- Management participation in security awareness events
    
- Security training opening sessions
    
- Security campaigns
    
- Phishing awareness initiatives
    

This demonstrates that security is a **business priority**, not merely an IT requirement.

---

# 11. Management as a Role Model

Top management should demonstrate appropriate security behavior.

This includes:

### Exemplary Compliance

Management should follow the same security requirements expected from employees.

For example:

- Using MFA
    
- Following access control requirements
    
- Protecting confidential information
    
- Completing mandatory security training
    

---

### Providing Traceable Resources

Management decisions regarding security resources should be:

- Sufficient
    
- Justifiable
    
- Traceable
    

Example:

Management approves additional SOC resources because incident volume has increased.

---

### Expecting Leadership from Other Managers

Top management should require other management levels to demonstrate appropriate security behavior.

For example:

Department managers should ensure that:

- Employees complete security training
    
- Access reviews are performed
    
- Security policies are followed
    
- Incidents are reported
    

---

# 12. Responding to Nonconformities

Management must consistently address security nonconformities.

A nonconformity may include:

- Failure to follow an approved security procedure
    
- Missing audit evidence
    
- Inadequate access reviews
    
- Failure to meet a security objective
    

Management should ensure that the organization:

```text
Identify Nonconformity
        ↓
Understand Root Cause
        ↓
Take Corrective Action
        ↓
Verify Effectiveness
        ↓
Prevent Recurrence
```

Ignoring repeated security nonconformities weakens the ISMS.

---

# 13. Commitment to Continual Improvement

Management must demonstrate commitment to continually improving the ISMS.

Improvement can be driven by:

- Internal audits
    
- Security incidents
    
- Risk assessments
    
- Management reviews
    
- Changes in business
    
- Changes in technology
    
- New threats
    
- Regulatory changes
    

Example:

A ransomware incident reveals weaknesses in backup procedures.

Management supports:

- Improved backup architecture
    
- Additional monitoring
    
- Updated incident response procedures
    
- Additional employee training
    

This demonstrates leadership commitment to improvement.

---

# 14. Who Is "Top Management"?

In ISO 27001, **top management** refers to the management level that:

- Controls the organization within the ISMS scope
    
- Has authority over the organization or relevant part of it
    
- Can make decisions regarding resources
    

It does **not necessarily have to mean the highest management level of the entire corporate group**.

---

# 15. Large Organizations

In a large organization, the ISMS may cover only a particular:

- Business unit
    
- Division
    
- Region
    
- Subsidiary
    
- Location
    

Therefore, the relevant top management may be the management responsible for that specific ISMS scope.

### Example

```text
Global Corporation
│
├── Europe Division
│      └── ISMS Scope
│
├── Asia Division
│
└── Americas Division
```

If the ISMS covers only the **Europe Division**, the relevant top management may be the management responsible for that division.

---

# 16. Certification Audit Consideration

When certification is being pursued, it is important to clarify the expected level of management involvement with the certification body.

A certification body may expect evidence that the management responsible for the relevant ISMS scope is involved and has appropriate authority.

Therefore, organizations should clarify this point with the certification body before beginning the certification process.

# 24. Success Factors from Practice

The most important practical success factors are:

### 1. Visible Management Commitment

Management should actively demonstrate support rather than simply approve documents.

### 2. Management as a Role Model

Leaders should follow the same security requirements expected from employees.

### 3. Adequate Resources

Resources should be:

- Sufficient
    
- Appropriate
    
- Traceable
    

### 4. Clear Decision-Making

Management should make clear decisions regarding:

- Security priorities
    
- Risk acceptance
    
- Risk treatment
    
- Investments
    

### 5. Regular Reporting

Management should receive regular information about:

- Risks
    
- Incidents
    
- Audit results
    
- Control effectiveness
    
- Security objectives
    

### 6. Response to Nonconformities

Security gaps should be addressed rather than ignored.

### 7. Continual Improvement

Management should actively support improvements to the ISMS.

## Final Key Concept

The central message of **ISO/IEC 27001 Leadership and Commitment** is:

> **Information security must be led by management, aligned with business objectives, supported with adequate resources, integrated into business processes, communicated throughout the organization, regularly reviewed, and continuously improved.**

In short:

```text
Management Commitment
        ↓
Business Alignment
        ↓
Resources + Governance
        ↓
Risk Management
        ↓
ISMS Operation
        ↓
Management Review
        ↓
Continual Improvement
```

**Without genuine management commitment, an ISMS risks becoming only a collection of policies and controls rather than an effective management system.**
