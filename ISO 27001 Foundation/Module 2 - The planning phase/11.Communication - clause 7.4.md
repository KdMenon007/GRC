
## 1. Purpose of Clause 7.4

Clause 7.4 of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 deals with **Communication**.

The organization must determine the information security communications it needs, including:

- **What** needs to be communicated
    
- **When** it needs to be communicated
    
- **Who** should communicate it
    
- **To whom** it should be communicated
    
- Whether the communication is **internal or external**
    

In simple terms:

> **Clause 7.4 ensures that the right security information reaches the right people, at the right time, through the right person or channel.**

---

# 2. Why Is Communication Important?

Information security depends heavily on effective communication.

An organization may have:

- Good security policies
    
- Effective controls
    
- Competent employees
    
- Strong security technology
    

But if important security information is not communicated properly, the ISMS may still fail.

For example:

> The SOC detects a critical security incident, but the appropriate management team is not informed quickly.

The organization may then be unable to:

- Make timely decisions
    
- Contain the incident
    
- Meet notification obligations
    
- Communicate with customers
    
- Protect its reputation
    

Therefore:

> **Effective communication supports effective decision-making and ISMS performance.**

---

# 3. What Must the Organization Determine?

Clause 7.4 requires the organization to determine its communication needs.

A useful way to remember this is:

```text
WHAT?
  ↓
WHEN?
  ↓
WHO?
  ↓
TO WHOM?
  ↓
HOW?
```

Let's examine each one.

---

## 3.1 What Should Be Communicated?

The organization should identify the information that needs to be communicated.

Examples:

- Information security policy
    
- Information security objectives
    
- Security risks
    
- Security incidents
    
- Security alerts
    
- Audit results
    
- Compliance requirements
    
- Security performance
    
- Changes to security policies
    
- Security responsibilities
    
- Security requirements for suppliers
    

---

## 3.2 When Should It Be Communicated?

The organization should determine the appropriate timing or frequency.

Communication may be:

### Regular

Examples:

- Monthly security reports
    
- Quarterly risk reports
    
- Annual security awareness communication
    
- Periodic management reporting
    

### Event-Based

Examples:

- Major security incident
    
- Data breach
    
- Critical vulnerability
    
- Significant change to the ISMS
    
- Regulatory requirement
    

### Immediate

Some information may require urgent communication.

Example:

> A critical cybersecurity incident affecting a business-critical system.

---

## 3.3 Who Should Communicate?

The organization should identify the person or function responsible for communicating specific information.

Examples:

|Information|Responsible Person|
|---|---|
|Information security policy|CISO / ISMS Manager|
|Security objectives|CISO / Top Management|
|Security incident|Incident Manager / designated authority|
|Regulatory notification|Legal / Compliance|
|Customer communication|Account Management / authorized representative|
|Public communication|Public Relations / authorized spokesperson|
|Management security report|CISO|

The exact allocation depends on the organization's structure.

---

# 4. To Whom Should Information Be Communicated?

The organization should identify the appropriate recipients.

### Internal Stakeholders

Examples:

- Employees
    
- Management
    
- CISO
    
- SOC
    
- IT team
    
- HR
    
- Legal
    
- Risk management
    
- Internal audit
    

### External Stakeholders

Examples:

- Customers
    
- Suppliers
    
- Regulators
    
- Business partners
    
- External auditors
    
- Insurance providers
    
- Law enforcement, where applicable
    
- Public/media, where authorized
    

Not every piece of security information should be shared with everyone.

---

# 5. Internal vs External Communication

## Internal Communication

Information shared within the organization.

Examples:

> CISO communicates a new information security policy to employees.

> SOC communicates a critical security incident to IT and management.

> Risk management communicates updated risk requirements to business owners.

---

## External Communication

Information shared with parties outside the organization.

Examples:

> The organization informs a customer about a security incident affecting the customer's data.

> Legal or compliance communicates with a regulator regarding a reportable incident.

> An authorized public relations representative issues an external statement about a major security event.

External communication must be carefully controlled because security information may be confidential or sensitive.

---

# 6. Example – Information Security Policy

Suppose the organization updates its Information Security Policy.

A communication process might be:

```text
Top Management Approves Policy
              ↓
CISO / ISMS Manager
              ↓
Communicates Policy
              ↓
Employees
              ↓
Employees Acknowledge / Understand
```

The organization should know:

- Who communicates it?
    
- Who receives it?
    
- When is it communicated?
    
- Which communication channel is used?
    

Possible channels:

- Email
    
- Intranet
    
- Employee portal
    
- Security awareness training
    
- Town-hall meeting
    

---

# 7. Example – Security Incident

Consider a major data breach.

Different communication responsibilities may apply:

```text
Security Incident
       ↓
SOC / Incident Response Team
       ↓
Incident Management
       ↓
CISO / Management
       ↓
Legal / Compliance
       ↓
Customers / Regulators
       ↓
Public Relations, if required
```

The important point is that **different audiences may receive different information through different authorized channels**.

For example:

- SOC receives technical information.
    
- Management receives business impact and risk information.
    
- Legal receives information necessary to assess regulatory obligations.
    
- Customers receive appropriate information relevant to them.
    
- Public/media communication is handled by an authorized spokesperson.
    

---

# 8. Example – CISO and Public Relations

A practical communication arrangement could be:

### Information Security Policy

**Responsible:** CISO

**Audience:** Employees

**Purpose:** Ensure employees understand information security requirements.

### Major Security Breach

**Technical communication:** CISO / Incident Response Team

**Management communication:** CISO → Top Management

**Regulatory communication:** Legal / Compliance

**External communication:** Public Relations Officer or authorized spokesperson

This prevents unauthorized or inconsistent communication.

---

# 9. Communication in Small vs Large Organizations

The level of formality can depend on the organization's size and complexity.

### Large Organization

Responsibilities may be distributed among:

- CISO
    
- Security Operations
    
- Incident Manager
    
- Legal
    
- Compliance
    
- HR
    
- Public Relations
    
- Executive Management
    

Communication responsibilities are often formally defined.

### Small Organization

The same person may perform several communication functions.

For example:

> The CEO may communicate security policies, objectives, major incidents, and security decisions.

The standard does not require every organization to have a separate communication department.

The communication arrangements should be **appropriate to the organization's context and needs**.

---

# 10. Does Clause 7.4 Require Documentation?

An important point:

> **Clause 7.4 does not specifically require the organization to maintain a separate documented communication procedure.**

The organization must determine its communication requirements, but it has flexibility in how these arrangements are established.

Communication rules can be:

### Formal

A dedicated:

- ISMS Communication Procedure
    
- Security Communication Plan
    
- Incident Communication Procedure
    

### Integrated

Communication requirements can instead be included in:

- Information Security Policy
    
- Incident Response Plan
    
- Business Continuity Plan
    
- Crisis Management Plan
    
- Supplier Security Procedure
    
- Communication Policy
    

### Simple

For a small organization, responsibilities may be defined in a few clear statements within existing policies and procedures.

# 11. Communication Matrix – Good Practice

Although not necessarily required as a separate document, a **communication matrix** can be very useful.

|Information|Audience|Frequency/Trigger|Responsible|Channel|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|IS Policy|Employees|On approval/change|CISO|Email/Intranet|
|Security Objectives|Management|Periodic|CISO|Management meeting|
|Security Incidents|Management/Relevant teams|As required|Incident Manager|Incident system|
|Major Breach|Regulators/customers|As legally required|Legal/Compliance|Approved channel|
|Risk Status|Risk owners/Management|Periodic|Risk Manager|Risk report|
|Security Awareness|Employees|Periodic|Security/HR|Training platform|
|Audit Results|Management|After audit|Internal Auditor|Audit report|

This makes communication responsibilities clear and reduces confusion.

---

# 12. Communication and Information Classification

Communication must also consider the sensitivity of the information.

For example:

### Public Information

May be communicated externally.

### Internal Information

May be restricted to employees.

### Confidential Information

Should only be provided to authorized recipients.

### Highly Sensitive Security Information

Should be shared strictly on a need-to-know basis.

For example, detailed vulnerability information about a critical production system should not automatically be distributed to all employees.

Therefore:

> **Effective communication does not mean communicating everything to everyone.**

It means communicating the **right information to the right audience**.

# 13. Communication During Security Incidents

For SOC and incident-response environments, Clause 7.4 is particularly relevant.

A communication structure could be:

```text
Detection
   ↓
SOC Analyst
   ↓
Incident Escalation
   ↓
Incident Response Team
   ↓
CISO / Management
   ↓
Legal / Compliance
   ↓
Customers / Regulators
   ↓
Public Communication, if authorized
```

This should be supported by clearly defined:

- Escalation criteria
    
- Communication responsibilities
    
- Contact information
    
- Notification timelines
    
- Approval requirements
    
- Communication channels
    

---

# 14. Communication vs Awareness

Do not confuse **Clause 7.3 – Awareness** with **Clause 7.4 – Communication**.

|Awareness – 7.3|Communication – 7.4|
|---|---|
|Ensures people understand security|Ensures security information is communicated effectively|
|Focuses on understanding and responsibilities|Focuses on what, when, who, and to whom|
|Example: phishing awareness training|Example: communicating a phishing incident to management|
|"Do people understand?"|"Who needs to know what, when, and from whom?"|

They work together.

### Example

**Awareness:**

Employees learn how to recognize phishing.

**Communication:**

Employees know where to report a suspected phishing email, and the SOC knows how to communicate significant phishing incidents to management.

# 16. Key Points to Remember

### Clause 7.4 requires the organization to determine:

**What → When → Who → To Whom**

And practically:

**How → Through which channel**

### Important points:

- Communication can be internal or external.
    
- Security communication responsibilities should be clear.
    
- Different information may require different recipients.
    
- Sensitive information must be appropriately controlled.
    
- Incident communication should be clearly defined.
    
- The CISO may communicate security matters, but external communications may belong to legal, compliance, PR, or another authorized function.
    
- Small organizations may use a simpler communication structure.
    
- A separate communication procedure is not specifically required.
    
- Communication arrangements can be included in existing policies, procedures, and plans.
    

---
### Easy memory:

> **7.4 = "What, When, Who, To Whom?"**
