
**A.7 Physical Controls** contains **14 controls** and focuses on protecting information, systems, equipment, and facilities from **unauthorized physical access, damage, loss, and environmental threats**.

It has two main areas:

1. **Physical areas / facilities**
    
2. **Equipment**
    

### 1. Securing Physical Areas

The objective is to prevent unauthorized people from accessing sensitive areas and to protect facilities from physical and environmental threats.

Examples:

- Restrict public access to offices and facilities.
    
- Restrict access to sensitive areas such as **data centers/server rooms**.
    
- Use physical entry controls such as:
    
    - Keys
        
    - Smart cards
        
    - Access badges
        
    - Biometric authentication
        
- Use reception/visitor management for external visitors.
    
- Monitor secure areas using **CCTV, access logs, or other monitoring mechanisms**.
    
- Protect facilities against:
    
    - Fire
        
    - Flood
        
    - Earthquake
        
    - Power failure
        
    - Malicious attacks
        
    - Other environmental threats
        

### A.7.4 — Physical Security Monitoring

**A.7.4 Physical Security Monitoring** requires appropriate physical security monitoring to detect unauthorized physical access.

Examples:

- CCTV
    
- Access-control logs
    
- Security guards
    
- Alarm systems
    
- Monitoring of entry/exit points
    

**Important:** A.7.4 was introduced as a **new physical control in ISO/IEC 27001:2022** compared with the 2013 Annex A structure.

## 2. Securing Equipment

The objective is to prevent equipment from being **lost, damaged, stolen, compromised, or malfunctioning**.

Examples:

### Equipment placement

Place equipment appropriately to reduce risks.

**Example:** Don't position servers or computers where water leakage could damage them.

### Power protection

Protect equipment against power interruptions and fluctuations.

Examples:

- UPS
    
- Power generator
    
- Surge protection
    

### Cable protection

Protect power and network cables from:

- Damage
    
- Unauthorized access
    
- Interception
    
- Accidental disconnection
    

**Example:** Use protected cable channels instead of exposed cables across office floors.

### Equipment maintenance

Perform regular maintenance to ensure equipment remains reliable and secure.

### Off-premises equipment

Establish rules for protecting company equipment when it leaves company premises.

**Example:** Requirements for employees carrying company laptops while working remotely.

### Clear desk and clear screen

Organizations should establish rules to prevent sensitive information from being exposed.

Examples:

- Lock the computer when leaving the workstation.
    
- Log out when appropriate.
    
- Don't leave confidential documents unattended.
    
- Secure sensitive paper documents.
    
- Don't leave passwords or sensitive information visible on screens.
    

### Removable media

Define procedures for:

- Using removable media
    
- Protecting removable media
    
- Controlling access
    
- Secure disposal
    

### Secure areas

Employees working in restricted areas should follow specific security procedures to prevent unauthorized access or accidental damage.

---

## Why A.7 is Important

A common mistake is to think **information security = cybersecurity/IT security**.

Physical security is equally important.

For example:

> A laptop may have strong authentication and encryption, but if it is left unlocked and unattended in an area accessible to unauthorized people, the organization can still suffer a security incident.

Therefore:

**Strong technical security + weak physical security = overall security weakness**

### Simple A.7 structure

**Physical Controls**

→ **Protect facilities**  
→ **Control physical access**  
→ **Monitor secure areas**  
→ **Protect against environmental threats**  
→ **Protect equipment**  
→ **Protect cables and power**  
→ **Maintain equipment**  
→ **Protect off-premises assets**  
→ **Clear desk / clear screen**  
→ **Control removable media**
