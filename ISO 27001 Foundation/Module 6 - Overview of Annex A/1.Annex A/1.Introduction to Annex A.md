

## ISO 27001 Annex A — 93 Security Controls

**ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A** provides a **catalog of 93 information security controls**, grouped into four categories.

|Annex A|Category|No. of Controls|Main Focus|
|---|---|--:|---|
|**A.5**|Organizational controls|**37**|Policies, governance, assets, access, suppliers, incidents, continuity|
|**A.6**|People controls|**8**|Employees, training, awareness, responsibilities|
|**A.7**|Physical controls|**14**|Physical security, secure areas, equipment|
|**A.8**|Technological controls|**34**|IT systems, access, malware, backup, logging, development|

**Total = 37 + 8 + 14 + 34 = 93 controls**

### 1. A.5 — Organizational Controls

These controls focus on **how the organization manages information security**.

Examples:

- Information security policies
    
- Roles and responsibilities
    
- Segregation of duties
    
- Asset management
    
- Access control
    
- Threat intelligence
    
- Information classification
    
- Supplier security
    
- Incident management
    
- Business continuity
    
- Legal and regulatory requirements
    

**Example:** Establish rules defining who is responsible for information security and who can approve security-related decisions.

---

### 2. A.6 — People Controls

These controls focus on **employees and other people working for the organization**.

Examples:

- Screening
    
- Terms and conditions of employment
    
- Security awareness and training
    
- Disciplinary process
    
- Responsibilities after termination
    
- Remote working
    
- Reporting information security events
    

**Example:** Employees receive security awareness training and understand their responsibilities under the ISMS.

---

### 3. A.7 — Physical Controls

These controls protect information and systems against **physical threats**.

Examples:

- Physical security perimeters
    
- Physical entry controls
    
- Securing offices and facilities
    
- Physical monitoring
    
- Protection against environmental threats
    
- Equipment security
    
- Secure disposal of equipment
    
- Clear desk and clear screen
    

**Example:** A server room may use restricted access, CCTV, fire protection, and environmental monitoring.

---

### 4. A.8 — Technological Controls

These controls focus on **technical protection of information and IT systems**.

Examples:

- Endpoint security
    
- Access control
    
- Authentication
    
- Malware protection
    
- Backup
    
- Logging
    
- Monitoring
    
- Network security
    
- Cryptography
    
- Secure development
    
- Vulnerability management
    
- Data leakage prevention
    

**Example:** A.8.13 **Information Backup** requires information, software, and systems to be backed up and backups to be tested appropriately.

---

## Are all 93 controls mandatory?

**No.**

This is one of the most important points for ISO 27001 interviews.

The organization determines which controls are **applicable** based on:

1. Information security risk assessment
    
2. Risk treatment decisions
    
3. Business requirements
    
4. Legal/regulatory requirements
    
5. Contractual requirements
    
6. Requirements of interested parties
    
7. Management decisions
    

The applicable controls are documented in the **Statement of Applicability (SoA)**.

### Important distinction

> **Annex A is a reference control set, not a checklist where every control must automatically be implemented.**

A control can be excluded if it is genuinely not applicable, but the organization must provide an appropriate **justification for exclusion in the SoA**.

---

## Does ISO 27001 tell you exactly how to implement each control?

**No.**

ISO 27001 generally defines **what needs to be achieved**, rather than prescribing a specific technology or implementation.

For example, backup requirements do not mean:

> "You must use Product X and perform backups every 4 hours."

Instead, the organization determines the appropriate backup approach based on its **risks and business requirements**.

For a critical banking system, this could involve frequent backups, redundancy, replication, and extensive recovery testing.

For a low-risk system, a simpler backup solution may be sufficient.

## ISO 27001 vs ISO 27002

|ISO 27001|ISO 27002|
|---|---|
|ISMS requirements|Control implementation guidance|
|Used for certification|Not used for certification|
|Contains Annex A control set|Provides more detailed guidance for controls|
|Defines requirements for an ISMS|Explains possible ways to implement controls|
|**Certifiable**|**Not certifiable**|

**Key interview statement:**

> **ISO 27001 tells an organization what requirements it must meet to establish and operate an ISMS, while ISO 27002 provides guidance on implementing information security controls.**

## How Annex A fits into Risk Management

The overall flow is:

**Risk Identification → Risk Analysis → Risk Evaluation → Risk Treatment → Select Controls → Statement of Applicability → Risk Treatment Plan → Implement Controls**

For example:

**Risk:** Confidential customer information may be exposed.

⬇️

**Risk treatment:** Reduce the risk.

⬇️

**Control:** Select appropriate access control, encryption, classification, etc.

⬇️

**SoA:** Document why the controls are applicable and how they are implemented.

⬇️

**Risk Treatment Plan:** Define who will implement them, resources, deadlines, and status.
