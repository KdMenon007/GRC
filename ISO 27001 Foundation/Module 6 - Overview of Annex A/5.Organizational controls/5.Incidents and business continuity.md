
These controls focus on how an organization **prepares for, detects, responds to, and learns from security threats, incidents, and major disruptions**.

They are important because effective preparation can:

- Reduce the likelihood of incidents.
    
- Reduce the impact of incidents.
    
- Improve response and recovery.
    
- Reduce business downtime and financial losses.
    
- Protect the **confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA)** of information.
    

---

### 1. Basic Security Terms

#### Weakness

A **flaw or weak point** in an information system, process, or service.

**Example:** Inadequate security controls against cyberattacks.

#### Threat

An activity or event that could negatively affect the **confidentiality, integrity, or availability** of information.

**Example:** An attacker planning a ransomware attack.

#### Event

An identified occurrence in an information system, service, or network that may indicate a security issue.

**Example:** An unsuccessful login attack.

#### Incident

One or more unwanted or unexpected events that **actually compromise or could compromise** information security.

**Example:** A successful ransomware attack encrypting company systems.

#### Disruption

A **major incident** that significantly affects normal business operations.

**Example:** A cyberattack taking critical IT systems offline for several days.

## 2. Threat Management

The organization should identify threats and take proactive measures to reduce their impact.

### A.5.7 — Threat Intelligence

The 2022 revision introduced **A.5.7 Threat Intelligence**.

The purpose is to:

- Collect relevant information about threats.
    
- Monitor current security threats.
    
- Analyze threat information.
    
- Identify potential risks to the organization.
    
- Proactively implement security measures.
    

### Example

If threat intelligence shows that attackers are exploiting a new vulnerability in a particular VPN product, the organization can:

1. Check whether it uses the affected product.
    
2. Determine whether the vulnerability applies.
    
3. Apply the required patch.
    
4. Implement additional security controls.
    
5. Monitor for exploitation attempts.
    

**Key idea:**

> Threat intelligence helps the organization act **before a threat becomes an incident**.

---

## 3. Information Security Event and Incident Management

Organizations should establish processes for detecting, reporting, assessing, and responding to security events and incidents.

The organization should define:

- How incidents are reported.
    
- Who is responsible for handling incidents.
    
- How incidents are categorized.
    
- How incidents are prioritized.
    
- How incidents are escalated.
    
- How incidents are investigated.
    
- How evidence is collected.
    
- How incidents are documented.
    
- How incidents are resolved.
    
- How lessons learned are identified.
    

---

## 4. Incident Classification

The organization should establish criteria for determining whether an event becomes an incident.

Classification can consider:

- Business impact
    
- Data sensitivity
    
- Number of affected users
    
- System criticality
    
- Financial impact
    
- Availability impact
    
- Confidentiality impact
    
- Integrity impact
    

### Example

A failed login attempt may be treated as a normal **security event**.

Multiple successful unauthorized logins to a privileged account may be classified as a **security incident**.

---

## 5. Incident Response

The organization should establish appropriate **incident response plans**.

Different incidents may require different response procedures.

### Example

**Ransomware:**

- Isolate affected systems.
    
- Investigate the attack.
    
- Preserve evidence.
    
- Identify affected systems.
    
- Recover from clean backups.
    
- Monitor for further compromise.
    

**Earthquake:**

- Ensure employee safety.
    
- Activate business continuity procedures.
    
- Move operations to an alternate location.
    
- Recover critical IT services.
    

---

## 6. Incident Logging and Evidence

Security incidents should be properly recorded and evidence should be collected.

Records may include:

- Date and time
    
- Incident description
    
- Affected systems
    
- Affected users
    
- Detection method
    
- Actions performed
    
- Evidence collected
    
- Root cause
    
- Recovery actions
    
- Final resolution
    

Evidence may be required for:

- Internal investigation
    
- Legal proceedings
    
- Regulatory reporting
    
- Insurance claims
    
- Root-cause analysis
    

---

## 7. Lessons Learned

After an incident, the organization should determine:

- What happened?
    
- Why did it happen?
    
- Which controls failed?
    
- What worked well?
    
- What could be improved?
    
- What changes are required?
    
- How can similar incidents be prevented?
    

### Example

If ransomware entered through an unpatched server, the organization may improve:

- Vulnerability management
    
- Patch management
    
- Network segmentation
    
- Endpoint protection
    
- Monitoring
    
- Security awareness
    

The objective is to **learn from incidents and improve the ISMS**.

# Business Continuity

Information security should continue even when a major disruption occurs.

The organization should identify critical activities and prepare plans to maintain or restore them.

### Business continuity planning may include:

- Identifying critical business processes.
    
- Identifying critical information and systems.
    
- Defining recovery requirements.
    
- Preparing disaster recovery plans.
    
- Establishing alternate locations.
    
- Preparing backup systems.
    
- Identifying replacement personnel.
    
- Establishing alternative suppliers.
    
- Testing and exercising recovery plans.
    

---

## A.5.30 — ICT Readiness for Business Continuity

**A.5.30 ICT Readiness for Business Continuity** is a new control introduced in the **2022 revision**.

Its purpose is to ensure that ICT systems and resources are prepared to support business continuity.

Examples include:

- Secondary/alternate IT locations.
    
- Redundant systems.
    
- Backup infrastructure.
    
- Replacement equipment.
    
- Alternative service providers.
    
- Replacement personnel.
    
- Disaster recovery capabilities.
    
- Recovery procedures.
    

### Example

If the primary data center becomes unavailable, the organization should have an alternative way to recover critical IT services.

## Incident Management Flow

**Threat → Event → Incident → Major Disruption → Recovery → Lessons Learned → Improvement**

Example:

**New ransomware threat**  
↓  
Suspicious endpoint activity detected  
↓  
Ransomware infection confirmed  
↓  
Critical systems become unavailable  
↓  
Incident response + disaster recovery activated  
↓  
Systems restored from clean backups  
↓  
Root cause analyzed  
↓  
Security controls improved

## Key Takeaways

|Area|Main Purpose|
|---|---|
|**Threat Intelligence**|Identify and proactively address threats|
|**Event Management**|Detect and assess security events|
|**Incident Management**|Respond to and control security incidents|
|**Evidence Management**|Preserve information for investigation/legal needs|
|**Lessons Learned**|Improve security after incidents|
|**Business Continuity**|Maintain critical operations during disruption|
|**ICT Readiness**|Ensure IT can support recovery and continuity|
