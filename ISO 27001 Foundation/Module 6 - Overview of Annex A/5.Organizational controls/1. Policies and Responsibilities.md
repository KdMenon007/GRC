
**Purpose:**  
A.5 establishes the organizational framework needed to manage information security. It defines **policies, roles, responsibilities, segregation of duties, external contacts, and security requirements within projects**.

There are **37 organizational controls** in ISO/IEC 27001:2022.

### A.5.1 Information Security Policies

**Purpose:**  
Information security policies establish the organization's rules and requirements for managing and protecting information security.

**Key points:**

- Annex A.5 covers organizational controls.
    
- Information security controls are often implemented through **policies and procedures**.
    
- Organizations can create **topic-specific information security policies**.
    
- Policies can be maintained separately or combined into fewer documents.
    
- Smaller organizations may have fewer and simpler policies.
    
- Larger organizations may have more numerous and complex policies.
    
- Information security documents should be **regularly reviewed**.
    
- Policies should especially be reviewed when there are **changes in the organization**.
    

**Examples of policies:**

- Access Control Policy
    
- Backup Policy
    
- Information Classification Policy
    
- Teleworking Policy
    
- Encryption Policy
    
- Clear Desk and Clear Screen Policy
    

**Implementation example:**

An organization can establish an **Access Control Policy** defining how access rights are managed, while a **Backup Policy** defines the organization's requirements for protecting and backing up information.

**Key takeaway:**

> Information Security Policies define the organization's security rules and provide a structured approach for implementing information security controls.

### A.5.2 Roles and Responsibilities

**Purpose:**  
To clearly define **who is responsible for what** in information security.

**Key points:**

- Annex A.5 defines security **roles and responsibilities**.
    
- Responsibilities can be defined through:
    
    - Policies
        
    - Procedures
        
    - Responsibility matrices
        
- Roles and responsibilities should be clearly defined at **all levels of the organization**.
    
- Clearly defined responsibilities prevent confusion about who should perform particular security activities.
    
- Without clearly defined responsibilities, an organization may have many security technologies and controls but lack clarity about:
    
    - Who needs to perform an activity
        
    - Who is responsible for an activity
        
    - Who manages a particular security responsibility
        
- Lack of clearly defined responsibilities can significantly reduce the organization's security level.
    

**Implementation example:**

An organization can create a **responsibility matrix** that clearly assigns information security responsibilities to different employees or functions.

**Key takeaway:**

> Roles and Responsibilities ensure that everyone understands **who needs to do what and who is responsible for what** within information security.
