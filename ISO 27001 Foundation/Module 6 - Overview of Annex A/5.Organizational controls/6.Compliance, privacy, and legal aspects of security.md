
This group of **organizational controls** focuses on ensuring that information security complies with:

- Laws and regulations
    
- Contractual requirements
    
- Customer requirements
    
- Partner requirements
    
- Intellectual property requirements
    
- Privacy requirements
    
- Internal ISMS policies and standards
    

The main purpose is to **avoid legal violations, fines, contractual penalties, data breaches, and other losses**.

---

## 1. Identify Legal, Regulatory & Contractual Requirements

The organization should identify all requirements applicable to its information security activities.

These may include:

- Laws and regulations
    
- Industry regulations
    
- Customer contracts
    
- Supplier agreements
    
- Partner requirements
    
- Privacy requirements
    
- Internal security requirements
    

The organization should maintain a **compliance requirements register** containing:

- Requirement
    
- Applicable law/regulation/contract
    
- Responsible person
    
- Applicability
    
- Compliance status
    
- Review date
    

### Example

A customer contract may require:

> Customer data must be encrypted during transmission.

The organization should identify this requirement, assign an owner, and verify that the required encryption is actually implemented.

---

## 2. Assign Compliance Responsibilities

The organization should define **who is responsible for monitoring and fulfilling each requirement**.

Responsibilities may be assigned to:

- CISO
    
- Legal team
    
- Compliance team
    
- DPO/Privacy team
    
- IT team
    
- HR
    
- Business process owners
    

### CISO Role

The **CISO** may coordinate information-security compliance by:

- Identifying security-related requirements.
    
- Working with legal and compliance teams.
    
- Monitoring compliance with security requirements.
    
- Reviewing security policies against legal requirements.
    
- Coordinating remediation of compliance gaps.
    
- Reporting significant compliance issues to management.
    

---

## 3. Intellectual Property Rights

The organization should protect and comply with **intellectual property (IP) rights**.

This includes:

- Using legally licensed software.
    
- Managing software licenses.
    
- Preventing unauthorized copying.
    
- Protecting copyrighted materials.
    
- Protecting proprietary documents.
    
- Protecting images, designs, code, and other intellectual property.
    

### Example

A company should maintain a software license inventory and ensure that employees do not install unauthorized or pirated software.

---

## 4. Protection of Records

Records must be protected according to:

- Legal requirements
    
- Regulatory requirements
    
- Contractual requirements
    
- Business requirements
    

The organization should determine:

- What records must be retained.
    
- How long records must be retained.
    
- Who can access them.
    
- How they are protected.
    
- When and how they can be securely disposed of.
    

### Example

If a regulation requires a particular record to be retained for **10 years**, the organization must ensure that the record remains available and protected for the required period.

---

## 5. Privacy & Personally Identifiable Information (PII)

Organizations must protect **personal data/PII** according to applicable privacy laws and regulations.

Examples of personal information include:

- Name
    
- Address
    
- Phone number
    
- Email address
    
- Identification information
    
- Financial information
    
- Other information that can identify an individual
    

Privacy requirements may include requirements for:

- Access control
    
- Encryption
    
- Data retention
    
- Data deletion
    
- Data minimization
    
- Secure processing
    
- Data breach handling
    

### Example

Under **GDPR Article 32**, organizations are required to implement appropriate technical and organizational measures to protect personal data.

---

## 6. Independent Review of Information Security

The organization should periodically review information security to determine whether it complies with:

- Legal requirements
    
- Regulatory requirements
    
- Contractual requirements
    
- ISO 27001 requirements
    
- Internal policies
    
- Security standards
    

Reviews can be performed through:

- Internal audits
    
- External audits
    
- Certification audits
    
- Compliance assessments
    
- Independent security reviews
    

Reviews should be performed at **planned intervals** and when significant changes occur.

---

## 7. Compliance with Security Policies and Standards

Management should regularly review whether employees and other relevant personnel are following the organization's security requirements.

Reviews can check:

- Policy compliance
    
- Access control compliance
    
- Password/security requirements
    
- Data handling
    
- Security procedures
    
- Use of company assets
    
- Incident reporting
    
- Remote-working requirements
    

### Example

A manager may periodically review whether employees are following the organization's **Acceptable Use Policy** and report any violations.

## Key Takeaways

|Area|Main Purpose|
|---|---|
|**Legal & Regulatory Requirements**|Comply with applicable laws and regulations|
|**Contractual Requirements**|Meet customer/partner obligations|
|**Intellectual Property**|Protect and legally use IP and software|
|**Records Protection**|Retain and protect required records|
|**Privacy & PII**|Protect personal information|
|**Independent Review**|Verify security and compliance|
|**Policy Compliance**|Ensure employees follow security requirements|

### CISO Perspective

The **CISO's key responsibility** is to ensure that information-security requirements are translated into practical security controls and that compliance gaps are identified, tracked, and reported to management. The CISO normally works closely with **Legal, Compliance, Privacy/DPO, IT, HR, and business owners** because legal and privacy compliance is not solely an IT responsibility.
