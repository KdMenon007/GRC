
**Clause 8.2 — Information Security Risk Assessment** requires the organization to perform information security risk assessments **at planned intervals** and whenever **significant changes are proposed or occur**.

The purpose is to ensure that the organization's understanding of its information-security risks remains **current and relevant**.

---

### 1. Why is risk assessment included in Clause 8?

Risk assessment is initially performed during the **Plan phase** to establish the ISMS and decide how risks will be treated.

But risks do not remain constant.

Business operations, technology, threats, vulnerabilities, suppliers, and regulations can change.

Therefore:

> **Risk assessment is not a one-time activity. It must be repeated during ISMS operation.**

---

## 2. Conduct risk assessments at planned intervals

The organization should define when risk assessments will be performed.

A common practice is:

> **At least once a year.**

However, ISO 27001 does **not simply prescribe "once every year" as a universal fixed interval**. The organization should determine an appropriate frequency based on its circumstances and risk environment.

For example:

|Organization|Possible approach|
|---|---|
|Small low-risk company|Annual review|
|Financial organization|More frequent reviews|
|High-threat environment|Quarterly or more frequent|
|Rapidly changing technology environment|Frequent reassessment|

The important point is that the organization establishes **planned intervals** and follows them.

---

## 3. Review previous risk assessments

The organization does not necessarily need to start from zero every time.

During a periodic review, the organization can take the existing risk register and ask:

- Is this risk still relevant?
    
- Has the likelihood changed?
    
- Has the impact changed?
    
- Has the threat changed?
    
- Has the vulnerability been fixed?
    
- Is the existing control still effective?
    
- Has the risk owner changed?
    
- Has the risk treatment changed?
    
- Are there any new risks?
    

This makes subsequent assessments much more efficient.

---

## 4. Reassess risks when major changes occur

This is one of the most important points in Clause 8.2.

The organization should perform a risk assessment when **significant changes are planned or occur**.

### Example: Moving to the cloud

Suppose a company previously hosted its customer database on its own servers.

Now management decides:

> "We are going to move the customer database to a cloud service."

This major change can introduce new risks.

For example:

- Cloud misconfiguration
    
- Unauthorized cloud access
    
- Supplier failure
    
- Data exposure
    
- Incorrect identity permissions
    
- Data-location concerns
    
- Service availability problems
    
- Dependency on the cloud provider
    
- Inadequate backup/recovery arrangements
    

The organization should therefore reassess its risks.

---

## 5. What should happen during the reassessment?

A practical process is:

**Identify changes → Identify new risks → Reassess existing risks → Evaluate risks → Update treatment → Document results**

For example:

### Before cloud migration

**Risk:** Server hardware failure

**Impact:** High

**Likelihood:** Medium

**Treatment:** On-site backup and hardware redundancy

### After cloud migration

The original risk may change, but new risks may appear:

**Risk:** Cloud service outage

**Impact:** High

**Likelihood:** Medium

**Treatment:** Business continuity planning, backup strategy, recovery procedures, etc.

The risk register should therefore be updated.

---

## 6. Why is regular reassessment important?

Because the risk environment changes continuously.

### Internal changes

- New applications
    
- New employees
    
- New processes
    
- New offices
    
- Organizational restructuring
    
- New technologies
    
- New suppliers
    

### External changes

- New cyber threats
    
- New vulnerabilities
    
- Changes in laws
    
- Regulatory changes
    
- Changes in the threat landscape
    
- Changes in suppliers or markets
    

A risk assessment performed several years ago may no longer accurately represent the organization's current risk profile.

---

## 7. Documentation

ISO 27001 requires the **results of the information security risk assessment to be documented**.

A practical organization can maintain a version-controlled risk register.

For example:

**Risk Register v1.0 — January 2026**

Then after the annual review:

**Risk Register v2.0 — January 2027**

The updated version can show:

- New risks
    
- Removed risks
    
- Changed risk ratings
    
- Changed risk owners
    
- Updated controls
    
- New treatment actions
    
- Closed risks
    

This creates traceability and allows the organization to demonstrate how its risk profile has changed.

---

## 8. Important distinction: Clause 6.1.2 vs Clause 8.2

This is useful for understanding the ISO 27001 structure.

### Clause 6.1.2 — Risk assessment

**Plan phase**

> Establish and perform the organization's risk assessment methodology and initial risk assessment.

### Clause 8.2 — Risk assessment

**Do phase**

> Perform risk assessments at planned intervals and when significant changes occur.

So:

**Clause 6.1.2 → Establish/perform risk assessment as part of planning**

**Clause 8.2 → Continue performing risk assessments during operation**

---

### Simple example

A company performs its initial risk assessment in January.

During the year:

- It launches a new application.
    
- It moves a database to the cloud.
    
- It changes its main IT supplier.
    

The company should not wait until the next annual review if these changes significantly affect information security.

It should reassess the relevant risks **when the major changes occur**.

---

### Easy memory

**Clause 8.2 = Keep reassessing the risks.**

> **Regularly + After major changes + Document the results**
