
### 1. What does Clause 8.1 mean?

In simple terms:

> **Clause 8.1 means putting the plans created during the Plan phase into actual operation and making sure those activities are performed as planned.**

During the Plan phase, the organization identifies:

- Information security risks
    
- Risk treatment options
    
- Applicable controls
    
- Information security objectives
    
- Actions for risks and opportunities
    
- Required resources and responsibilities
    

Clause 8.1 ensures that these plans are **actually executed and controlled**.

---

### 2. What does the organization need to do?

The organization must establish and control the processes required to meet its information security requirements.

This includes:

#### A. Implement the planned actions

The organization must carry out the actions identified during planning.

For example:

**Risk:** Customer database could be lost due to hardware failure.

**Treatment:** Implement regular backups.

**Clause 8.1 implementation:**

- Create a backup policy.
    
- Configure backup software.
    
- Schedule daily backups.
    
- Store backups securely.
    
- Restrict access to backups.
    
- Monitor backup jobs.
    
- Test restoration periodically.
    

The important point is that the organization must **actually perform the planned activities**, not just document them.

---

### 3. Implement the risk treatment plan

One of the most important activities under operational planning and control is implementing the **risk treatment plan**.

For example:

|Risk|Selected Control|Operational Action|
|---|---|---|
|Server failure|Backup|Configure daily backups|
|Unauthorized access|Access control|Implement MFA|
|Malware infection|Malware protection|Deploy endpoint protection|
|Data disclosure|Encryption|Encrypt sensitive data|
|Power failure|Physical/environmental protection|Install UPS|

The risk treatment plan tells the organization **what needs to be implemented**, while Clause 8.1 ensures that these activities are **carried out and controlled in practice**.

---

### 4. Control operational processes

The organization should not simply implement controls and forget about them.

It must establish criteria for how important information-security processes should operate and ensure that those processes are performed according to those criteria.

For example, if the organization requires:

> "Critical servers must be backed up every day."

Then the organization should have an operational process to:

1. Schedule the backup.
    
2. Execute the backup.
    
3. Monitor whether it succeeded.
    
4. Investigate failed backups.
    
5. Maintain backup records.
    
6. Periodically test restoration.
    

This demonstrates that the control is **operational**, rather than merely existing on paper.

---

### 5. Control planned changes

Operational planning also involves managing changes that could affect information security.

For example, suppose an organization plans to migrate its customer database to a new cloud platform.

Before implementing the change, it should consider:

- Security requirements
    
- Access controls
    
- Encryption
    
- Backup requirements
    
- Logging and monitoring
    
- Data protection requirements
    
- Business continuity
    
- Risks introduced by the change
    

The change should therefore be **planned and controlled**, rather than implemented without considering its security impact.

---

### 6. Control outsourced processes

If an organization outsources an information-security-related process, it still needs to control that process.

For example, a company may outsource:

- Cloud hosting
    
- Security monitoring
    
- Backup services
    
- Data-center operations
    
- Vulnerability scanning
    

The organization should establish appropriate requirements for the external provider and monitor whether those requirements are being met.

**Important:** Outsourcing a process does **not automatically remove the organization's responsibility for managing its ISMS requirements**.

---

### 7. Keep evidence of operation

The organization should maintain appropriate **documented information** to demonstrate that planned processes and controls have been carried out as intended.

Examples include:

- Backup logs
    
- Access review records
    
- Vulnerability scan reports
    
- Security monitoring records
    
- Incident records
    
- Change records
    
- Training records
    
- Configuration records
    
- Risk treatment status
    
- Control implementation evidence
    

The exact amount of documentation depends on the organization's needs.

---

## Example: Backup Control under Clause 8.1

Imagine the risk assessment identifies:

**Risk:** Loss of customer information due to server failure.

**Risk treatment:** Implement backups.

### Plan phase

The organization decides:

> Customer database must be backed up twice per day.

A backup control is selected and included in the risk treatment plan.

### Do phase — Clause 8.1

The organization now:

- Deploys backup software.
    
- Configures two daily backup jobs.
    
- Allocates backup storage.
    
- Restricts access to backup data.
    
- Monitors backup success/failure.
    
- Investigates failed backups.
    
- Performs restoration tests.
    

### Evidence

The organization can demonstrate:

- Backup configuration
    
- Backup logs
    
- Restoration test results
    
- Backup policy
    
- Monitoring records
    

This shows that the organization has **operationally implemented the planned control**.

## Clause 8.1 and PDCA

Think of it like this:

**Plan — Clauses 4–7**

> What do we need to protect, what are the risks, and what are we going to do?

⬇️

**Do — Clause 8.1**

> **Actually implement and operate what we planned.**

⬇️

**Check — Clause 9**

> Is it working? Are the controls effective?

⬇️

**Act — Clause 10**

> What needs to be corrected or improved?

### Key point for exams/interviews

> **ISO 27001 Clause 8.1, Operational Planning and Control, requires the organization to plan, implement, and control the processes necessary to meet information security requirements, implement the actions identified during planning, and control planned changes and relevant outsourced processes.**

**Easy memory:**  
**Clause 8.1 = "Do what you planned, control how you do it, and keep it operating as intended."**
