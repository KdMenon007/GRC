
This part of **Clause 8.1 — Operational Planning and Control** is about ensuring that when an organization **outsources a process to another company**, the information-security requirements are still controlled.

### 1. Why is this important?

An organization may outsource important activities such as:

- Cloud hosting
    
- IT infrastructure management
    
- SOC/SIEM monitoring
    
- Payroll and HR services
    
- Recruitment
    
- Data-center operations
    
- Backup services
    
- Software development
    
- Managed security services
    

Even though another company performs the activity, the outsourced process can still affect the organization's **confidentiality, integrity, and availability (CIA)**.

For example:

> A company outsources recruitment and provides candidate personal information to the recruitment company.

If the recruitment company does not protect that information properly, there could be a confidentiality breach.

Therefore, the organization needs to **identify and control outsourced processes from an information-security perspective**.

---

## 2. What does the organization need to do?

A practical approach is:

**Identify → Define requirements → Communicate → Agree → Monitor → Review**

### Step 1 — Identify outsourced processes

First, determine which processes are outsourced and could affect information security.

Example:

> Recruitment is outsourced to ABC Recruitment.

The organization should ask:

- What information does ABC receive?
    
- What systems can they access?
    
- What personal/confidential information is involved?
    
- What security risks exist?
    
- What security requirements must they follow?
    

---

### Step 2 — Define security requirements

The organization should establish the security requirements that the supplier must follow.

For recruitment, requirements might include:

- Background checks
    
- NDA/confidentiality agreements
    
- Secure handling of candidate information
    
- Restricted access to candidate data
    
- Secure transmission of documents
    
- Data retention requirements
    
- Secure deletion
    
- Incident reporting
    
- Access removal when employees leave
    

---

### Step 3 — Communicate the requirements

The organization must make sure the outsourced provider understands the security requirements.

For example:

> The design company gives the recruitment company its HR security requirements and explains how candidate information must be handled.

The supplier should understand **what is required and why**.

---

### Step 4 — Include requirements in the agreement

A strong practical approach is to include information-security requirements in the contract or service agreement.

For example:

> "The recruitment provider shall protect all candidate information, restrict access to authorized personnel, maintain confidentiality, report security incidents, and securely delete information according to the agreed retention period."

This gives the organization a contractual basis for controlling the outsourced activity.

---

### Step 5 — Monitor the outsourced process

Simply giving the supplier a policy is not enough.

The organization should determine whether the supplier is actually meeting the security requirements.

Depending on the risk, this could involve:

- Supplier reviews
    
- Security questionnaires
    
- Audit reports
    
- Certifications
    
- Performance reviews
    
- Incident reporting
    
- Periodic assessments
    

For a high-risk cloud provider, much stronger monitoring may be appropriate than for a low-risk supplier.

---

### Step 6 — Review when circumstances change

The organization should reassess outsourced processes when there are significant changes.

For example:

> The recruitment company previously received only candidate names and CVs.

Later, it is given access to a system containing identity documents and salary information.

The security risk has increased, so the organization should review the security requirements and controls.

---

## Example: Outsourced Recruitment

### Situation

A design company outsources employee recruitment.

### Information involved

- CVs
    
- Names
    
- Contact details
    
- Identification documents
    
- Employment history
    
- Background-check information
    

### Risks

|Risk|Example|
|---|---|
|Confidentiality|Candidate data disclosed|
|Integrity|Candidate information modified|
|Availability|Recruitment data becomes unavailable|

### Controls

The design company requires the recruitment provider to:

- Perform background checks where required.
    
- Sign confidentiality/NDA agreements.
    
- Restrict access to authorized employees.
    
- Protect candidate information.
    
- Securely transmit information.
    
- Report security incidents.
    
- Delete information according to agreed requirements.
    

### Evidence

The design company could maintain:

- Supplier contract
    
- Security requirements
    
- Supplier assessment
    
- Audit/assessment reports
    
- Incident records
    
- Periodic supplier review
    

---

## Relationship with Annex A

This is an important distinction for understanding ISO 27001.

**Clause 8.1** establishes the requirement to **control outsourced processes operationally**.

**Annex A supplier-related controls** provide specific controls that can help manage supplier relationships and information-security requirements.

So they work together:

> **Clause 8.1 → Control outsourced processes**

> **Annex A → Apply appropriate supplier-security controls**

The exact controls selected depend on the organization's risks, requirements, and Statement of Applicability.

---
### Easy way to remember

**Outsource the work, not the responsibility.**

The supplier performs the activity, but the organization must still ensure that **information-security requirements are properly controlled.**
