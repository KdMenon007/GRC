
Clause **10.2** belongs to the **Act phase of the PDCA cycle**. It focuses on identifying **nonconformities**, correcting them, finding their **root causes**, and ensuring they do not happen again.

### What is a Nonconformity?

A **nonconformity** occurs when a requirement is not fulfilled.

The requirement could come from:

- ISO/IEC 27001
    
- Applicable laws and regulations
    
- ISMS policies and procedures
    
- Contractual requirements
    
- Requirements of interested parties
    

**Example:**

The organization's procedure requires internal audits to be performed by competent auditors, but an audit was conducted by an untrained employee.

➡️ This is a **nonconformity**.

---

## Nonconformity vs Corrective Action

### Nonconformity

The **problem or deviation** from a requirement.

### Correction

The immediate action taken to **fix the problem**.

### Corrective Action

The action taken to **eliminate the cause of the nonconformity** and prevent it from happening again.

**Simple example:**

> Nonconformity: Internal audit was performed by an untrained auditor.

> Correction: Train the auditor and conduct the audit again.

> Corrective action: Identify why an untrained person was assigned and establish a process requiring auditor competency verification before assigning an audit.

---

## ISO 27001 Required Steps

When a nonconformity occurs, the organization should:

### 1. Control and Correct the Nonconformity

Immediately address the problem and deal with its consequences.

**Example:**

An internal audit was performed by an untrained auditor.

Actions:

- Stop relying on the invalid audit results.
    
- Train competent auditors.
    
- Conduct the audit again.
    
- Assess whether any risks resulted from the incorrect audit.
    

---

### 2. Determine the Root Cause

The organization should investigate **why the nonconformity happened**.

Don't stop at the immediate problem.

**Example:**

❌ Root cause:

> "The auditor was untrained."

Ask **why**:

> Why was an untrained auditor assigned?

Possible root cause:

> There was no process for verifying auditor competence before assignment.

---

### 3. Determine Whether Similar Nonconformities Exist

The organization should consider whether the same problem exists elsewhere.

For example:

> Are other internal auditors also untrained?

This helps prevent the organization from fixing only one occurrence while leaving the same problem elsewhere.

---

### 4. Implement Corrective Action

Implement actions that address the root cause.

**Example:**

- Define auditor competency requirements.
    
- Train internal auditors.
    
- Maintain training records.
    
- Assign an owner for the audit program.
    
- Verify auditor competence before assigning audits.
    

---

### 5. Review Effectiveness

The organization must verify whether the corrective action **actually worked**.

For example, after several months:

> Check whether all internal audits are now being conducted by competent auditors.

If the problem does not recur, the corrective action can be considered effective.

---

### 6. Make Changes to the ISMS if Necessary

If the nonconformity reveals a weakness in the ISMS, the organization should update it.

**Example:**

Create or update an **Internal Audit Procedure** that defines:

- Auditor competency requirements
    
- Auditor selection
    
- Independence requirements
    
- Responsibilities
    
- Audit planning
    
- Training requirements
    

---

## CISO's Role

The **CISO typically plays an important role in managing nonconformities and corrective actions**, especially those related to information security.

The CISO may:

- Review security-related nonconformities.
    
- Assess their information-security impact.
    
- Coordinate root-cause analysis.
    
- Assign corrective-action owners.
    
- Track corrective actions to completion.
    
- Verify or coordinate effectiveness reviews.
    
- Update policies, procedures, and controls when required.
    
- Report significant nonconformities to top management.
    
- Ensure recurring problems are addressed systematically.
    

**Important:** The CISO does not necessarily have to personally perform every corrective action. Responsibility should be assigned to the appropriate process or control owner.

---

## Documentation

ISO 27001 requires documented information as evidence of:

1. **The nature of the nonconformity**
    
2. **Actions taken**
    
3. **Results of corrective actions**
    

A common practical document is a **Corrective Action Form (CAF)**.

### Example Corrective Action Record

|Field|Example|
|---|---|
|Nonconformity|Internal audit performed by untrained auditor|
|Requirement|Auditor must be competent|
|Immediate correction|Train auditor and repeat audit|
|Root cause|No auditor competency verification process|
|Corrective action|Establish competency verification process|
|Owner|ISMS/Internal Audit Manager|
|Deadline|30 days|
|Effectiveness check|Review next two audits|
|Result|No recurrence|
|Status|Closed|

## CISO Example

Suppose an audit discovers:

> **Nonconformity:** 20% of terminated employees still had active accounts after termination.

The CISO should not simply disable those accounts and close the issue.

Instead:

**Correction:**

- Disable the accounts immediately.
    
- Investigate potential unauthorized access.
    

**Root-cause analysis:**

- Why were accounts not disabled?
    
- Was HR notifying IT?
    
- Was the process unclear?
    
- Was there no automated workflow?
    

**Corrective action:**

- Establish an HR-to-IT termination process.
    
- Define responsibilities.
    
- Implement automated account deprovisioning.
    
- Update the relevant procedure.
    

**Effectiveness review:**

- Check account termination for employees leaving over the next few months.
    
- Confirm that accounts are consistently disabled on time.
    

**ISMS improvement:**

- Update the access-control procedure if necessary.
