
Clause **9.3** is the final part of **Clause 9** and the final activity in the **Check phase of the PDCA cycle**.

The purpose is for **top management to review the ISMS at planned intervals** to ensure that it remains:

- **Suitable** — appropriate for the organization's purpose and context.
    
- **Adequate** — sufficient to meet information security requirements.
    
- **Effective** — achieving the intended information security results.
    

### What does management need to do?

Top management does **not** need to understand technical details such as firewall configuration. Instead, management needs to understand:

> **Is the ISMS working, are security objectives being achieved, are risks being controlled, and what decisions or resources are needed?**

---

## Inputs to Management Review

Top management should review:

### 1. Previous Management Review Actions

Check whether actions decided during the previous review were completed.

**Example:**

Previous decision:

> Train three employees as internal auditors.

Management checks:

- Was the training completed?
    
- Are the employees now competent?
    
- Did the additional auditors improve the audit program?
    

---

### 2. Changes in Internal and External Issues

Management should consider changes that could affect the ISMS.

**Examples:**

- New business processes
    
- New technologies
    
- New regulations
    
- Organizational restructuring
    
- New threats
    
- New customer requirements
    

---

### 3. Performance of the ISMS

This includes reviewing:

- Monitoring and measurement results
    
- Security KPIs/KRIs
    
- Audit results
    
- Corrective actions
    
- Achievement of information security objectives
    

**Example:**

If the objective was:

> Reduce critical vulnerabilities by 90%.

Management should determine whether the objective was achieved and, if not, why.

---

### 4. Feedback from Interested Parties

Management should consider relevant feedback from:

- Customers
    
- Employees
    
- Suppliers
    
- Regulators
    
- Business partners
    

**Example:**

Several customers complain about information-security issues. Management should review the complaints and determine whether changes to the ISMS are necessary.

---

### 5. Risk Assessment and Risk Treatment

Management reviews:

- Current risk assessment results
    
- Changes in risk levels
    
- Unacceptable risks
    
- Risk treatment progress
    
- Status of controls in the risk treatment plan
    

**Example:**

If a control was supposed to be implemented by June but is still incomplete, management should understand:

- Why it was delayed
    
- The impact of the delay
    
- Whether additional resources are required
    
- Whether the risk remains acceptable
    

---

### 6. Opportunities for Continual Improvement

Management should identify opportunities to improve the ISMS.

**Examples:**

- Automating incident management
    
- Improving employee awareness
    
- Implementing better monitoring
    
- Improving vulnerability management
    
- Introducing new security technologies
    

---

## Management Review Outputs

The review should result in **decisions and actions** related to:

- Improvements to the ISMS
    
- Changes to the ISMS
    
- Changes to information security objectives
    
- Resource requirements
    
- Actions needed to address identified problems
    

### Example

Management determines that incident response is too slow.

**Decision:**

> Implement an incident-management platform and allocate budget for the project.

This decision becomes an improvement action.

---

## CISO's Role in Management Review

The **CISO usually plays a major role in preparing and supporting the management review**.

The CISO may:

1. Prepare the management review agenda and inputs.
    
2. Present **security KPIs/KRIs**.
    
3. Present monitoring and measurement results from **Clause 9.1**.
    
4. Present internal audit results from **Clause 9.2**.
    
5. Present information security objectives and their achievement.
    
6. Present current risks and the **risk treatment plan**.
    
7. Present security incidents and trends.
    
8. Present corrective actions and their status.
    
9. Present feedback from customers and other interested parties.
    
10. Recommend improvements and required resources.
    
11. Record or coordinate the resulting actions.
    
12. Track management decisions until completion.
    

**Important:** The **CISO does not replace top management**. Top management is responsible for making the management decisions and ensuring the necessary resources are provided.

---

## Documentation

ISO 27001 requires the organization to retain **documented information as evidence of the results of management reviews**.

Typical records include:

- Management review agenda
    
- Management review minutes
    
- Presentation/report
    
- Decisions and action items
    
- Assigned responsibilities
    
- Deadlines
    
- Improvement decisions
    

---

## Simple Example

Imagine the CISO presents the following during the management review:

|Area|Result|
|---|---|
|Security incidents|Increased by 20%|
|Critical vulnerabilities|Reduced by 85%|
|Awareness training|98% completion|
|Internal audits|3 completed|
|Risk treatment|90% controls implemented|
|Customer complaints|2 security-related complaints|
|Security objective|One objective not achieved|

Management then decides to:

- Increase phishing-awareness training.
    
- Allocate additional vulnerability-management resources.
    
- Implement incident-management automation.
    
- Review the missed security objective.
    
- Update the risk treatment plan.
    

These decisions become **outputs of the management review**.

**Clause 9 = CHECK**

- **9.1** → Monitoring, Measurement, Analysis & Evaluation
    
- **9.2** → Internal Audit
    
- **9.3** → Management Review
    

➡️ **Clause 10 = ACT — Improvement**
