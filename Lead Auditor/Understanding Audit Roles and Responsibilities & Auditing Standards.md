
## Audit Team Leader / Lead Auditor

### Primary Responsibility

- Represents the certification body and manages the audit process.
- Recommends if the organization qualifies for certification.

### Initial Contact and Coordination

- Engages with the audit client to:
    - Confirm the audit process.
    - Discuss audit content.
    - Ensure the audit is feasible, considering risks and resource needs.

### Audit Preparation

- Prepares audit documents.
- Coordinates logistics for the audit team.
- Develops the audit plan, assigning roles and determining the audit schedule.

### Audit Execution

- Leads the opening meeting with the client.
- Manages audit delivery and team.
- Maintains a positive relationship with the auditee.

### Evaluation and Reporting

- Analyzes findings to reach conclusions.
- Conducts the closing meeting.
- Prepares and distributes the audit report, and may approve or verify corrective actions.

### Single vs. Larger Audits

- In single-auditor audits, that auditor handles all Lead Auditor responsibilities.
- In larger audits, the Lead Auditor may also perform auditor tasks.

---

## Role of Auditor

### Data Collection and Analysis

- Analyzes and evaluates data collected during the audit.
- Conducts site visits and interviews with auditees.

### Verification and Documentation

- Verifies information received from auditees.
- Documents audit findings and maintains appropriate records.

### Remote Auditing

- Uses remote auditing techniques if needed.

### Meetings

- Attends opening and closing meetings.
- Contributes to discussions on audit conclusions.

### Audit Reporting

- Assists in preparing the audit report.

### Responsibility in Findings

- Identifies potential findings, but the Lead Auditor decides their applicability and validity.

---

## Role of Technical Specialists in Audits

### Purpose and Expertise

- Provide specific knowledge that auditors may lack, such as regulatory, legislative, or process-specific expertise.
- Examples include specialists for complex systems, chemistry, or legal and contract knowledge.

### Support Roles

- May assist with language, translation, and cultural context.
- Helps auditors understand local practices, cultural nuances, or specific industry requirements (e.g., union rights, social security).

### Audit Findings

- Can identify findings related to their expertise but must share these with the lead auditor for validation.

### Integration into Audit

- Lead auditor determines the best ways to use their expertise, including:
    - Accompanying an auditor on a site visit.
    - Participating in interviews (though not necessarily leading them).

---

## Role of Certification Reviewer

### Review and Verification

- Reviews the Lead Auditor’s recommendation and audit documents.
- Verifies that audit conclusions are accurate and all processes were correctly followed (e.g., correct audit days, auditor competencies).

### Independence

- Provides an independent decision on certification, with no prior contact with the client.

### Competency Requirements

- Must have the same competencies as a Lead Auditor, including knowledge of audit processes, the relevant standard, and the specific industry.

---

## Role of Audit Client

### Definition and Role

- The organization requesting the audit, typically a senior manager or director, or someone overseeing the management system.
- Coordinates between the lead auditor and the organization.

### Responsibilities Before and During the Audit

- Provides essential information for audit planning, including any factors that could impact audit risks (e.g., shutdowns, manager availability, health/safety issues).
- Arranges and coordinates meetings, interviews, site visits, and resources (e.g., workspace, documentation) for the audit team.
- Answers questions from management regarding audit scope, dates, and process.

### Post-Audit Responsibilities

- Manages communication on corrections and corrective actions.
- Reports and ensures closure of audit findings.

---

## Role of Management Team in an Audit

### Composition

- Includes senior leaders (e.g., CEO, directors, department heads) with responsibility over management systems.
- In smaller organizations, may include most managers; in larger ones, only those directly involved with relevant systems.

### Audit Participation

- Attends opening, closing, and daily debrief meetings as required.
- Participates in interviews, providing accurate and honest information.

### Support and Resources

- Communicates audit requirements to staff and facilitates their involvement.
- Provides necessary resources, such as meeting rooms, interview time, and access for site visits.
- Supplies audit evidence like documents and records as needed.

### Post-Audit Actions

- Assists in preparing and implementing corrective actions for audit findings.

### Authority and Coordination

- While the audit client manages logistics, the Management Team approves and supports these efforts.

---

## Role of Auditees in an Audit

### Definition

- Refers to anyone involved in the audit process, such as senior management, the audit client, supervisors, operators, and contractors.

### Participation and Availability

- Must be aware of the audit, make time to participate, and adjust schedules if needed.

### Responsibilities

- Explain processes, provide documents and records, and guide auditors around the site if required.
- Answer questions openly and honestly to help assess the management system’s effectiveness.

### Concerns and Auditor Approach

- Auditors, especially the Lead Auditor, should communicate respectfully to create a comfortable environment, addressing any concerns Auditees may have about potential findings.

---

## Role of Hosts and Guides in an Audit

### Primary Role

- Accompany the audit team and help gather requested audit evidence.

### Guidelines for Interaction

- Avoid answering questions directed at management or staff, allowing auditors to assess the knowledge of the person being questioned.

# Understanding Auditing Standards

---

## Certification Overview

- Certification is provided by certification bodies, which are accredited by accreditation bodies.
- Certification is open to any organization, regardless of size or sector, with uniform processes and requirements.

## Role of Certification Auditors

- Certification auditors conduct audits on behalf of the certification body.
- Auditors must:
    - Have experience relevant to the type of organization they audit (e.g., construction industry experience for a construction company).
    - Be competent in the specific standards (e.g., ISO 9001 for quality management or ISO 22001 for food safety).
    - Have training and experience in auditing management systems.

## Competency Assurance

- Certification bodies are responsible for ensuring their auditors are competent.
- Accreditation bodies audit the certification bodies to verify they maintain effective processes for auditor competency.

## Certification Process Overview

### Key Elements of the Certification Process

#### Preparation and Implementation

- Organizations begin with an implementation project to meet standard requirements, developing necessary processes and documentation.

#### Choosing a Certification Body

- Organizations select a certification body, which will collect information to determine audit days and develop the audit program.
- An optional gap analysis audit may be offered to identify any initial gaps.

#### Initial Certification Audit

- Conducted in two stages:
    - **Stage 1:** Auditors assess if the organization has a basic framework, processes, and documentation. They report any gaps to be addressed.
    - **Stage 2:** Auditors test the management system’s implementation, verify fixes for previous gaps, and confirm the organization meets all requirements.
- **Certification Issuance:** If standards are met, the certificate is issued, even if there are minor non-conformities, provided the organization commits to improvements.

#### Surveillance Audits

- Certificate validity: 3 years, with surveillance audits every 6–12 months.
- Surveillance audits check for system maintenance and continuous improvement.

#### Recertification Audit

- After 3 years, if certification is to continue, a recertification audit is conducted (one stage, more in-depth than surveillance).
- Organization must show ongoing management system maintenance and improvements over the three-year period.

## Certification of Integrated Management Systems

### Key Elements

#### Multiple Standards Certification

- Organizations can be certified against multiple ISO standards (e.g., ISO 9001, ISO 14001, ISO 50001).

#### Integrated Management System Audit

- A combined audit assesses compliance with multiple standards in a single process.
- Certification auditors ensure that requirements for each standard are met.

#### Benefits of Integrated Audits

- **Reduced Audit Days:** Overlapping requirements allow for fewer audit days compared to separate audits.
- **Single Audit Program:** The organization only needs to manage one audit schedule, saving time and resources.

## Introduction to Accreditation and ISO 17021

### Key Points on ISO 17021-2015

#### Overview of ISO 17021-2015

- ISO 17021-2015 is the standard certification bodies must meet to gain accreditation from an accreditation body.
- Accreditation bodies are typically independent organizations, often government-regulated, operating at a national level (e.g., UCAS in the UK, ANSI in the US).

#### Purpose of Accreditation

- Accreditation enables certification bodies to issue formally recognized management system certificates.

#### Accreditation Process

- Involves an audit of the certification body’s management systems and witness audits to assess lead auditors’ competency and processes.
- The accreditation body audits the certification body’s performance, not the client being audited.

#### ISO 17021-1 Requirements

- Covers requirements for all certification body audits, focusing on:
    - **Audit Principles (Clause 4):** Fundamental principles that should guide auditing practices.
    - **Audit Processes (Clause 9):** Detailed procedures that must be followed during audits.
    - **Annexes** provide additional information on auditing competencies and evaluation methods.

#### Certification Body Requirements

- Beyond auditor competencies, ISO 17021-1 outlines requirements related to:
    - **Issuance of certificates**
    - **Management of confidentiality and conflict of interest**
    - **Handling appeals and complaints**

## Competencies of Lead Auditors Required by ISO 17021-1

### Key Points

#### Establishment of Competency Processes

- ISO 17021-1:2015 requires certification bodies to develop processes for:
    - Setting detailed competency criteria.
    - Defining evaluation methods.
    - Implementing competency development programs.

#### General Competency Requirements for Auditors (Annex A)

##### Knowledge Areas:

- Audit principles, practices, and techniques applicable to all management system audits.
- Certification body processes and procedures.
- Specific sector business operations, products, processes, and organizations.
- General business management practices across various business types.

##### Skills:

- Presentation, note-taking, and report writing.
- Interviewing techniques.
- Audit management capabilities.
- Proficiency in relevant languages.

#### Additional Competency Requirements

- Other ISO 17021 standards outline further competencies:
    - **ISO 17021-2:** Competencies for auditors of ISO 14001.
    - **ISO 17021-3:** Competencies for auditors of ISO 9001.

#### Multidisciplinary Audits

- Lead auditors performing multidisciplinary audits must be:
    - Competent in at least one management system standard.
    - Knowledgeable about the other standards involved in the audit.

## Introduction to the International Accreditation Forum (IAF) Documents

### Key Points

#### What is the IAF?

- The International Accreditation Forum (IAF) is an association of accreditation bodies worldwide.
- Its purpose is to coordinate and harmonize the efforts of different accreditation bodies and organizations involved in conformity assessments.

#### Multilateral Recognition Arrangement (MLA)

- Accreditation bodies that sign the IAF MLA agree to recognize each other’s accreditations.
- Example: A certification body accredited by the UK Accreditation Service (UCAS) is recognized in Australia without needing separate accreditation from JAS-ANZ.

#### IAF Published Documents

##### Mandatory Documents (MDs):

- Used by accreditation bodies during audits of certification bodies.
- Provide guidance on implementing ISO 17021-1 requirements.
- Cover topics such as:
    - Calculating audit duration for certification bodies.
    - Developing multi-site audit programs and sampling processes.
    - Evaluating audit competencies.

##### Guidance Documents (GDs) and Information Documents (IDs):

- Offer additional advice and information, not specific requirements.
- Help certification bodies and lead auditors understand expectations from accreditation bodies.

## Introduction to ISO 19011

### Key Points

#### Purpose of ISO 19011

- ISO 19011 provides guidance for conducting audits of management systems.
- It is optional for organizations undertaking internal audits, even if they are certified to standards like ISO 9001 or ISO 14001.
- Certification against ISO 19011 is not possible; instead, organizations use its guidance to develop internal audit processes.

#### Principles of Auditing (ISO 19011)

- **Integrity:** Auditors must act professionally, honestly, and within legal boundaries.
- **Fair Presentation:** Audit conclusions, findings, and reports should accurately reflect audit results.
- **Due Professional Care:** Auditors are expected to provide informed and professional opinions.
- **Confidentiality:** Auditors must treat all client information and data with confidentiality.
- **Independence:** Auditors should remain independent from the organization being audited.
