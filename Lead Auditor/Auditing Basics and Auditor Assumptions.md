
## Understanding Auditing Basics

### Purpose of Audits

- Clarifies why audits are conducted.
- Emphasizes objective assessment, accountability, and ISMS improvement.

### Types of Audits

- **Internal Audit**: Conducted within the organization.
- **External Audit**: Performed by a third party.
- **Compliance Audit**: Ensures adherence to ISO 27001 and other standards.

### Stages of an Audit

1. **Planning**: Define scope, objectives, and methods.
2. **Conducting**: Gather evidence through documentation, interviews, and observations.
3. **Reporting**: Document findings, including non-conformities and observations.
4. **Follow-up**: Ensure corrective actions are taken.

## Principles of Auditing

### Integrity

- Maintain honesty, impartiality, and professionalism.

### Evidence-Based Approach

- Ensure findings are based on sufficient, relevant, and reliable evidence.

### Confidentiality

- Keep audit findings secure and only share with relevant stakeholders.

## Auditor Assumptions

### Neutrality

- Avoid bias and preconceived notions about ISMS effectiveness.

### Focus on Risk and Compliance

- Verify compliance and assess risks without unnecessary fault-finding.

### Open-mindedness

- Remain open to new information and understand the organization’s context.

## Practical Guidelines for Auditors

### Effective Questioning

- Use open-ended questions to gather necessary information without leading auditees.

### Observation Skills

- Objectively observe processes and interactions to identify strengths and weaknesses.

### Documentation Review

- Review policies, procedures, and records for alignment with ISO 27001.

## Case Studies and Examples

- Examples of common auditor assumptions and strategies to ensure fair audits.

## Techniques for Finding Evidence in an ISO 27001 Audit

### Review Documents and Records

- **Purpose**: Provides hard evidence.
- **Example**: Request "Training Procedure" and "Training Records" to assess compliance.

### Interview Employees

- **Purpose**: Validates processes when documents are unavailable.
- **Example**: Ask employees about electronic disposal procedures to check consistency.

### Observe Activities and Facilities

- **Purpose**: Visually confirm compliance with procedures.
- **Example**: Check physical access control to the server room.

## Sampling Records During an ISO 27001 Audit

### Determine the Sample Size

- **Small Workforce**: Review all records.
- **Large Workforce**: Use sampling for efficiency.

### Focus on Relevant Records

- **Critical Areas**: Prioritize records related to high-risk processes.
- **New Employees**: Check for potential training deficiencies.

### Maintain Control Over Record Selection

- The auditor selects records, not the organization.

## Effective Interviewing Techniques for Evidence Gathering

### Use Open-Ended Questions

- **Example**: “Please describe how you manage access control to your IT system.”

### Avoid Closed-Ended Questions

- **Example**: "Is this the room where you store electronic waste?"

### Identify Root Causes of Non-Conformities

- **5-Why Technique**: Ask "why" multiple times to trace issues.

### Importance of Communication Skills

- Encourage open discussion and provide meaningful feedback.

## Audit Findings: Understanding Non-Conformities and Observations

### Non-Conformities

- Failure to comply with internal policies, ISO 27001 standards, or legal requirements.

### Observations

- **Positive**: Recognizes effective practices.
- **Negative**: Suggests improvements without sufficient evidence of non-compliance.

## Recording Audit Findings

- Document all findings in an **Internal Audit Report**.

## Definition of Non-Conformity in ISO 27001

### Four Main Types of Requirements

1. **ISO 27001 Standard** (e.g., missing risk assessment).
2. **Legislative** (e.g., non-compliance with data protection laws).
3. **Internal Policies** (e.g., backup frequency not followed).
4. **Interested Parties** (e.g., failure to meet partner requirements).

## Observations in Internal Audits

### Improvement Suggestions

- Recommend enhancements (e.g., increase backup frequency).

### Highlighting Positive Practices

- Recognize departments excelling in security awareness training.

## Internal vs. External Audit

### Definition

- **Internal Audit**: Conducted by employees.
- **External Audit**: Conducted by a third party.

### Purpose

- **Internal**: Improve ISMS.
- **External**: Evaluate compliance and issue certification.

### Use of Results

- **Internal**: Used within the organization.
- **External**: Results may be public.

## Annual Audit Program for ISO 27001

### Key Elements

1. **Audit Period**: Typically one year.
2. **Scheduling**: Define audit timelines per department.
3. **Scope**: Prioritize high-risk areas.
4. **Audit Criteria**: ISO 27001, legal requirements, internal documentation.
5. **Audit Methods**: Documentation review, interviews, observations.
6. **Auditor Assignment**: Define audit team roles.
7. **Confidentiality**: Ensure security of audit information.

## Audit Plan for an Individual Audit (ISO 27001)

### Audit Elements

- Define scope by processes, departments, or ISO 27001 clauses.

### Timing and Schedule

- Allocate sufficient time per audit area.

### Contact Persons

- Inform relevant staff in advance.

### Audit Team Roles

- Assign responsibilities within the audit team.

### Consideration of Audit Impacts

- Assess potential effects on business processes.

## Creation of the Internal Audit Checklist

### Purpose

- Ensures all key audit elements are reviewed.

### Structure

- Typically a four-column table:
    1. **Clause Number**
    2. **Description**
    3. **Compliance Status (Yes/No)**
    4. **Notes**

## Internal Audit Report

### Key Elements

1. **General Information** (dates, auditor details, scope & criteria).
2. **Non-Conformities** (details of non-compliance).
3. **Observations** (both positive and improvement suggestions).

### Distribution

- Follow internal audit plan guidelines.

## Corrective Action Follow-up

### Key Steps

1. **Corrective Action Request Form**
2. **Verification** (on-site checks to confirm resolution)
3. **Mini-Audits for Follow-up**

## Recording the Evidence in Internal Audits

### Key Elements

- **Documents**: Name, version, date.
- **Records**: Exact name, date, serial number.
- **Interviews**: Name, job title, key quotes.
- **Observations**: Location, time, description.
- **Internal Audit Checklist**: Track verification items.

### Best Practices

- Maintain accurate, retrievable records.
