
### Audit Criteria and Objectives

#### Audit Criteria

- Standards to be audited against, such as ISO 9001 or ISO 27001.
- Defined by the audit client and certification body.
- Auditors should be competent in the relevant standards; for combined audits, not all team members need to know every standard.

#### Audit Objectives

- Define what the audit aims to achieve, which may include:
    - Assessing compliance with management system standards (e.g., ISO 40001).
    - Verifying that the organization follows its own procedures.
    - Ensuring compliance with legal and other obligations.
    - Evaluating the effectiveness of the management system in meeting its goals.
    - Identifying potential areas for improvement.

#### Purpose of Objectives

- Provided by the certification body and outline the audit’s purpose.
- Help focus the audit on achieving specific outcomes, such as risk control, compliance, and performance improvement.

### Audit Scope

#### Definition

- Specifies the “what” and “where” of the audit, such as which sites and processes are included.

#### Inclusions and Exclusions

- Clearly outlines what parts of the organization are covered (e.g., only facilities management).
- Notes any exclusions, such as off-site locations or specific processes not included in the audit.

#### Determination and Importance

- Typically set by the audit client and certification body, not the lead auditor.
- Essential for creating an accurate audit plan to ensure correct locations and processes are audited.

### Selecting Auditing Methods

#### On-site Human Interactions

- Conducting interviews, using questionnaires or checklists, site visits with questions, and document reviews with the auditee.

#### Off-site Human Interactions

- Similar methods can be used remotely, such as interviews via phone or video conferencing.

#### On-site No-Human Interaction

- Reviewing documents, evaluating data, observing activities, and conducting sampling or analysis without the auditee’s involvement.

#### Off-site No-Human Interaction

- Examples include reviewing data or documents remotely.

#### Planning Considerations

- Choose methods based on audit objectives, scope, and criteria.
- Effective audit plans incorporate a mix of methods to gather comprehensive evidence.

### Sampling Evidence in Audits

#### Justification

- Sampling audit evidence is a valid and necessary process.

#### Standards Setting

- Lead auditors should establish consistent sampling standards with their team, ensuring compliance with certification body requirements.

#### Pre-Audit Agreement

- Agree on sampling rates with auditors before the audit and include them in the audit plan.

#### Flexibility

- Allow flexibility for auditors to explore audit trails if concerns arise, promoting effective auditing practices.

#### Minimum vs. Maximum Sampling

- Define minimum sampling rates, but let auditors use their judgment for maximum sampling to avoid restricting their auditing capability.

### Types of Remote Auditing Techniques

#### Computer-Assisted Audit Techniques (CATs)

- Utilize software to automatically evaluate data and information, though not widely used in management system audits yet.

#### Communication Methods

- Range from simple emails to video conferencing, teleconferencing, instant messaging, and collaborative working platforms.

#### No-Human Interaction Methods

- Document reviews, data analysis, and evaluations can be effectively conducted remotely without face-to-face communication.

#### Surveillance Techniques

- Use of technologies like CCTV for remote observation, though concerns about privacy limit this practice.

#### Growing Opportunities

- Advances in technology provide increasing options for incorporating remote auditing techniques into audit plans.

### Deciding When to Use Remote Auditing Techniques

#### Criteria for Use

- Decision to use remote auditing techniques should be based on audit objectives, scope, criteria, and the type of evidence needed.

#### Technology and Competency

- Consider the available technology and the auditor’s competency in using it.

#### Certification Body Guidelines

- Follow any processes established by the certification body regarding the use of remote auditing techniques.

#### Meeting Audit Objectives

- Assess whether remote auditing can effectively meet audit objectives without compromising the audit quality.

#### Cost-Saving Pressure

- Avoid using remote techniques solely for cost-saving purposes; prioritize meeting audit objectives and enhancing the audit process.

#### Supplemental Use

- Consider using remote auditing as a supplemental method alongside on-site auditing to save time or enhance efficiency.

### Planning the Use of Remote Auditing Techniques

#### Inclusion in Audit Plan

- Clearly document remote auditing techniques in the audit plan, specifying when and what criteria will be tested.

#### Team Roles

- Ensure all team members understand their roles in using remote auditing techniques.

#### Identifying Techniques and Information

- Develop a plan that identifies the specific remote techniques to be used and the information to be gathered.

#### Equipment and Software

- Capture details of any software or equipment needed for remote auditing.

#### Competency Communication

- Communicate with both auditees and auditors about the necessary competencies for using the planned software or equipment.

#### Impact on Team Dynamics

- Consider how remote auditing affects team communication and collaboration, especially if auditors are in different locations.

#### Documentation in Audit Report

- Include a note in the audit report to clarify when remote auditing techniques were used.

### Selecting the Audit Team

#### Audit Preparation

- The audit team selection occurs after defining the audit objectives, scope, criteria, and methods.

#### Team Size and Competencies

- Determine the number of auditors needed and their required competencies based on the audit’s specifics.

#### Knowledge and Understanding

- Auditors should have knowledge of the organization’s operations and an understanding of the audit criteria.

#### Personal Qualities

- Consider auditors’ personal behavior and qualities to ensure alignment with auditing principles.

#### Pre-Audit Contact

- If unfamiliar with auditors, contact them beforehand to assess their experience and understanding of the business.

#### Pressure to Compromise

- Be cautious of pressure to select auditors based solely on availability; prioritize competence.

#### Lead Auditor Responsibility

- As the lead auditor, ensure you are comfortable with the selected team. If necessary, reschedule the audit to include competent auditors.

### Managing Audit Risk

#### Types of Risks

- Consider risks to the business from audit activities, including product conformity and personnel safety.
- Identify security risks related to data or information provided by auditees.

#### Risk Mitigation

- Develop a mitigation plan in collaboration with the auditee to address identified risks.

#### Audit Team Risks

- Evaluate risks to the audit team during on-site activities, including:
    - Travel risks and weather conditions.
    - Personal security and safety during the audit.
    - Welfare considerations (e.g., food, restroom facilities, suitable workspaces).

#### Audit Objectives

- Ensure the audit meets its objectives while managing risks related to auditor competencies and auditee responsiveness.

#### Formal Risk Plans

- For larger or more complex audits, create a formal risk identification and control plan to reduce unexpected issues.

# Managing the Audit Process

## Open Meeting

### 1. Introduce Participants

- Include the audit team, relevant management, and the audit client.
    - For limited-scope audits, only invite management from the audited area.
    - For shorter audits (1-2 days), invite only essential managers related to the audit scope.

### 2. Confirm Audit Plan

- Ensure the audit client and team agree on the audit plan’s applicability.

### 3. Agenda Overview

- **Audit Objectives, Scope, and Criteria**
- **Audit Plan Outline**: Steps and structure of the audit process.
- **Audit Team Introduction**: Present audit team members.

### 4. Audit Methods

- Brief on methods used and expected conclusions for clarity.

### 5. Business Introduction (if necessary)

- A short overview by the auditees on business operations, including any specific security, safety, or risk controls relevant to the audit team.

### 6. Q&A Session

- Opportunity for auditees to ask questions or express concerns.

### 7. Meeting Duration

- Should not exceed **30 minutes**, ensuring clear communication of the audit process.

---

## Managing Site Visits

### 1. Site Visit Orientation & Evidence Gathering

- **Follow Audit Trails**: Allow time for questions to explore audit trails and verify answers.
- **Team Structure**: Conduct visits individually, in small groups, or split the team based on specific roles or areas of interest.
- **Extended or Group Visits**: Plan as needed depending on the audit scope.

### 2. Deployment and Verification Visits

- **Shorter, Targeted Checks**: Focus on verifying tasks are performed as specified.
- **Follow-up Questions**: Allow time for questions and evidence gathering during visits.

### 3. Include Less-Visited Site Areas

- **Comprehensive Coverage**: Ensure all areas within the audit scope are visited, including peripheral or less frequently accessed areas.
- **Visit Strategy**: Start with outer site areas and move toward central operations.

### 4. Lead Auditor Responsibilities

- **Balance Leadership and Inquiry**: Allow the Auditee to lead in demonstrating compliance but maintain control over audit direction.
- **Prevent Divergence**: Stay focused on areas of concern and prevent the Auditee from diverting the audit.
- **Professional Persistence**: Be assertive in inquiries while avoiding argumentativeness.

---

## Interviews

### 1. Purpose and Planning

- Arrange interviews in advance where possible but remain flexible for new interviews during the audit.
- Coordinate interview objectives among the audit team to ensure meaningful inquiries and prevent repeated interviews.

### 2. Team Coordination

- Each auditor should prepare an interview plan outlining:
    - Interviewee
    - Objectives
    - Specific questions
- Share interview plans with the team to avoid duplication.

### 3. Recording Interview Outcomes

- Document answers and findings, especially noting non-conformities and concerns.
- Share notes among the team to ensure consistent understanding.

### 4. Use of Pro Forma Sheet

- If not available, create a **pro forma sheet** to guide interviews.
- Suggested headings:
    - Interviewee name, job title, role
    - Specific questions or queries
    - Responses and evidence gathered
    - Audit findings and potential non-conformities

---

## Managing Audit Evidence Requests

### 1. Coordination and Tracking

- Maintain a log of all audit evidence requests.
- Use tools like a **flip chart, whiteboard, or Post-it notes** to visually track requests and responses.
- Consolidate requests to streamline the process for auditees.

### 2. Efficient Request Presentation

- Present a **single, organized list** to each auditee to simplify evidence collection.

### 3. Daily Debrief Sessions

- Use daily debrief sessions to manage requests, ensuring all items are accounted for.
- Ensure the audit team is aware of the tracking process.

---

## Debriefing Sessions

### 1. Purpose

- Ensure alignment between the audit team, audit client, and necessary hosts or guides.
- Decide if manager attendance is beneficial.

### 2. Pre-Audit Communication

- Clarify the purpose of debriefing sessions before the audit to prevent informal extensions.

### 3. Typical Agenda Items

1. **Audit Evidence Requests**: Review outstanding requests.
2. **Additional Needs**:
    - Requests for further interviews or site visits.
    - Confirmation of upcoming audit activities.
3. **Audit Plan Adjustments**: Discuss necessary changes.
4. **Progress Verification**:
    - Check progress on audit outcomes.
    - Review issues, observations, or audit findings.

### 4. Managing Audit Outcome Expectations

- Explain that audit conclusions are drawn only after full analysis.
- Share preliminary **evidence only**, not conclusions, until the audit is finalized.

---

## Dealing with Conflicts

### 1. Purpose

- Resolve conflicts effectively to ensure audit outcomes are not compromised.

### 2. Managing Conflicts

- Identify and address conflicts early, particularly due to misunderstandings or differences in opinion.
- Conduct conflict discussions away from the main audit setting if possible.

### 3. Resolution Approach

1. **Stay Impartial**: Clarify neutrality and focus on evidence.
2. **Separate Facts from Assumptions**: Establish agreement on factual evidence first.
3. **Discuss Opinions and Consequences**:
    - Reach consensus on whether the facts indicate a non-conformity.
    - Try to agree on potential impacts or consequences.
4. **Seek Common Ground**: Aim for mutual understanding, even if full agreement isn’t possible.

### 4. If Disagreement Persists

- **Document the Disagreement**: Record unresolved issues for the closing presentation and audit report.
- **Remind Both Parties**: Stress that findings are preliminary, not final audit conclusions.

# Managing your audit team

## 1. Importance of Team Coordination

- Ensure the audit team works cohesively to meet audit objectives.
- Support less experienced auditors with guidance, and help experienced auditors align with the current audit plan.

## 2. Pre-Audit Preparation

- Start managing the team before the audit begins by discussing the audit scope, criteria, and objectives with the certification body and audit client.
- Fully understand the audit objectives to direct the team effectively.

## 3. Knowing Your Team

- Assess each team member’s strengths, weaknesses, and skills.
- Assign tasks based on each auditor’s competencies to optimize performance.

## 4. Role Clarity and Leadership

- Clearly define each auditor’s role within the audit plan to prevent misalignment.
- Balance guidance with autonomy by providing a structured plan that allows auditors flexibility within defined parameters.

## 5. Structured Planning and Flexibility

- Create an audit framework that allows auditors freedom, such as choosing specific areas for site visits.

## 6. Benefits of Strong Leadership

- Good leadership, planning, and organization enhance team motivation and efficiency.
- Instills confidence in the audit client that the process is valuable and professionally managed.

# Pre-Audit Team Communication

## 1. Team Preparation

- Review team members’ experience and knowledge, either via CVs/profiles or through discussion of their relevant auditing background.
- Conduct a meeting (in-person or remote) to review key topics and gather input.

## 2. Key Discussion Topics

- **Audit Objectives, Scope, and Criteria:** Outline what the audit will cover.
- **Team Roles and Methods:** Explain planned auditor activities, audit methods, and any remote auditing techniques.
- **Logistics:** Discuss travel, accommodation, workspaces, and safety equipment needs.
- **Audit Risks:** Address risks such as security protocols, confidentiality, and business processes.

## 3. Documentation and Information Needs

- Identify any information or documents needed in advance (e.g., org charts, previous non-conformity closures, schematics).
- Consider reviewing these documents off-site or request them early for on-site availability.

## 4. Final Plan Development

- Hold this meeting before discussing the audit plan with the client to gather insights for planning.
- Notify the team of the final audit plan and any changes after the client discussion.

# Managing the Audit Process

## 1. Audit Management Flexibility

- Adjust the formality of management based on the audit’s nature, team procedures, and personal experience.

## 2. Key Information to Track

- Evidence requests (pending and received).
- Documents reviewed, interview and site visit progress.
- Audit plan status and amendments.
- Questions raised and answered, audit evidence, and findings.

## 3. Information Management Tools

- Use shared documents, software, flip charts, post-its, or templates to organize and coordinate information.

## 4. On-Site Audit Steps

1. Opening meeting.
2. Evidence gathering (site visits, interviews, document reviews).
3. Form audit findings and assess for non-conformities.
4. Draw audit conclusions (does the organization meet standards?).
5. Prepare and conduct the closing meeting.

## 5. Time Allocation

- Allocate most time (75%) to evidence gathering.
- Plan time for evaluating evidence, agreeing on findings, and preparing the closing presentation.

## 6. Closing Preparation Timing

- Work backward from closing time to allocate sufficient time for each step.
- Example: Closing at 5 p.m., with a 1-hour presentation, requires starting at 4 p.m.
- Factor in presentation preparation time based on your efficiency.

## 7. Presentation Preparation

- Allow ample time for preparing visuals or media (photos, slides).
- Consider preparing materials daily if needed for better quality.

# Managing Audit Findings

## 1. Use a Template

Include:

- Description of the non-conformity.
- Supporting evidence (e.g., photos, documents, interviews).
- Criteria the non-conformity applies to.

## 2. Ensure Consistency

- Regularly discuss and review findings within the audit team to maintain consistency in reporting.

## 3. Draft Findings

- Treat findings as drafts until the debriefing session with the audit client for clarification and additional evidence.

## 4. Debriefing Sessions

- Always review findings with the audit client before finalizing conclusions to avoid mistakes and ensure clear understanding.

## 5. Client Objections

- Allow the audit client to raise objections, even if they disagree with findings.
- Document any objections in the final report.

# Managing Audit Records

## 1. Balance Documentation

- Maintain only necessary records to avoid excessive documentation.
- Use digital photographs instead of paper copies for easier management.

## 2. Types of Audit Documentation

- Audit plans, reports, and non-conformity reports.
- Auditor notes, photographs, and lists of evidence (e.g., reviewed documents or interviews).
- Post-audit records like corrective actions and follow-up audits.

## 3. Collecting Audit Records

- Lead auditors may use shared files, software, or cloud storage to collect records.
- A separate list with unique references can help organize the records.

## 4. Monitor Compliance

- Regularly check that auditors follow the record-keeping process and address any issues in team meetings.

# What to Do When You Hit Problems

## 1. Assess the Impact

- Understand how the problem affects the audit objectives.
- Decide on appropriate actions based on the issue at hand.

## 2. Local Solutions

- Start by addressing the problem locally with the auditee and audit client.
- Possible actions:
    - Adjusting the audit plan.
    - Reallocating auditee members or rescheduling activities.

## 3. Maintain Audit Objectives

- Try to meet the objectives without changing the scope or criteria.
- If that’s not possible, contact the certification body to discuss potential changes such as:
    - Adjusting audit duration.
    - Reducing the scope or changing criteria.
    - Switching to remote auditing or off-site methods.

## 4. Re-evaluate Objectives

- If the audit objectives are still at risk, discuss with the certification body and audit client to modify the objectives, scope, or criteria.

## 5. Postpone or Cancel

- If achieving the audit objectives is impossible, consider postponing or canceling the audit.
- Make this decision only after consulting with the certification body and audit client.

# Evaluating Your Audit Management

## 1. Self-Reflection

- Consider how well you managed the audit team, kept the process on track, and developed good relationships.
- Ask yourself:
    - Did you leverage the team’s strengths?
    - How effectively did you manage interactions with the audit client and auditees?
    - Was your communication clear with all parties?

## 2. Evaluate Key Areas

- The effectiveness of your closing meeting.
- The accuracy and value of your audit conclusions.

## 3. Seek Feedback

- Ask for feedback from the audit client, auditees, or audit team members.

## 4. Continuous Improvement

- Use feedback to identify areas for growth.
- Recognize both your strengths and areas for improvement.
